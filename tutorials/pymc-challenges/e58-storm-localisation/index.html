    E58 STORM localisation microscopy¶

    This tutorial does the main part of the pymc-challenges notebook E58_storm_localization_microscopy.py in the web app. You use the model sci.storm_localisation@1. You do not write PyMC code. The model reads the raw camera frames, finds the isolated spots and gives the position of each molecule with an error bar in nm.

    You need:

    • The platform (start it with make dev) and a project that you can upload datasets to. This tutorial uses the project microscopy. If your project has a different name, change the project part of each dataset:// and model:// reference.
    • A local copy of pymc-challenges. The export command reads one file from its data/ directory.
    • About 20 minutes. On a laptop, the fit of 200 spots took less than 2 minutes with nutpie.

    1. The question¶

    A light microscope cannot separate two points that are closer than about 250 nm. This is the diffraction limit. A single fluorescent molecule shows as a blob of about that width: the point spread function (PSF). Single-molecule localisation microscopy (STORM, PALM and dSTORM) uses molecules that blink. In each camera frame, only a small number of molecules are bright, and they are far apart. The centre of an isolated blob is much more precise than its width. The positions of all the blinks make a super-resolved image.

    The notebook uses 2,000 raw frames of a dSTORM recording of the synaptic protein Unc-13 at a Drosophila neuromuscular junction (Dannhäuser et al. 2022). Each frame is a 40 × 40 pixel crop with 127 nm pixels and an exposure of 10 ms. The camera is an EMCCD. The notebook asks these questions:

    1. What does the camera do to the photons? (Notebook section 1: the photon-transfer curve.)
    2. Where is each molecule, and how sure are we? (Notebook sections 2 and 3.)
    3. How precise can a position be? (Notebook section 4: the Cramér-Rao bound.)
    4. Is a blob one molecule or more? (Notebook sections 5 and 6.)
    5. How do we make an image from uncertain points? (Notebook section 7.)
    6. How many molecules made a cluster of blinks? (Notebook section 8.)

    The notebook ends in this decision for the real recording: keep each localisation with an error bar of less than 30 nm, and draw each one as a Gaussian with its own error bar. On the real frames, a model with a Gaussian PSF on a flat background adds faint emitters around bright spots. The evidence for the number of emitters is only as good as the forward model. Thus the notebook does not trust the emitter counts of bright blobs. The notebook keeps 4,506 of 8,342 localisations, with a median error bar of 9.5 nm and a median of about 2,300 photons. One isolated spot (blob 12 in frame 5) has a position error of about 7 nm.

    What you will get, and the notebook section that each item replaces:

    In the web app Notebook
    The dataset microscopy/dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm: the raw frames in ADU Section 1: the raw frames
    The camera calibration as two settings (offset_adu, scale_adu) Section 1: the photon-transfer curve
    The posterior of the position, photons, background and PSF sd of 200 isolated spots (Parameters) Section 3: one spot in PyMC, here for 200 spots in one model
    The positions with 90% error ellipses over the super-resolved image (Plots, sci.point_pattern) Section 7: the render with each error bar
    The data, the fit and the residuals of each spot (Plots, sci.image_residuals) Section 3: the residuals of one spot
    The positions in nm with a 90% interval for each spot (Score) Section 3: the position in nm
    A comparison of a flat and a sloped background by elpd_loo "Try it yourself", item 1

    The port does not do sections 4, 5, 6 and 8. See Differences from the notebook.

    2. Get the data¶

    The export command reads dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm_roi.npz and writes a NetCDF file that the platform can read. It does these steps:

    • It keeps the 2,000 frames of 40 × 40 pixels as integers in ADU (the camera units).
    • It numbers the frames, rows and columns from 0, as the notebook does.
    • It calculates the camera calibration from the photon-transfer curve of the full 200 × 200 field, as notebook section 1 does. The file keeps the result in its attributes.

    Run the command from the root of the modeling-api repository:

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e58 \
    --challenges <path to pymc-challenges>/data \
    --out unc13_dstorm.nc --spec unc13_dstorm.yaml

    The command writes:

    wrote unc13_dstorm.nc (variables frames on frame=2000, row=40, col=40)
upload it as dataset microscopy/dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm and fit sci.storm_localisation
wrote the suggested spec to unc13_dstorm.yaml

    The file holds one dataset of about 13 MB:

    Name Dims Type Meaning
    frame 2000 integer, fixed The camera frames, 0 to 1999
    row, col 40, 40 integer, fixed The camera pixels of the crop, 0 to 39
    frames frame, row, col int32, ADU, target The raw camera counts. The values are from 783 to 40,176

    The attributes of the file keep the constants of the recording:

    Attribute Value Meaning
    pixel_nm 127 The size of one camera pixel in the sample (nm)
    offset_adu 457.04 The camera offset (ADU) from the photon-transfer curve
    scale_adu 13.86 ADU for each effective count. This is twice the EM gain of 6.93 ADU for each photon
    exposure_s 0.01 The exposure of one frame (s)
    first_frame, roi_row, roi_col 3000, 25, 50 The position of the crop in the full recording

    The attribute mapi_schema gives the variable its role, so you do not map columns.

    No pymc-challenges copy?

    --synthetic writes a movie that the model's own forward model simulates: 60 frames of 24 × 24 pixels with known blinks, on the camera of the real recording (offset 457 ADU, gain 6.9). Use it to try the steps. The numbers in this tutorial are for the real frames. For the simulated movie, use the settings of STORM_SPEC_YAML in mapi_plugin_science.storm_data.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e58 --synthetic --out storm_synthetic.nc

    3. Upload the dataset¶

    1. Open Datasets in the sidebar.
    2. Click New dataset.
    3. Type dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm in Name. Type a description, for example "Raw dSTORM frames of Unc-13 at a Drosophila synapse, 2,000 frames of 40 × 40 pixels".
    4. Click Create dataset. The upload panel opens.
    5. Click Choose files and select unc13_dstorm.nc.
    6. Make sure that the format is netCDF (or HDF5 written by xarray).
    7. Click Preview structure. The panel shows Structure of the file. Make sure that it shows frames on frame, row and col.
    8. Optional: type a message in Version message (optional).
    9. Click Upload and ingest.

    The ingest run reads the file in the sandbox. When it is done, the version page shows the tabs Schema, Coordinates, Missingness, Preview and About. On Schema, frames has the role target and the units ADU. The three dims are integer and fixed. Missingness shows no missing values. The model needs each pixel of each frame.

    The file is a plain dataset, not a tree. Do not open "Put this file in a node of a tree (optional)".

    3.1 Explore the data¶

    Look at the data before you build the spec.

    1. On the version page, click Explore this version. The Data Explorer opens. The sections under Pictures of the data come first.

    2. Click Images and fields.

    3. The viewer shows frames, the camera images of 40 × 40 pixels. The frame slider shows 24 of the 2,000 frames.

    4. The line below the image covers all 2,000 frames: the mean, the minimum and the maximum of each frame. A peak of the maximum is a frame with a bright spot (a molecule that blinks).
    5. The histogram shows the values of all pixels. Most pixels show only the background.
    6. The time sections are in Not for this data. The frame number is a list of images, not a time dim of the schema.

    Refer to Scientific data.

    4. Build the spec¶

    1. Open Build and click New spec. The editor opens with the geo MMM template.
    2. Set the Editor view to YAML.
    3. Open the Insert panel on the right.
    4. Open the Datasets section and select dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm. The toast "Dataset set" shows.
    5. Type storm in the search field. Under Models, select Molecule positions in STORM images (sci.storm_localisation@^1). The toast "Model set" shows.
    6. Select all the text in the editor and paste the full spec below. The template has other blocks (a pipeline, a split and the MMM plots), so the spec must replace all of it.
    7. Click Validate. The status shows valid.
    8. Click Save spec. The spec gets its name unc13-dstorm-localisation from metadata.name.

    This is the full spec:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata:
  name: unc13-dstorm-localisation
  project: microscopy
data:
  dataset: dataset://microscopy/dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm@latest
model:
  use: sci.storm_localisation@^1
  settings:
    pixel_nm: 127.0
    offset_adu: 457.04184713091655
    scale_adu: 13.861648333299126
    kind: flat
    max_spots: 200
    threshold_sd: 5
    smooth_px: 1
    patch_px: 11
    photons_median: 1500
    psf_sd_px: 1.0
    position_sd_px: 2
    score_draws: 40
    ppc_draws: 20
    render_px_nm: 16
  fit:
    mode: train_only
    seed: 58
    tune: 1000
    draws: 1000
    chains: 4
    engine: nutpie
    keep:
      log_likelihood: true
evaluate:
  metrics: [elpd_loo, rmse, mae, coverage_90]
outputs:
  plots:
    - core.convergence
    - sci.point_pattern
    - sci.image_residuals
    - sci.field_map

    The settings pixel_nm, offset_adu, scale_adu and max_spots come from the suggested spec of the export (unc13_dstorm.yaml). Copy the two camera values with all their digits. With 457.04 and 13.86, the detector finds 4,193 blobs, not the 4,192 of the notebook. The spec adds some defaults so that you can see them. This table tells what each setting means for this recording:

    Setting Value Meaning for this question
    pixel_nm 127.0 The pixel size in the sample. The model gives all positions and the PSF sd in nm
    offset_adu, scale_adu 457.04, 13.86 The camera calibration of notebook section 1. The model changes ADU to effective counts: (ADU − 457.04) / 13.86. These counts are Poisson with a mean of half the photons. Set the two values together
    kind flat One background value in each patch, as in the notebook. plane adds a slope in x and y (notebook "Try it yourself", item 1)
    max_spots 200 The number of isolated spots to fit, evenly spaced in frame order. The notebook fits one spot with NUTS
    threshold_sd, smooth_px 5, 1 The detector of notebook section 3: smooth each frame with a Gaussian of 1 px and keep the regions above 5 robust noise sds
    patch_px 11 Each spot gets a patch of 11 × 11 pixels (1.4 µm). The value must be odd
    photons_median 1500 The prior median of the photons of a spot, as in the notebook. The prior sd on the log scale is 1 (photons_log_sd)
    psf_sd_px 1.0 The prior median of the PSF sd: one pixel (127 nm), with a log sd of 0.2. A 1.49 NA objective at 670 nm gives about this width
    position_sd_px 2 The prior sd of the position around the centre of the detected blob (px)
    score_draws 40 The posterior draws that each score uses, and the draws of the render
    ppc_draws 20 The predictive patch draws that the fit keeps for sci.image_residuals
    render_px_nm 16 The pixel size of the super-resolved image render. The render is 318 × 318 pixels
    • fit.mode: train_only fits on all of the frames. The model has no split, because the detector needs each frame. Without this line, the validator shows the warning SPLIT_INVALID ("There is no holdout for the evaluation"). The suggested spec of the export does not have this line, so add it.
    • engine: nutpie is the sampler of the notebook. The default engine of the model is pymc. It gives the same posterior, but it is slower.
    • keep.log_likelihood: true keeps the pointwise log likelihood of each patch pixel. The fit then calculates elpd_loo. You need it to compare flat and plane (step 6.5).
    • The fit does not calculate rmse, mae and coverage_90, because there is no holdout. The Evaluate score of step 7.2 uses them.

    Settings that the validator does not check

    Validate checks the type and the range of each setting. It does not check the rules between settings. For example, offset_adu without scale_adu, or an even patch_px, is valid in the editor. The fit then stops at once with SETTINGS_INVALID, before the detector starts.

    5. Fit and watch the run¶

    1. Click Fit. The app opens the run page.
    2. The badge shows Live while the run sends events.
    3. The Progress card shows the steps of the fit. The Events card shows the stages of the run and, at the end, the diagnostics. The Logs card stays empty when the model writes no output.
    4. When the run is done, the Result card shows Inspect model://microscopy/unc13-dstorm-localisation@v1. Click it.

    The first progress message tells you what the detector found: "200 spots of 4192 blobs selected". The detector finds 4,192 blobs, as in the notebook. 1,503 of them are isolated in their patch and have 13 to 39 pixels. The fit uses 200 of the isolated blobs. Then nutpie samples the 1,000 parameters (5 for each spot) in one model. The last message is "posterior ready".

    After the fit, the run calculates the plots and the explorer views. You can also find the run under Runs. Filter it with the kind fit.

    The warning EVALUATION_VIEW_FAILED

    After the fit, the run tries to score all 2,000 frames for the plots of the predictions. That score has more than 50 million values, so the model refuses it. The run shows the warning EVALUATION_VIEW_FAILED, and the fit is correct. The four plots of the spec do not need that score. The fit stores the super-resolved image and the patch draws in the model version.

    6. Read the result¶

    On the model version page, the tabs are Overview, Diagnostics, Plots, Data tree, Preprocessing and Provenance. Click Explore to open the Model Explorer.

    6.1 Can you trust the fit?¶

    Open Diagnostics in the Model Explorer (group "Can you trust it?").

    Expect these results:

    Code Value Message
    RHAT 1.018 "The independent sampler runs do not agree about 'logN'. Its estimates are not reliable at this time."
    ESS_BULK 426 "There are enough effective draws for stable averages."
    ESS_TAIL 389 "There is too little independent information about 'logs' (about 389 effective draws). The intervals are not stable."
    DIVERGENCES 0 "The sampler had no problems with the model."
    PARETO_K_HIGH 47 "Some data points have a strong effect on the model, so the leave-one-out score is only approximate."

    The warnings are for three of the 200 spots: 18, 56 and 66. On the Plots tab, the caption of core.convergence says "16 of 2000 parameters have not converged". It names photons[66], logN[66] and x[66]. Spot 66 is the brightest spot, with about 9,800 photons, and its error bar is 24 nm. A blob this bright is possibly two molecules (notebook section 7). Most of the other spots have a bulk ESS of more than 1,000. If you want fewer warnings, fit with tune: 2000.

    The model has its own check, PATCH_MISFIT. It shows when the Pearson χ² for each patch pixel is more than 1.5 at the posterior mean. The flat model does not show it on this recording.

    6.2 What did the model learn?¶

    Open Parameters (group "What it learned"). The posterior has these variables, each with one value for each spot:

    Variable Meaning
    x_px, y_px The position in the patch (px). These are the parameters of the notebook table
    logN, logb, logs The log of the photons, of the background and of the PSF sd (px)
    x, y The position in the movie (nm): the column or row number times pixel_nm
    photons The photons that the molecule emitted in the frame
    background The background at the centre of the patch (photons for each pixel)
    psf_sd The PSF sd (nm)

    The table shows the posterior mean, the sd and the 90% interval of each value. It has 2,000 rows, so it does not show all of them. The summary of the 200 spots:

    Quantity This fit Notebook
    Blobs found by the detector 4,192 4,192
    EM gain (ADU for each photon) 6.93 6.9
    Median background (photons for each pixel) 115 about 110
    Median PSF sd 137 nm 125 nm for blob 12
    Median photons of a spot 2,862 about 2,300 (the 4,506 kept localisations)
    Median error bar of a position 7.5 nm 9.5 nm (the 4,506 kept localisations)
    Spots with an error bar of less than 30 nm 198 of 200 (99%) 4,506 of 8,342 (54%)

    The error bar of a spot is the posterior sd of x and y (the root mean square of the two). The fit uses only isolated blobs of 13 to 39 pixels. These are brighter and cleaner than the localisations of the notebook, which include the faint extra emitters of the count model. Thus the median error bar is smaller, and almost all spots pass the 30 nm filter.

    6.3 The plots¶

    On the model version page, open Plots:

    Plot What to look for
    core.convergence R-hat near 1. Some warnings for faint spots (step 6.1)
    sci.point_pattern Each dot is one spot at its posterior mean, with an ellipse that has the true position with 90% probability. The background is the mean of render. Small ellipses are bright spots. A large ellipse is a spot that you must not trust
    sci.image_residuals For each spot: the observed patch, the mean of 20 predictive draws, the residual and the normalised residual. The normalised residual must look like noise. A ring or a second peak shows a second molecule or a wrong PSF
    sci.field_map The mean and the sd of render, the super-resolved image of the 200 spots. The sd is high where the positions are not certain

    Use the slider or Play in sci.image_residuals to see each spot. In this fit, the caption says: "The normalised residuals have an sd of 1.07 and 6% are outside ±2." A perfect model gives 1 and 5%. Thus the flat Gaussian model is close for these isolated spots. The caption of sci.point_pattern says: "The median sd of a coordinate is 7.25."

    The Fields section

    The Model Explorer section Fields shows the grid outputs of a model. For this model, it needs an in-sample score of the full movie, and the model refuses that score (the note in step 5). If Fields shows "The server cannot calculate this view for this data or model.", use the plots sci.field_map and sci.image_residuals. They show the same outputs from the model version.

    6.4 The decision¶

    The decision. Keep each localisation with an error bar of less than 30 nm. Draw each one as a Gaussian with its own error bar. Do not use the count of molecules in a bright blob.

    To make the decision from these outputs:

    1. Score x and y with their sd (step 7.1). The error bar of a spot is √((sd_x² + sd_y²) / 2).
    2. Remove the spots with an error bar of 30 nm or more. In this fit, two spots go (spots 40 and 54, with 47 nm and 74 nm).
    3. Look at sci.point_pattern. The large ellipses are the spots that the filter removes.
    4. Look at the residuals of the brightest spots in sci.image_residuals. If you see a ring or a slope in the residual, the flat Gaussian model does not describe the spot. Then fit kind: plane (step 6.5) or remove the spot.

    The render render is the mean of 40 posterior draws of the positions. Thus a spot with a large error bar spreads over many pixels and is faint. That is the image of notebook section 7.

    6.5 One spot as in the notebook, and a sloped background¶

    One spot. To fit the spot of notebook section 3 (blob 12 in frame 5), save a copy of the spec as unc13-one-spot and change two settings:

        max_spots: 1
    amplitude_band: [0.45, 0.55]

    amplitude_band keeps the isolated blobs whose amplitude is between the 45% and 55% quantiles: a typical spot. The fit takes some seconds. These are the results:

    Parameter This fit Notebook
    x_px 4.460 ± 0.053 4.461 ± 0.056
    y_px 4.399 ± 0.052 4.399 ± 0.053
    sd of x, y 6.8 nm, 6.7 nm 7.1 nm, 6.7 nm
    photons 2,161 2,157
    background 112 112
    psf_sd 125 nm 125 nm

    R-hat is 1.002 and there are no divergences.

    A sloped background. Fit the spec again with kind: plane and compare the two versions by elpd_loo (Compare on the model version page). Both versions use the same 200 patches. On this recording, the plane model does not converge with the default gradient_sd (R-hat 1.76) and it shows PATCH_MISFIT. Its elpd_loo is lower (−94,436 against −85,943). Keep kind: flat for these isolated spots.

    7. Score the model¶

    A score uses only the spots that the fit found. It cannot find spots in new frames. Each score also makes frames_hat, the camera image of each scored frame. For all 2,000 frames, frames_hat has more than 50 million values, and the model refuses the score with PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE. Thus you score a short part of the movie.

    Make a dataset with the first 100 frames. Run this Python script in the directory of the export:

    import xarray as xr
from mapi_plugin_science import storm_data
from mapi_plugin_science.export import write_export

ds = xr.load_dataset("unc13_dstorm.nc")
window = ds.isel(frame=slice(0, 100)).copy()
window.attrs.pop("mapi_schema", None)  # write_export adds the schema again
write_export(window, "unc13_dstorm_first100.nc", schema=storm_data.STORM_SCHEMA)

    Upload unc13_dstorm_first100.nc as the dataset unc13_dstorm_first100, as in step 3.

    7.1 The positions with their error bars¶

    1. On the model version page, click Score.
    2. In What do you want to do?, select Predict.
    3. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select unc13_dstorm_first100 in Dataset.
    4. Open Advanced. Set Seed (optional) to 58. Type x, y, photons, psf_sd in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Predict.

    The Results card shows the mean and the 90% interval (hdi_90) of each output for each of the 200 spots. The frames of the dataset do not change these values. For example, spot 0 is at x = 2,386 nm (90% interval 2,379 to 2,389 nm) with 4,975 photons.

    No new data

    The page does not show No new data for this model. The model cannot forecast, so a request without a dataset has no forecast horizon, and the server refuses it. A note under Values to score gives this reason. Do not select the full movie dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm. The model then refuses the score with PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE.

    This is the request that the page sends (POST /v1/models/{model_id}/versions/{v}:score):

    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",

    The sd of x and y gives the error bar of step 6.4. To get the camera image of the fit, type frames_hat in Variables (optional). Set Quantity to Predictive to add Poisson noise to the background and to the patches.

    7.2 Evaluate the fit on the frames¶

    1. In What do you want to do?, select Evaluate.
    2. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select unc13_dstorm_first100.
    3. Open Advanced. Set Quantity to Predictive and Seed (optional) to 58.
    4. Click Evaluate.
    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://microscopy/unc13-dstorm-localisation@latest",
  "mode": "evaluate",
  "data": "dataset://microscopy/unc13_dstorm_first100@latest",
  "output": {"kind": "predictive"},
  "seed": 58
}

    The metrics compare frames_hat with the frames of the dataset. In this fit: rmse 469 ADU, mae 138 ADU and coverage_90 0.81. Spots that the fit did not use are misfit, because the model shows only the background there. Thus the coverage is less than 0.9. Use the metrics to compare two fits of the same frames, not as a measure of one spot.

    • The model has no simulate mode. To make new frames from known blinks, use storm_data.simulate_storm() in Python.
    • If you promote the version to an endpoint, Integrations → the endpoint → Schema and Try it sends the same requests.

    8. Run a workflow recipe¶

    The recipe sci_image_model_check (Image model check) is for models that predict an image. It fits the model, makes the residual maps and the image grid, does a posterior predictive check and writes a report.

    1. Set the header to Guided and open Recipes. In Expert mode, open Workflows and then the Templates tab.
    2. Find the card Image model check and click Run on my data.
    3. In 1. Your data, select dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm in Dataset.
    4. In 2. What the recipe needs to know, type sci.storm_localisation@^1 in Model.
    5. Open Fine-tune (optional: the defaults are correct for most data). Set these values:
      • Settings: the settings of step 4 as JSON, for example {"pixel_nm": 127.0, "offset_adu": 457.04184713091655, "scale_adu": 13.861648333299126, "max_spots": 200}.
      • Draws 1000, Tune 1000 and Seed 58.
      • Var: patch_fit. The image check is for the fitted patches, one image for each spot.
    6. Click Run this recipe.

    The monitor of the run has the tabs Graph, Steps, Decisions, Outputs and Events. Outputs shows the parameter table, the plots sci.image_residuals and sci.ppc_image_grid of patch_fit, and the report.

    The recipe bayes_workflow_audit (Bayesian workflow audit) does all the checks of a Bayesian workflow on any model with draws: prior predictive draws, a convergence gate, the posterior predictive check, sensitivity.prior and recovery.simulated. Run it in the same way with the model sci.storm_localisation@^1. The gate needs the largest R-hat below Rhat max (1.01) and the smallest bulk ESS at or above Min ess (400). The R-hat of logN in step 6 is 1.018, so the default gate stops the audit before the checks. Increase Draws and Tune, or make the gate less strict.

    The posterior predictive check of a long movie

    If a step of a recipe scores all 2,000 frames, the model refuses it with PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE. Then run the recipe on a shorter movie. A dataset of the first 500 frames is below the limit with score_draws: 40. Make it with the script of step 7 and slice(0, 500).

    The CLI has no models command. Take the model id from the run (mapi runs get <run>) or from Models in the web app.

    import numpy as np
from mapi_client import Client

api = Client(project="<tenant>/microscopy")
api.datasets.upload("unc13_dstorm.nc", dataset="dannhauser2022_unc13_dstorm")
api.datasets.upload("unc13_dstorm_first100.nc", dataset="unc13_dstorm_first100")

with open("unc13-dstorm-localisation.yaml", encoding="utf-8") as fh:
    spec_yaml = fh.read()
revision = api.specs.upsert(spec_yaml)
run = api.runs.fit(revision.spec_id, revision.revision)
run = api.runs.wait(run.id, raise_on_failure=True)

model = next(m for m in api.models.list() if m.name == "unc13-dstorm-localisation")
result = api.models.score(
    model.id,
    model.latest_version,
    {
        "model": "model://microscopy/unc13-dstorm-localisation@latest",
        "mode": "predict",
        "data": "dataset://microscopy/unc13_dstorm_first100@latest",
        "output": {"summary": ["mean", "sd"], "vars": ["x", "y"]},
        "seed": 58,
    },
)
summary = result.tree["summary"]  # an xr.DataTree (needs the `xarray` extra)
sd_x = summary["x"].sel(stat="sd").values
sd_y = summary["y"].sel(stat="sd").values
error_nm = np.sqrt((sd_x**2 + sd_y**2) / 2)
keep = error_nm < 30  # the quality filter of the notebook
print(f"{keep.sum()} of {keep.size} spots pass, median error {np.median(error_nm):.1f} nm")

    api.datasets.upload also takes an xr.Dataset. Thus you can upload the 100-frame window of step 7 without a file: api.datasets.upload(window, dataset="unc13_dstorm_first100").

    10. Differences from the notebook¶

    The port keeps the camera model of notebook sections 1 and 2 and the Bayesian localisation of section 3, for many spots at once. It leaves out these parts:

    • The photon-transfer curve of the full field. The recording has no dark frames. The notebook fits the curve on the statistics of the full 200 × 200 field. The export does the same and gives the result as offset_adu and scale_adu. If you do not set them, the model fits the curve on the 40 × 40 crop. The crop is over one bright structure, so that calibration is less good.
    • The simulated camera check (section 2). The package tests of the plugin do the same check.
    • The least squares and maximum likelihood fits, and the Cramér-Rao bound (section 4). The model gives only the Bayesian posterior.
    • The emitter count (sections 5 and 6). The model fits one emitter in each patch. It uses only isolated blobs of 13 to 39 pixels. It does not do the Laplace evidence, the SMC check or the comparison with a simple threshold at two densities.
    • The Laplace fit of all spots (section 7). The notebook localises all 8,342 spots with a Laplace approximation. The model samples up to max_spots (at most 5,000) isolated spots with NUTS.
    • The Fourier ring correlation (section 7). The platform does not calculate it.
    • The count of molecules from their blinks (section 8). The platform does not have the negative-binomial blink model.

    Next steps:

    • Fit the simulated movie of step 2 (--synthetic). In Python, storm_data.simulate_storm() returns the same movie and its true blinks, so you can measure the error of each position.
