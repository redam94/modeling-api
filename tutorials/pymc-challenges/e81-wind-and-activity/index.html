    E81 wind directions and animal activity (circular)¶

    This tutorial does the work of the pymc-challenges notebook E81_circular_data.py in the web app. You use the model sci.circular_regression@1. You do not write PyMC code.

    You need:

    • The platform (start it with make dev) and two projects that you can upload datasets to. This tutorial uses the projects meteorology (the wind) and ecology (the camera traps). If your projects have different names, change the project part of each dataset:// and model:// reference, and metadata.project in the specs.
    • A local copy of pymc-challenges. The export commands read one file each from its data/ directory.
    • About 20 minutes. The two fits of the main path take about one minute of sandbox time together.

    1. The question¶

    Some measurements are angles. A wind blows from a direction between 0 and 360 degrees, and 359 degrees is next to 1 degree. An animal walks past a camera at a time of day, and 23:59 is next to 00:01. An arithmetic mean or a normal likelihood on these values can give a wrong answer with high confidence. The notebook uses two real datasets:

    • Wind at Santa Barbara airport (ASOS station KSBA, 2021 to 2023). At night, cool air flows down from the mountains to the sea. In the afternoon, the sea breeze blows inland. Thus the direction turns through half of the compass each day, and the turn changes with the season.
    • Camera traps on Barro Colorado Island, Panama (Rowcliffe et al. 2014). The ocelot hunts at night. The data give the time of day of 16,282 photographs of the ocelot and five animals that it can hunt.

    The notebook asks these questions:

    1. How does the wind direction change with the hour and the season, and how sure are you? (Notebook parts B and D.)
    2. Which model of the direction predicts better on a year that it did not see? The models are one von Mises direction for each hour and season, a projected normal and a mixture of two wind regimes. (Part D, LOO and the held-out year 2023.)
    3. When is the ocelot active, and how much does its day overlap with the day of each prey animal? (Part E, the log-Fourier density, the overlap and the activity level.)

    The notebook ends with these decisions:

    • Use the von Mises in its natural parameters (the canonical link). It has one mode, and it has no seam at ±180 degrees. Do not use a uniform prior on an angle.
    • For the wind, a mixture of two regimes predicts much better than one direction for each hour. The single von Mises puts mass between the two lobes of the evening rose, where the wind seldom blows.
    • Report the overlap of two activity curves with its posterior range. The ocelot overlaps least with the agouti (0.30) and most with the brocket, the spiny rat and the paca (0.66 to 0.75).

    What you will get, and the notebook section that each item replaces:

    In the web app Notebook
    The rose of the observed directions with the range of the model in each sector (sci.rose) Part D, the circular posterior predictive check
    The density of the direction in each block of three hours, with mean, sd and draws (Fields, sci.field_map) Part B, the rose diagrams by hour, and part D, the model roses
    The mean direction and the mean resultant length of each block of three hours (Score) Part D, the clock of wind arrows
    elpd_loo of the 12,023 reports of 2021 and 2022 (Metrics) Part D, "Which model predicts better?"
    The circular holdout metrics of 2023 (Score, mode Evaluate) Part D, the held-out year
    The activity density of each species (Fields, sci.profile_bands) Part E, the log-Fourier density
    The overlap of each pair of species, and the activity level of each species (Fields and Score) Part E, the overlap and the activity level

    See Differences from the notebook for the parts that the port leaves out.

    2. Get the data¶

    The two datasets have different cases in the export command: e81 for the wind and e81b for the camera traps. Run the commands from the root of the modeling-api repository.

    2.1 The wind¶

    The export reads asos_ksba_2021_2023.csv.gz. It removes the calm reports, because a calm wind has no direction. It uses Pacific standard time (UTC − 8) all year, so that the sea breeze follows the sun and not the clock.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e81 \
    --challenges <path to pymc-challenges>/data \
    --out ksba_wind_2021_2023.nc --spec ksba_wind_2021_2023.yaml

    The command writes:

    wrote ksba_wind_2021_2023.nc (variables wind_from, hour_lst, day_of_year, year on report=18049)
upload it as dataset meteorology/ksba_wind_2021_2023 and fit sci.circular_regression
wrote the suggested spec to ksba_wind_2021_2023.yaml

    The file holds one dataset:

    Name Dims Type Meaning
    report 18,049 integer, policy extend The reports in time order. The 6,026 reports of 2023 are the last ones
    wind_from report float64, target, deg The direction that the wind blows from, clockwise from true north. ASOS gives it in steps of 10 degrees
    hour_lst report float64, covariate, h Hours since midnight, local standard time
    day_of_year report float64, covariate, d Days since 1 January, with the fraction of the day
    year report int64, constant The year of the report: 6,096 reports in 2021, 5,919 in 2022 and 6,026 in 2023

    2.2 The camera traps¶

    The export reads bci_camera_trap_times.csv. It keeps the ocelot and the five prey species of the notebook, and it changes the time of each photograph to hours since midnight.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e81b \
    --challenges <path to pymc-challenges>/data \
    --out bci_camera_traps.nc --spec bci_camera_traps.yaml

    The command writes:

    wrote bci_camera_traps.nc (variables clock_time, species_idx on record=16282, species=6)
upload it as dataset ecology/bci_camera_traps and fit sci.circular_regression
wrote the suggested spec to bci_camera_traps.yaml
    Name Dims Type Meaning
    record 16,282 integer, policy extend One photograph
    species 6 categorical, policy fixed ocelot, agouti, paca, rat, brocket and peccary
    clock_time record float64, target, h The time of day of the photograph, 0 to 24 hours
    species_idx record int64, index of species The species of the photograph

    The attribute mapi_schema of each file gives each variable its role, so you do not map columns.

    No pymc-challenges copy?

    --synthetic writes data that the model itself simulates, with known parameters. The wind file has 480 reports of one year (2021). The camera-trap file has 700 records of three groups (owl, lark and drifter). Use them to try the steps. Remove the split line from the wind spec of step 4, because the synthetic file has no year 2023. The numbers in this tutorial are for the real data.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e81 --synthetic --out wind.nc
uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e81b --synthetic --out activity.nc

    3. Upload the datasets¶

    Do these steps in the project meteorology for the wind file. Then do them again in the project ecology for the camera-trap file.

    1. Open Datasets in the sidebar.
    2. Click New dataset.
    3. Type ksba_wind_2021_2023 in Name. Type a description, for example "Hourly wind directions at Santa Barbara airport, 2021 to 2023, without calms".
    4. Click Create dataset. The upload panel opens.
    5. Click Choose files and select ksba_wind_2021_2023.nc.
    6. Make sure that the format is netCDF (or HDF5 written by xarray).
    7. Optional: click Preview structure. The panel shows Structure of the file. Make sure that it shows four variables on report.
    8. Optional: type a message in Version message (optional).
    9. Click Upload and ingest.

    For the camera traps, use the name bci_camera_traps and the file bci_camera_traps.nc. Structure of the file shows clock_time and species_idx on record, and the coordinate species.

    The ingest run reads the file in the sandbox. When it is done, the version page shows the tabs Schema, Coordinates, Missingness, Preview and About. On Schema, wind_from has the role target and the units deg. clock_time has the role target and the units h.

    Each file is one plain dataset. You do not need the tree fields ("Put this file in a node of a tree (optional)") or the HDF5 path.

    4. Build the specs¶

    4.1 The wind¶

    1. Open Build and click New spec. The editor opens with the geo MMM template.
    2. Set the Editor view to YAML.
    3. Open the Insert panel on the right.
    4. Open the Datasets section and select ksba_wind_2021_2023. The toast "Dataset set" shows.
    5. Type circular in the search field. Under Models, select Directions and times of day (sci.circular_regression@^1). The toast "Model set" shows.
    6. Select all the text in the editor and paste the full spec below. The template has other blocks (a pipeline, a split and the MMM plots), so the spec must replace all of it.
    7. Click Validate. The status shows valid.
    8. Click Save spec. The spec gets its name ksba-wind-direction from metadata.name.

    This is the full spec:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: ksba-wind-direction, project: meteorology}
data:
  dataset: dataset://meteorology/ksba_wind_2021_2023@latest
  bind:
    dims: {obs: report}
    vars: {direction: wind_from, hour: hour_lst, day: day_of_year}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: obs, last: 6026}
model:
  use: sci.circular_regression@^1
  settings: {kind: von_mises, units: degrees, daily_harmonics: 3, annual_harmonics: 2,
             interaction_harmonics: 2}
  fit: {mode: train_only, seed: 81, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, engine: nutpie,
        keep: {log_likelihood: true, posterior_predictive: {thin: 20}}}
evaluate: {metrics: [elpd_loo]}
outputs:
  plots: [core.convergence, core.posterior_forest, sci.rose, sci.profile_bands, sci.field_map]

    The model calls its row dim obs, the target direction, and the covariates hour and day. The bind block connects them to the names in the dataset.

    The split and the fit mode do the same as the notebook:

    • split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: obs, last: 6026} keeps the last 6,026 reports (all of 2023) out of the fit.
    • fit.mode: train_only fits on the 12,023 reports of 2021 and 2022, and it does not fit again on all reports. Thus the model version is the model of the notebook, and you can score 2023 in step 7.
    • keep.log_likelihood: true keeps the log density of each report, for elpd_loo.
    • keep.posterior_predictive: {thin: 20} keeps simulated directions from 200 draws, for the rose of sci.rose.

    The suggested spec of the export (ksba_wind_2021_2023.yaml) is the same spec without sci.profile_bands in the plots.

    The settings for this question:

    Setting Value What it means here
    kind von_mises One von Mises direction for each report. The hour and the day of the year move its natural parameters η = F B (the canonical link). The mean direction is atan2(η₂, η₁) and the concentration is |η|. regime_mixture is the mixture of two regimes (section 6.5)
    units degrees The direction is in degrees. A turn is 360. Use hours for a time of day and radians for radians
    daily_harmonics 3 The cosine and the sine of 1, 2 and 3 cycles each day. Midnight is next to 00:01
    annual_harmonics 2 The cosine and the sine of 1 and 2 cycles each year. 31 December is next to 1 January
    interaction_harmonics 2 The first two daily harmonics times the first annual harmonic. The daily turn of the wind can change with the season. With 3, 2 and 2, the model has 19 features, the same as the notebook
    intercept_sd, coef_sd 2, 1 (default) The prior sds of the intercepts and of the other coefficients, as in the notebook
    frames 8 (default) The density output has one row for each block of 24 / 8 = 3 hours, as the rose diagrams of the notebook
    angle_points 72 (default) The density output has 72 angles, one each 5 degrees
    rows_per_frame 200 (default) The density of a block is the mean of the densities of up to 200 fit reports in that block
    score_draws 400 (default) The number of posterior draws that one score uses

    Settings that do not agree

    Validate does a check of each setting by itself. It does not compare two settings. For example, interaction_harmonics: 4 with daily_harmonics: 3 shows valid, but the run stops with SETTINGS_INVALID.

    4.2 The camera traps¶

    Make a second spec in the project ecology. Do the steps of section 4.1 with the dataset bci_camera_traps and the name bci-ocelot-activity:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: bci-ocelot-activity, project: ecology}
data:
  dataset: dataset://ecology/bci_camera_traps@latest
  bind:
    dims: {obs: record}
    vars: {direction: clock_time, species_idx: species_idx}
model:
  use: sci.circular_regression@^1
  settings: {kind: activity, units: hours, group_dim: species, harmonics: 16, tau_sd: 2.0,
             grid_points: 256}
  fit: {mode: train_only, seed: 81, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, engine: nutpie,
        keep: {log_likelihood: true}}
evaluate: {metrics: [elpd_loo]}
outputs: {plots: [core.convergence, sci.field_map, sci.profile_bands]}

    The settings for this question:

    Setting Value What it means here
    kind activity One log-Fourier density for each species: log f(θ) = Σ aₖ cos kθ + bₖ sin kθ − log Z. It has no components, so no labels can change places
    units hours The angle is a time of day. A turn is 24 hours
    group_dim species The dim of the groups. The index variable is species_idx
    harmonics 16 The number of Fourier terms K, as in the notebook
    tau_sd 2.0 The prior is aₖ, bₖ ~ N(0, (τ/k)²) with τ ~ HalfNormal(2) for each species. Higher harmonics shrink more. This prior sets how smooth each curve is
    grid_points 256 The number of angles in the sum that gives Z
    angle_points 72 (default) The density output has 72 times of day, one each 20 minutes

    fit.mode: train_only fits on all records. The spec has no split. Without this line, the validator shows the warning SPLIT_INVALID ("There is no holdout for the evaluation"). The suggested spec of the export (bci_camera_traps.yaml) does not have this line, so add it.

    engine: nutpie is the sampler of the notebook. The default engine of the model is pymc.

    5. Fit and watch the run¶

    1. Click Fit. The app opens the run page.
    2. The badge shows Live while the run sends events.
    3. The Progress card shows the progress of the sampler. The Events card shows the stages of the run and, at the end, the diagnostics. The Logs card stays empty when the model writes no output.
    4. When the run is done, the Result card shows Inspect model://meteorology/ksba-wind-direction@v1. Click it.

    On a laptop with nutpie, the wind fit took 45 seconds and the camera-trap fit took 21 seconds. After the sampler, the run calculates the pointwise log densities, the simulated directions, the density field, the plots and the explorer views.

    You can also find the run under Runs. Filter it with the kind fit.

    6. Read the result¶

    On the model version page, the tabs are Overview, Diagnostics, Plots, Data tree, Preprocessing and Provenance. Click Explore to open the Model Explorer. Sections that do not apply to this model are under "Does not apply".

    6.1 Can you trust the fit?¶

    Open Diagnostics in the Model Explorer, or the plot Sampler convergence on the Plots tab. In the wind fit for this tutorial, there were no divergences. The largest R-hat was 1.003 and the smallest bulk ESS was 6,416 (of 4,000 draws). The caption says "All 614 parameters look converged". This count includes the cells of the density field.

    In the camera-trap fit, there were no divergences. The largest R-hat was 1.010 and the smallest bulk ESS was 578. Both values are for the standardised coefficients za and zb.

    The canonical link gives one mode. Thus the four chains agree without special start values. The notebook shows two problems of other models that give zero divergences: a wall at ±180 degrees from a uniform prior, and a second mode of the tan-half link. This model has neither problem.

    6.2 The coefficients¶

    Parameters in the Model Explorer, and the plot Posterior forest, show the coefficients B(feature, component). There are 19 features (1, cos1h, sin1h, … sin2h*sin1d) and two components, eta1 and eta2. The components are the natural parameters of the von Mises. eta1 points to north and eta2 points to east. A coefficient by itself is hard to read. Use the mean directions of section 6.4 for the result.

    6.3 Where does the wind come from? The fields¶

    Open Fields in the Model Explorer. The model has 1 output on a grid: density, the predictive density of the direction, per degree. The rows are the 8 blocks of three hours. The label of a block is the hour at its start (0, 3, … 21). The columns are the 72 directions, from 0 to 355 degrees.

    1. Select Mean in Statistic. A bright cell is a direction that the wind often comes from in that block.
    2. Select Std. dev. to see where you are not sure of the density.
    3. Select Draws and click Play the draws to see possible densities that agree with the data.
    4. Move the pointer over a cell to read its values.

    The plot Field map of density on the Plots tab shows the same output as panels (mean and sd). Look for these features, which the notebook also finds:

    • Night (21:00 to 06:00). A wide band from the north-east and the east: air that flows down from the mountains.
    • Afternoon (12:00 to 18:00). A narrow, bright band from the south-west (about 225 to 230 degrees): the sea breeze.
    • Evening (18:00 to 21:00). A flat, dark row. The wind comes from two directions in this block, and one von Mises cannot show two directions.

    6.4 The rose and the profiles¶

    The plot Rose of theta (sci.rose) shows a rose of the observed directions of 2021 and 2022. The thick radial lines show the 90% range of the same count in data that the model simulated. A bar that ends outside its range shows a direction that the model gets wrong.

    The default rose is in radians, and 0 is at the right

    The rose reads the angles theta in radians from the bundle. In its default settings, 0 is at the right (east on a compass), and the angles go anticlockwise. Thus a wind from the west (270 degrees) is at the bottom, not at the left. For a compass (0 at the top, clockwise), use sci.rose with clockwise: true in a plot.render step of a workflow (step 8).

    In the default rose (24 sectors of 15 degrees), only 4% of the bars end inside the range. Two causes make this number low:

    • ASOS gives the direction in steps of 10 degrees. Thus a sector of 15 degrees holds one or two of the reported values, and the observed bars go up and down. The simulated directions do not have steps.
    • One von Mises direction for each hour and season cannot put mass on two lobes at the same time.

    With bins: 12 (sectors of 30 degrees, as in the notebook) and clockwise: true, 42% of the bars end inside the range. This rose adds all hours together. Thus it does not show the misfit of each block, and it does not separate the models of section 6.6 (the mixture gives 33%). For each block, use the Fields of section 6.3.

    The plot Profiles of theta (sci.profile_bands) shows the simulated directions along report, with the 50% and 90% bands, and the observed directions as dots. The direction is an angle, so a band near 0 or 360 degrees is not a good summary. Use the rose and the fields.

    6.5 The mean resultant vectors¶

    The mean resultant vector of each block summarises the wind of that block. Its direction is mean_direction(frame), and its length is resultant_length(frame): 1 if all winds of the block come from one direction, near 0 if they come from all directions. Get these outputs with a score (step 7.1). In the fit for this tutorial, the values are:

    Block (start hour) mean_direction (degrees) resultant_length What it shows
    0 56 (52 to 60) 0.39 The night wind from the north-east
    3 37 (33 to 41) 0.43 The night wind from the north-east
    6 136 (132 to 139) 0.39 The morning wind from the south-east
    9 190 (189 to 192) 0.65 The turn to the sea breeze
    12 224 (223 to 225) 0.70 The sea breeze from the south-west
    15 230 (228 to 232) 0.54 The sea breeze from the south-west
    18 144 (133 to 154) 0.13 Two directions: the mean describes neither
    21 72 (69 to 75) 0.41 The night wind from the east-north-east

    The ranges are the 90% HDI of the posterior. A short vector (block 18) does not mean that the air is still, because the data have no calms. It means that the direction is not predictable. Always read a mean direction together with its length.

    6.6 Which model predicts better?¶

    The metrics are in the Metrics card of the Overview tab. elpd_loo scores the 12,023 reports of 2021 and 2022, in nats.

    Metric Value Meaning
    elpd_loo −17,385 (se 80, p_loo 38) The expected log density of a left-out report, summed over the reports. Higher is better. All Pareto k values are below 0.7

    To compare the models of the notebook, fit the spec again with each kind. Change only metadata.name and settings.kind, for example ksba-wind-regimes and kind: regime_mixture. Each fit makes a new model with its own elpd_loo. Compare the values only between fits on the same reports. On a laptop with nutpie, the mixture fit took 202 seconds.

    kind elpd_loo (this port) Notebook
    von_mises −17,385 −17,385
    regime_mixture −15,821 (se 97, p_loo 57) About 1,560 nats better than von_mises

    The mixture is better by 1,565 nats. Fields of the mixture shows why. In the block that starts at 9, the density has two peaks, at about 150 and 225 degrees. In the block that starts at 18, it has two peaks, at about 120 and 265 degrees. The von Mises regression has one peak in each block, between the two lobes.

    The reports are not independent

    The reports are hourly, and the wind of one hour is like the wind of the next hour. Thus the standard error of elpd_loo is too small. The held-out year (step 7.3) is a stricter test.

    6.7 When is the ocelot active?¶

    Open the model version bci-ocelot-activity, then Explore, then Fields. The model has 2 outputs on a grid. Select one in Output:

    • density: the activity density of each species (rows) at each time of day (columns, one each 20 minutes), per hour.
    • overlap: the coefficient of overlap of each pair of species (rows and columns). It is the area under the lower of the two densities: 1 for the same activity, 0 for animals that are never active at the same time.

    The plot Profiles of density (sci.profile_bands) shows the density of each species along the day with its 50% and 90% bands. Look for these features, which the notebook also finds:

    • The ocelot is mostly active at night, with a peak in the evening.
    • The agouti is active by day, with two peaks: after dawn and before dusk.
    • The paca and the spiny rat are active at night.
    • The red brocket deer is active at all hours.
    • The collared peccary is active by day, with peaks at dawn and dusk.

    In the fit for this tutorial, the overlaps of the ocelot with each prey are:

    6.9 Make the decisions¶

    Use these outputs to make the decisions of the notebook:

    1. The link. The model always uses the canonical link. Make sure that Diagnostics shows no divergences and an R-hat of 1.01 or less. You do not need special start values.
    2. One direction or two regimes. Compare elpd_loo of von_mises and regime_mixture (section 6.6). Then compare the Fields of the two versions in the blocks that start at 9 and 18. The mixture is better by 1,565 nats, and it shows the two lobes. Use it to predict the direction. Do not decide with mae or coverage_90 of step 7.3. They are almost the same for the two kinds (52.1 and 52.4 degrees, 0.87 and 0.87), because they use one point for each report and do not see two lobes.
    3. Report a direction with its length. Use mean_direction together with resultant_length (section 6.5). Do not report a mean direction with a length below about 0.2.
    4. Overlap with uncertainty. Report the median and the range of overlap (section 6.7). The ocelot overlaps least with the agouti. The ranges of the other four prey overlap each other, so do not rank them.

    7. Score the model¶

    7.1 Predict: the mean direction of each block¶

    1. On the model version page of ksba-wind-direction, click Score.
    2. In What do you want to do?, select Predict.
    3. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select ksba_wind_2021_2023.
    4. Open Advanced. Set Seed (optional) to 81. Type direction_hat, resultant, mean_direction, resultant_length in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Predict.

    The request that the page sends (POST /v1/models/{model_id}/versions/{v}:score) is:

    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://meteorology/ksba-wind-direction@latest",
  "mode": "predict",
  "data": "dataset://meteorology/ksba_wind_2021_2023@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "expected",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "vars": ["direction_hat", "resultant", "mean_direction", "resultant_length"]
  },
  "seed": 81
}

    The result has direction_hat(report) and resultant(report): the mean direction and the mean resultant length of the predictive distribution of each report. It also has mean_direction(frame) and resultant_length(frame) for the 8 blocks (section 6.5). The result also shows a warning: the dataset has directions after the fitted reports, and the predict mode does not use them. This is correct.

    The summary of a direction near north

    direction_hat and mean_direction are circular outputs. Their mean is the circular mean atan2(mean(sin θ), mean(cos θ)), and hdi_90 is centred on that mean. All values are from 0 to 360 degrees. For a report with a mean direction near north, the draws are on the two sides of north, and the interval can cross north. Then hdi_90_lower is more than hdi_90_upper: for example, from 350 to 10 degrees. In the table of summary.json, these variables have the field period (360).

    No new data

    The page does not show No new data for this model. The model cannot forecast, so a request without a dataset has no forecast horizon, and the server refuses it. A note under Values to score gives this reason.

    7.2 Simulate: new winds¶

    simulate gives one simulated direction for each report and each draw. Use it to see the spread of single winds around the mean direction.

    1. In What do you want to do?, select Simulate.
    2. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select ksba_wind_2021_2023.
    3. Open Advanced. Set Quantity to Predictive. Set Draws to 20.
    4. Type direction_hat in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Simulate.
    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://meteorology/ksba-wind-direction@latest",
  "mode": "simulate",
  "data": "dataset://meteorology/ksba_wind_2021_2023@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "predictive",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "draws": 20,
    "vars": ["direction_hat"]
  },
  "seed": 81
}

    Each draw of direction_hat is one simulated wind, in degrees from 0 to 360. The draws spread much more than the mean direction of section 6.5, because a single wind varies much around the mean. For a forecast of the wind at an hour of a future day, make a dataset with hour_lst and day_of_year of that hour, and any value in wind_from.

    7.3 Evaluate: the held-out year 2023¶

    The notebook scores the reports of 2023, which the fit did not see. To do this in the web app, make a dataset of the 2023 reports. Run this Python code in the directory of the exported file:

    import xarray as xr

ds = xr.load_dataset("ksba_wind_2021_2023.nc")
ds.isel(report=ds["year"].values == 2023).to_netcdf("ksba_wind_2023.nc")

    The new file has 6,026 reports and keeps the attribute mapi_schema. Upload it as the dataset ksba_wind_2023 (step 3). Then:

    1. In What do you want to do?, select Evaluate.
    2. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select ksba_wind_2023.
    3. Open Advanced. Set Quantity to Predictive.
    4. Click Evaluate.
    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://meteorology/ksba-wind-direction@latest",
  "mode": "evaluate",
  "data": "dataset://meteorology/ksba_wind_2023@latest",
  "output": {"kind": "predictive", "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"]},
  "seed": 81
}

    direction_hat is a circular output, so the metrics are circular. An error of 359 degrees against 1 degree is 2 degrees. In the fit for this tutorial, the metrics of 2023 are:

    Metric Value Meaning
    mae 52.1 degrees The mean error of the circular mean of the simulated winds
    rmse 67.0 degrees The root mean square error
    bias 2.8 degrees The mean signed error
    coverage_90 0.87 The share of the 2023 winds inside the central 90% range of the simulated winds
    crps 34.6 degrees The continuous ranked probability score with the arc distance. Lower is better

    mape, smape, wape and r2 are not defined for a circular output, and the result skips them.

    Use Predictive for the coverage

    With Expected, each draw is the mean direction of a report, not a single wind. The range of the mean is narrow, so coverage_90 is only 0.06. Use Predictive to compare single winds with their range.

    7.4 The camera traps¶

    For the activity model, the outputs for each species are activity_level(frame), mean_direction(frame) (the mean time of day), resultant_length(frame), density(frame, angle) and overlap(frame, frame_). Send the dataset that you fitted:

    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://ecology/bci-ocelot-activity@latest",
  "mode": "predict",
  "data": "dataset://ecology/bci_camera_traps@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "expected",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "vars": ["activity_level", "mean_direction", "resultant_length", "overlap"]
  },
  "seed": 81
}

    activity_level is 1 / (2π max f), the part of the day that a species is active, if all animals are active at the peak of the curve. In the fit for this tutorial, the ocelot is active 0.34 of the day (90% HDI 0.27 to 0.41), and the agouti 0.26 (0.25 to 0.28).

    • The model has no forecast mode.
    • A score dataset with a species that the fit did not see stops the score.
    • A score dataset without hour_lst or day_of_year (wind) stops with MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE.

    If you promote a version to an endpoint, Integrations → the endpoint → Schema and Try it sends the same requests.

    8. Run a workflow recipe¶

    The recipe bayes_workflow_audit (title Bayesian workflow audit) does the checks of a Bayesian workflow on any model with draws. It fits the model, gates on convergence (R-hat below 1.01 and a bulk ESS of 400 or more), makes the posterior predictive check, and runs the governance checks sensitivity.prior and recovery.simulated. Then it writes a report with the plots.

    1. Set the header to Guided and open Recipes. In Expert mode, open Workflows and then the Templates tab.
    2. Find the card Bayesian workflow audit and click Run on my data.
    3. In 1. Your data, select ksba_wind_2021_2023 in Dataset.
    4. In 2. What the recipe needs to know, type sci.circular_regression@^1 in Model.
    5. Open Fine-tune (optional: the defaults are correct for most data).
    6. In Settings, type the settings of step 4 as JSON: {"kind": "von_mises", "units": "degrees", "daily_harmonics": 3, "annual_harmonics": 2, "interaction_harmonics": 2}.
    7. In Bind, type the bind of step 4 as JSON: {"dims": {"obs": "report"}, "vars": {"direction": "wind_from", "hour": "hour_lst", "day": "day_of_year"}}.
    8. Click Run this recipe.

    The recipe can bind wind_from to direction, because the dataset has one target. It cannot bind hour_lst and day_of_year, because they both have the role covariate. Thus give the bind of step 7.

    The monitor of the run has the tabs Graph, Steps, Decisions, Outputs and Events. Decisions shows the convergence gate and the value that it read. Outputs shows the plots and the report. The model version link opens the Model Explorer.

    The audit fits all three years

    The recipe has no split. Its fit uses all 18,049 reports, so its elpd_loo is not on the same scale as the value of step 6.6.

    The checks of the audit and a circular target

    The model keeps prior draws of its coefficients, but not of the directions. Thus the plot "Prior predictive check" of the report can be empty. evaluate.ppc compares the simulated and the observed angles as numbers on a line, so read its coverage and p-value with care for winds near north. The checks that refit the model (sensitivity.prior, recovery.simulated) each take as long as one fit.

    To see the rose as a compass, add a plot.render node in the workflow editor (click Use this template on the card). Give it the input model: fit.model and with: {recipe: sci.rose, source: model, settings: {clockwise: true, bins: 12}}.

    The recipes sci_spatial_ladder, sci_network_ladder, sci_phylo_signal, sci_calibrate_then_design and sci_image_model_check are for other science models. They do not apply to this model.

    10. Differences from the notebook¶

    The port keeps the circular-linear regressions of part D: von Mises with the canonical link, projected normal and the mixture of two regimes. It also keeps the log-Fourier activity densities, the overlaps and the activity levels of part E. It leaves out these parts:

    • The control dataset and the four distributions (part A). The port does not fit the Oklahoma earthquake times, and it has no wrapped normal or wrapped Cauchy. To do a check of "no daily pattern", fit kind: activity with one group and units: hours, and look for a flat density.
    • The direction against the wind speed (part C). The dataset has no speed, and the model has no tan-half link. The notebook shows that link only to show its two problems.
    • The log score of the held-out year. The web app gives circular holdout metrics (step 7.3), not the log predictive density. For the log score, use CircularRegressionModel().holdout_log_score(bundle.model, ds) in Python.
    • The day-level standard error of the 2023 difference. Calculate it in Python from the pointwise log scores.
    • The circular PIT, the clock of wind arrows and the arcs of the circular HDI (part D). The web app has no plot for them. mean_direction and resultant_length (step 7.1) give the values of the arrows for each block of three hours.
    • The gate of the mixture by hour and season (part D). The model does not output the probability of each regime.
    • The von Mises mixtures of the activity, and their labels that change places (part E). The port uses only the log-Fourier density, which has no labels. The kernel estimate and the bootstrap of the R package overlap are not in the port.
