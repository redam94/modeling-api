    E77 how languages name colours¶

    This tutorial does the work of the pymc-challenges notebook E77_colour_naming.py in the web app. You use the model sci.colour_naming@1. You do not write PyMC code.

    You need:

    • The platform (start it with make dev) and a project that you can upload datasets to. This tutorial uses the project linguistics. If your project has a different name, change the project part of each dataset:// and model:// reference, and metadata.project in the spec.
    • A local copy of pymc-challenges. The export command reads four files from its data/ directory.
    • About 15 minutes. The fit takes about four minutes of sandbox time.

    1. The question¶

    The World Color Survey (WCS) asked the speakers of 110 unwritten languages to name 330 colour chips. Each speaker named each chip once. This tutorial uses Nafaanra, a language of Ghana with 29 speakers in the survey. Nafaanra has a small system of four major terms: F (finge, white and the palest chips), W (wɔɔ, dark, green, blue and purple), N (nyie, red, orange, yellow and brown) and B (bubuo, some pale purples).

    The notebook asks these questions of the naming of one language:

    1. Where are the boundaries between the colour terms, and how sure are you of them? (Notebook parts A, B and D.)
    2. Do the speakers agree? 55% of the speakers call a chip N. Is this chip on a fuzzy boundary that all speakers share? Or is it near a sharp boundary that each speaker puts in a different place? (Part C.)
    3. How many categories does the language really have? (Part E, the effective number of categories.)
    4. How well can you predict a new speaker? (Part G, the held-out speakers.)

    The notebook ends with these decisions:

    • Use a model in CIELAB colour space, not on the printed grid. A grid model must know that hue is a circle.
    • Use the hierarchical model (speaker effects) to describe the speakers. The pooled model gets each chip right, but it fails a check for each speaker.
    • Keep two uncertainties apart. New speakers vary much along the boundaries. The language itself is known to about one chip.
    • A focus (the best example that speakers mark) is not the chip that most speakers agree on.

    What you will get, and the notebook section that each item replaces:

    In the web app Notebook
    The share of the speakers who give each term to each chip, on the palette, with mean, sd and draws (Fields, sci.field_map) Part D, the disc charts of each term
    Each chip in the colour of its most probable term, next to the observed naming (sci.swatches) Parts A and B, the raw naming map and the "poster" maps
    The between-speaker sds sd and the weights W (Parameters, core.posterior_forest) Part C, the sds of the speaker effects
    The scores of the 9 held-out speakers (log_score, accuracy, accuracy_lift, brier) Part G, the log score of new speakers
    The effective number of categories of one speaker and of the language (Score) Part E, the effective categories
    Simulated new speakers (Score, mode Simulate) Part D, the animation of new speakers

    See Differences from the notebook for the parts that the port leaves out.

    2. Get the data¶

    The export command reads wcs_naming.npz, wcs_terms.csv, wcs_chips.csv and wcs_languages.csv. It keeps the 29 speakers of Nafaanra (WCS language 77). A term is major if at least a third of the speakers used it. Each major term keeps its WCS abbreviation, and all other terms become other. Run the command from the root of the modeling-api repository:

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e77 \
    --challenges <path to pymc-challenges>/data \
    --out wcs_nafaanra.nc --spec wcs_nafaanra.yaml

    The command writes:

    wrote wcs_nafaanra.nc (variables response, cie_l, cie_a, cie_b, wcs_row, wcs_col on speaker=29, chip=330)
upload it as dataset linguistics/wcs_nafaanra and fit sci.colour_naming
wrote the suggested spec to wcs_nafaanra.yaml

    The file holds one dataset:

    Name Dims Type Meaning
    speaker 29 categorical, policy new_levels, strategy hyperprior The speakers, s01 to s29
    chip 330 integer, policy fixed The WCS chip number
    grid chip text coordinate The name of the chip on the palette, for example E29
    response speaker, chip str, target, missing values allowed The term that the speaker gave to the chip. An empty value is no answer
    cie_l, cie_a, cie_b chip float64, covariate The CIELAB colour of the chip
    wcs_row, wcs_col chip int64, covariate The place of the chip on the palette. Rows A to J are 0 to 9. Column 0 holds the greys, and columns 1 to 40 are the hues

    The dataset holds 9,570 answers: W 5,082, N 2,757, F 1,550 and B 173. Only 8 cells have no answer. All terms of Nafaanra are major, so the dataset has no other term. 16 of the 29 speakers never use B. The other 13 use it for 1 to 24 chips. The attribute mapi_schema gives each variable its role, so you do not map columns. The attribute glosses gives the word for each abbreviation.

    No pymc-challenges copy?

    --synthetic writes naming that the model's own process simulates: 12 speakers, a small palette of 38 chips and three terms (light, dark and warm), with known parameters. Use it to try the steps. The numbers in this tutorial are for Nafaanra. With --synthetic, the --spec file holds out 2 random speakers, because the simulated data does not have the 9 speakers of the notebook.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e77 --synthetic --out colour_naming.nc

    3. Upload the dataset¶

    1. Open Datasets in the sidebar.
    2. Click New dataset.
    3. Type wcs_nafaanra in Name. Type a description, for example "Colour naming of the 29 Nafaanra speakers of the World Color Survey".
    4. Click Create dataset. The upload panel opens.
    5. Click Choose files and select wcs_nafaanra.nc.
    6. Make sure that the format is netCDF (or HDF5 written by xarray).
    7. Optional: click Preview structure. The panel shows Structure of the file. Make sure that it shows response on speaker and chip, and five variables on chip.
    8. Optional: type a message in Version message (optional).
    9. Click Upload and ingest.

    The ingest run reads the file in the sandbox. When it is done, the version page shows the tabs Schema, Coordinates, Missingness, Preview and About. On Schema, response has the role target and the type str. Missingness shows the 8 cells without an answer. The model accepts them: a missing answer leaves the likelihood.

    The file is one plain dataset. You do not need the tree fields ("Put this file in a node of a tree (optional)") or the HDF5 path.

    3.1 Explore the data¶

    Look at the data before you build the spec.

    1. On the version page, click Explore this version. The Data Explorer opens. The sections under Pictures of the data come first.

    2. Click Points.

    3. The chart shows the 330 chips on the grid of the survey (wcs_col and wcs_row). Each chip has its colour from cie_l, cie_a and cie_b.

    4. Set the colour to the most frequent term to see the four terms of Nafaanra on the chart.
    5. Set the colour to the agreement. Low agreement shows the boundaries between the terms, where the 29 speakers do not give the same answer.
    6. wcs_row and wcs_col are positions. Correlations does not use them.

    Refer to Scientific data.

    4. Build the spec¶

    1. Open Build and click New spec. The editor opens with the geo MMM template.
    2. Set the Editor view to YAML.
    3. Open the Insert panel on the right.
    4. Open the Datasets section and select wcs_nafaanra. The toast "Dataset set" shows.
    5. Type colour in the search field. Under Models, select How people name colours (sci.colour_naming@^1). The toast "Model set" shows.
    6. Select all the text in the editor and paste the full spec below. The template has other blocks (a pipeline, a split and the MMM plots), so the spec must replace all of it.
    7. Click Validate. The status shows valid.
    8. Click Save spec. The spec gets its name nafaanra-colour-naming from metadata.name.

    This is the full spec:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: nafaanra-colour-naming, project: linguistics}
data:
  dataset: dataset://linguistics/wcs_nafaanra@latest
  bind:
    vars: {naming: response, lab_l: cie_l, lab_a: cie_a, lab_b: cie_b,
           grid_row: wcs_row, grid_col: wcs_col}
  split: {kind: leave_group_out, dim: speaker,
          groups: [s03, s04, s07, s13, s18, s19, s22, s25, s27]}
model:
  use: sci.colour_naming@^1
  settings: {kind: hierarchical, features: cielab, new_speakers: 20}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, seed: 77, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4,
        engine: nutpie}
evaluate: {metrics: [log_score, accuracy, accuracy_lift, brier, elpd_loo]}
outputs:
  plots: [core.convergence, core.posterior_forest, sci.swatches, sci.field_map]

    The model calls the target naming and the chip variables lab_l, lab_a, lab_b, grid_row and grid_col. The bind block connects them to the names in the dataset. The dims speaker and chip have the same names in the model and in the dataset.

    The split holds out the same 9 speakers as the notebook. evaluate_then_refit fits on the other 20 speakers and scores the 9 held-out speakers as new speakers. Then it fits again on all 29 speakers. The suggested spec of the export (wcs_nafaanra.yaml) is the same spec without new_speakers: 20, which is the default.

    The settings for this question:

    Setting Value What it means here
    kind hierarchical Each speaker gets an intercept and linear weights (on L, a and b) of their own, around those of the language. A speaker can use a term more or less, and move its boundaries. pooled gives all speakers the same naming
    features cielab The logit of each term is a quadratic surface in CIELAB colour space. grid_line and grid_circle use the row and the hue column of the palette (notebook part B)
    new_speakers 20 In each draw, the fit simulates 20 new speakers to calculate the share of the speakers who give each term to each chip. The notebook also uses 20
    w_sd 3 (default) The prior sd of the zero-sum weights. The notebook uses the same scale after its prior predictive check
    sd_scale 1 (default) The half-normal scale of the sds between the speakers
    n_random 4 (default) The speaker effects are on the first 4 features: the intercept, L, a and b
    hue_columns the largest grid_col (default) Only for grid_circle: the number of hue columns around the circle. The WCS has 40
    score_draws 200 (default) The number of posterior draws that one score uses

    Settings that do not agree

    Validate does a check of each setting by itself. It does not compare two settings. For example, features: grid_line with n_random: 9 shows valid, but the run stops with SETTINGS_INVALID because grid_line has only 7 features.

    5. Fit and watch the run¶

    1. Click Fit. The app opens the run page.
    2. The badge shows Live while the run sends events.
    3. The Progress card shows the progress of the sampler. The Events card shows the stages of the run and, at the end, the diagnostics. The Logs card stays empty when the model writes no output.
    4. When the run is done, the Result card shows Inspect model://linguistics/nafaanra-colour-naming@v1. Click it.

    The run does two fits: one on the 20 training speakers for the metrics of the held-out speakers, and one on all 29 speakers. On a laptop with nutpie, the two fits took 232 seconds. After the fits, the run calculates the language-level shares for each draw, the plots and the explorer views.

    You can also find the run under Runs. Filter it with the kind fit.

    6. Read the result¶

    On the model version page, the tabs are Overview, Diagnostics, Plots, Data tree, Preprocessing and Provenance. Click Explore to open the Model Explorer.

    6.1 Can you trust the fit?¶

    Open Diagnostics in the Model Explorer, or the plot Sampler convergence on the Plots tab. In the fit for this tutorial, there were no divergences. The largest R-hat was 1.02 and the smallest bulk ESS was 278 (of 4,000 draws). Both values are for the weights W. The sds sd mix well (R-hat 1.01 or less, bulk ESS 730 or more). The scorecard shows warnings for R-hat, for the bulk ESS and for 17 chips with a high Pareto k in elpd_loo. If you need a stricter result, increase tune and draws to 2000.

    6.2 Do the speakers agree?¶

    Posterior forest shows the weights W and the sds sd between the speakers. The sds are on the logit scale of the standardised features. In this fit, the means of sd are:

    Feature W N F B
    Intercept (1) 0.25 0.36 0.47 2.64
    L 0.56 0.09 0.22 0.45
    a 0.54 0.54 0.13 1.13
    b 0.36 0.29 0.10 0.20

    Read the table as the notebook does:

    • The intercept sds of F, N and W are small. The speakers use these three terms at similar rates.
    • The intercept sd of B is large. Some speakers use B for a patch of chips, and others never use it.
    • The larger linear sds (W on L and a, N on a) show that the speakers put the light/dark and the warm/cool boundaries in different places.

    The pooled model cannot show this. It says that each speaker names a boundary chip with the same probabilities.

    6.3 Where are the boundaries? The maps¶

    Open Fields in the Model Explorer. The model has 1 output on a grid: p_term, the share of the speakers of the language who give each term to each chip. The rows are the 10 rows of the palette (A at the top), and the columns are the 41 columns (the greys in column 0).

    1. Move the slider term to select a term: W, N, F or B.
    2. Select Mean in Statistic. A bright cell is a chip that most speakers give this term.
    3. Select Std. dev. to see where you are not sure of the share. The sd is large along the boundaries.
    4. Select Draws and click Play the draws to see possible maps that agree with the data.
    5. Move the pointer over a cell to read its values.

    The cells of row A and row J, except column 0, have no chip. They are empty. The plot Field map on the Plots tab shows the same output as panels (mean and sd) with a slider.

    Look for these features, which the notebook also finds:

    • F covers white and row B. Its boundary under row B is sharp.
    • W covers the dark greys, black and almost all greens, blues and purples.
    • N covers the reds, oranges, pinks, browns and yellows. The N/W boundary runs along a diagonal from the dark browns to the yellow-greens, and through the purples near columns 34 to 37. It is wide: a band of several chips, not a line.
    • B is small everywhere. Its most probable chip is D35, a pale purple. Only 11% of new speakers (90% range 3% to 21%) give it B.

    6.4 The posterised map¶

    The plot Colour swatches (sci.swatches, title "Colours of mode_term_rgb") paints each chip on the palette in the display colour of its most probable term. The display colour of a term is the average colour of the chips where the term is most probable.

    • The first panel shows the observed naming: each chip in the colour of the term that most speakers gave it.
    • The mean panel shows the average over the draws.
    • The draw panels show possible maps of the language. Where they differ, the majority term of the chip is not sure.

    In this fit, the draw panels differ only on a small number of chips. On 27 of the 330 chips, less than 95% of the draws give the same majority term. Look for these chips along the boundaries of section 6.3.

    6.5 The held-out speakers¶

    The metrics are in the Metrics card of the Overview tab. They score the 2,970 answers of the 9 held-out speakers. The model did not see these speakers, so it predicts them as new speakers.

    Metric Value Meaning
    log_score −0.403 The mean log probability of the answer that the speaker gave, in nats for each answer. Higher is better
    accuracy 0.882 The share of the answers that are the most probable term
    accuracy_lift 0.341 The accuracy minus the accuracy of the most frequent term (W)
    brier 0.195 The mean squared error of the probabilities. Lower is better
    elpd_loo −3,959 (se 197) Leave one chip out, on the fit of all 29 speakers. 17 chips have a high Pareto k

    Compare elpd_loo only between fits with the same speakers and the same fit.mode. The value of a fit on 20 speakers is not on the same scale as the value of a fit on 29 speakers.

    6.6 The numbers of the notebook and of this port¶

    Quantity Notebook This port
    Intercept sd between the speakers: W, N, F 0.25 to 0.47 0.25, 0.36, 0.47
    Intercept sd between the speakers: B About 2.6 2.64
    Held-out log_score, pooled, CIELAB −0.392 −0.392
    Held-out log_score, grid with hue as a line, minus CIELAB −0.034 −0.034
    Held-out log_score, grid with hue as a circle, minus CIELAB −0.004 −0.004
    Held-out log_score, pooled and hierarchical −0.389 and −0.398 (part G, 150 chips for each speaker) −0.392 and −0.403 (all 330 chips)
    Effective categories of one speaker About 2 1.92 (90% range 1.86 to 1.98)
    Effective categories of the language Lower than for one speaker 1.84 (90% range 1.77 to 1.90)
    Chips where a new speaker gives the top term with probability below 0.7 71 71
    Chips with a majority term that is not sure (less than 0.95) 26 27
    Safest chip of B D35, 11% (4% to 21%) D35, 11% (3% to 21%)
    Safest chips of W and N G23 (0.99) and E4 G23 (0.99) and E4 (0.98)

    The last four rows come from the draws of p_term. Fields shows the same values for each chip. The held-out scores of the pooled and grid fits come from three fits of the spec with kind: pooled, one for each value of features.

    Your numbers can be slightly different if the sampler or its version is different.

    6.7 Make the decisions¶

    Use these outputs to make the decisions of the notebook:

    1. CIELAB or the grid. Fit the spec again with kind: pooled and each value of features. Compare log_score of the held-out speakers. In the fits for this tutorial, cielab scores −0.392 nats for each answer, the grid with hue as a line −0.426, and the grid with hue as a circle −0.396. Hue as a line costs about 100 nats over the 2,970 held-out answers. Keep cielab. It knows that hue is a circle, and its distances are about perceptual.
    2. Pooled or hierarchical. Look at the sds of section 6.2. A large intercept sd (B) or large linear sds (W, N) show that the speakers do not name the same way. Use the hierarchical model to describe the speakers. For the share of all speakers on one chip, the pooled model is as good and slightly better on log_score.
    3. Two uncertainties. For the variation between the speakers, use the predictions for new speakers (step 7, mode Simulate). For the uncertainty about the language, use the Std. dev. of p_term in Fields, and the draw panels of Colour swatches. In this fit, a new speaker gives the most probable term with a probability below 0.7 on 71 chips. But the majority term of the language is not sure on only 27 chips.
    4. How many categories. Use k_eff_speaker and k_eff_language (step 7). A language with k equally used, sharp categories scores k. Nafaanra has 4 major terms, but it acts like 2 sharp categories.
    5. Best example or safest chip. The model gives the chip that most speakers agree on. It does not give the focus. The dataset has no foci, so make this comparison in the notebook.

    7. Score the model¶

    The fitted version uses all 29 speakers. A score needs a dataset that has the dims speaker and chip. Send the dataset that you fitted.

    7.1 Predict: the speakers and the effective categories¶

    1. On the model version page, click Score.
    2. In What do you want to do?, select Predict.
    3. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select wcs_nafaanra.
    4. Open Advanced. Set Seed (optional) to 77. Type p_name, k_eff_speaker, k_eff_language in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Predict.

    The request that the page sends (POST /v1/models/{model_id}/versions/{v}:score) is:

    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://linguistics/nafaanra-colour-naming@latest",
  "mode": "predict",
  "data": "dataset://linguistics/wcs_nafaanra@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "expected",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "vars": ["p_name", "k_eff_speaker", "k_eff_language"]
  },
  "seed": 77
}

    The result has p_name(speaker, chip, term): the probability that each of the 29 speakers gives each term to each chip. Each fitted speaker keeps their own effects. The outputs k_eff_speaker and k_eff_language are the same for each speaker, because they describe the language. In this fit, k_eff_speaker is 1.92 (about 1.87 to 1.98) and k_eff_language is 1.83 (about 1.77 to 1.90). The value for the language is lower because the speakers do not agree on all chips.

    No new data

    The page does not show No new data for this model. The model cannot forecast, so a request without a dataset has no forecast horizon, and the server refuses it. A note under Values to score gives this reason.

    7.2 Simulate: new speakers¶

    simulate gives each speaker of the dataset new speaker effects. Thus the result is 29 new Nafaanra speakers, as in the animation of the notebook.

    1. In What do you want to do?, select Simulate.
    2. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select wcs_nafaanra.
    3. Open Advanced. Set Quantity to Predictive. Set Draws to 20.
    4. Type p_name in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Simulate.
    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://linguistics/nafaanra-colour-naming@latest",
  "mode": "simulate",
  "data": "dataset://linguistics/wcs_nafaanra@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "predictive",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "draws": 20,
    "vars": ["p_name"]
  },
  "seed": 77
}

    With Predictive, each draw holds one sampled term for each speaker and chip (a one-hot vector over the terms). Thus each draw is one complete naming of the palette by a new speaker. In this score, 37% of the simulated speakers never use B, and one simulated speaker uses B for 142 chips. In the data, 16 of the 29 speakers (55%) never use B, and no speaker uses it for more than 24 chips. The numbers of chips for each term also vary more between the simulated speakers (sd 12.5 for W) than between the real speakers (sd 8.9). The notebook finds the same weakness: a shift of one shared shape does not describe a term that only some speakers have.

    • To score a speaker that the fit did not see, upload a dataset with a new speaker label (for example new01) and the same chips. Predict then gives that speaker new effects in each draw. The mean over the draws is the prediction for a new speaker.
    • Evaluate needs a dataset with answers. It scores them with log_score, accuracy, accuracy_lift and brier.
    • A dataset with a chip that the fit did not see stops the score with UNSEEN_LEVELS.
    • The model has no forecast mode.

    If you promote the version to an endpoint, Integrations → the endpoint → Schema and Try it sends the same requests.

    8. Run a workflow recipe¶

    The recipe bayes_workflow_audit (Bayesian workflow audit) does the checks of a Bayesian workflow on any model with draws. It fits the model with prior predictive draws and plots them. A convergence gate needs the largest R-hat below Rhat max (1.01) and the smallest bulk ESS at or above Min ess (400). Only then does it make the posterior predictive check and run the governance checks sensitivity.prior and recovery.simulated. Then it writes a report with the plots.

    1. Set the header to Guided and open Recipes. In Expert mode, open Workflows and then the Templates tab.
    2. Find the card Bayesian workflow audit and click Run on my data.
    3. In 1. Your data, select wcs_nafaanra in Dataset.
    4. In 2. What the recipe needs to know, type sci.colour_naming@^1 in Model.
    5. Open Fine-tune. In Settings, type {"kind": "hierarchical", "features": "cielab", "new_speakers": 20}. In Bind, type {"vars": {"naming": "response", "lab_l": "cie_l", "lab_a": "cie_a", "lab_b": "cie_b", "grid_row": "wcs_row", "grid_col": "wcs_col"}}.
    6. Click Run this recipe.

    The monitor of the run has the tabs Graph, Steps, Decisions, Outputs and Events. Decisions shows the convergence gate and the value that it read. Outputs shows the plots and the report. The model version link opens the Model Explorer.

    Give the recipe the binding

    Without Bind, the recipe binds only the target response to naming. It cannot bind the five chip variables, because they all have the role covariate, and the fit stops.

    The default gate can stop this audit

    In the fit of this tutorial, the largest R-hat was 1.019 and the smallest bulk ESS was 278. Thus the default gate (R-hat below 1.01, bulk ESS 400 or more) takes the else arm, and the recipe skips the checks after the gate. Increase Tune and Draws to 2000 in Fine-tune, then run the recipe again.

    Note

    The checks after the gate refit the model. sensitivity.prior fits it one time for each value in Prior scales (two by default), and recovery.simulated fits it N sims times (20 by default). Thus the recipe takes many times as long as one fit.

    The recipes sci_spatial_ladder, sci_network_ladder, sci_phylo_signal, sci_calibrate_then_design and sci_image_model_check are for other science models. They do not apply to this model.

    10. Differences from the notebook¶

    The port keeps the naming model of parts B and C (pooled and hierarchical, CIELAB and the two grid feature sets) and the held-out speakers of part G. It leaves out these parts:

    • The other languages (parts E and F). The model fits one language. To compare languages, export each language with from_challenges(path, language=<WCS number>) in Python, and fit one spec for each. The web app does not calculate the similarity of two partitions (NMI) or the hue-rotation null.
    • The clusters of centroids and foci across 110 languages (part F). The notebook uses a Bayesian bootstrap over the speakers, not this model.
    • The foci. The dataset has no best examples. Thus the port cannot compare the safest chip with the focus of each term.
    • The per-speaker predictive check and the lineup (part C and part D). The web app has no plot for them. Calculate them from the draws of a simulate score (step 7.2): for each term, count the chips of each simulated speaker, and compare the sd over the speakers with the data.
    • The prior predictive check of part B. The default w_sd: 3 is the scale that the notebook chose after that check.
    • The glyph maps, the boundary widths and the disc charts of part D. Fields and Colour swatches show the same quantities in a different form.
    • The adaptive choice of the next chip (part G). The port does not update one speaker from some answers, and it does not calculate the expected information gain.
