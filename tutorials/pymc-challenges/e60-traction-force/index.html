    E60 traction force microscopy¶

    This tutorial does the real-data part of the pymc-challenges notebook E60_traction_force_microscopy.py in the web app. You use the model sci.traction_force@1. You do not write PyMC code.

    You need:

    • The platform (start it with make dev) and a project that you can upload datasets to. This tutorial uses the project biophysics. If your project has a different name, change the project part of each dataset:// and model:// reference.
    • A local copy of pymc-challenges. The export command reads one file from its data/ directory.
    • Some time for the fit. The model estimates three minutes of sandbox time. On a busy laptop, our fit took 45 minutes.

    1. The question¶

    A cell on a soft gel pulls on the gel surface. Beads in the gel move. Traction force microscopy (TFM) calculates the forces of the cell from the bead movement. This is an inverse problem: many force maps agree with the measured movement. Thus each TFM method adds an assumption. In a Bayesian model, that assumption is the prior.

    The notebook asks two questions about a colony of 7 cells on a 49 kPa gel (pyTFM example data, Bauer et al. 2021):

    1. Which prior on the traction field do the data support? (Notebook section 7.)
    2. How hard does the colony pull, with an honest range? The notebook reports the strain energy (fJ) and the contractility (µN), and compares them with pyTFM. (Notebook section 8.)

    The decision at the end of the notebook is this: use the Matérn prior, and report the strain energy and the contractility with a range that includes the uncertainty of the gel stiffness. The range from the displacement noise alone is less than 1%. A 15% error in Young's modulus moves the forces by about −21% to +25%.

    What you will get, and the notebook section that each item replaces:

    In the web app Notebook
    The posterior of the noise sd and of the three Matérn hyperparameters (Parameters) Section 7, the ML-II fits and the full-Bayes fit of section 8
    The NOISE_SPECTRUM_MISMATCH diagnostic (Diagnostics) Section 7, the right panel of the spectrum plot
    Traction maps with mean, sd and draws (Fields, sci.vector_field) Section 8, the map of the posterior mean and the map of mean ÷ sd
    strain_energy and contractility with a 90% range (Score) Section 8, the table of numbers
    A second fit with young_cv: 0.15 Section 8, the row "Matérn posterior, E ± 15%"

    Notebook sections 1 to 6 use a simulated cell with known forces. The port does not repeat them. See Differences from the notebook.

    2. Get the data¶

    The export command reads pytfm_colony_ko04.npz and writes a NetCDF file that the platform can read. Run it from the root of the modeling-api repository:

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e60 \
    --challenges <path to pymc-challenges>/data \
    --out pytfm_colony.nc --spec pytfm_colony.yaml

    The command writes:

    wrote pytfm_colony.nc (variables displacement, force_mask on component=2, row=189, col=193)
upload it as dataset biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04 and fit sci.traction_force
wrote the suggested spec to pytfm_colony.yaml

    The file holds one dataset:

    Name Dims Type Meaning
    component 2 categorical, x and y The direction of the displacement
    row, col 189, 193 integer The PIV grid. y goes along row, x along col
    displacement component, row, col float64, µm, target The PIV displacement of the gel surface
    force_mask row, col bool, mask The region that pyTFM sums the forces over (7,218 pixels)

    The attributes of the file keep the gel constants: grid_um 2.117, young_pa 49000, poisson 0.49, thickness_um 300 and piv_window_um 20. The attribute mapi_schema gives each variable its role, so you do not map columns.

    No pymc-challenges copy?

    --synthetic writes simulated data from the model's own prior: a 32 × 32 grid with a known traction field. Use it to try the steps. The numbers in this tutorial are for the real colony.

    uv run python -m mapi_plugin_science.export e60 --synthetic --out tfm_synthetic.nc

    3. Upload the dataset¶

    1. Open Datasets in the sidebar.
    2. Click New dataset.
    3. Type pytfm_colony_ko04 in Name. Type a description, for example "PIV displacement under a colony of 7 cells, 49 kPa gel".
    4. Click Create dataset. The upload panel opens.
    5. Click Choose files and select pytfm_colony.nc.
    6. Click Preview structure. The panel shows Structure of the file. Make sure that it shows displacement on component, row and col, and force_mask on row and col.
    7. Optional: type a message in Version message (optional).
    8. Click Upload and ingest.

    The ingest run reads the file in the sandbox. When it is done, the version page shows the tabs Schema, Coordinates, Missingness, Preview and About. On Schema, displacement has the role target and force_mask the role mask. Missingness must show no missing values: the model needs a value at each pixel.

    The file is one plain dataset. You do not need the tree fields ("Put this file in a node of a tree (optional)") or the HDF5 path.

    3.1 Explore the data¶

    Look at the data before you build the spec.

    1. On the version page, click Explore this version. The Data Explorer opens. The sections under Pictures of the data come first.

    2. Click Images and fields.

    3. The viewer shows displacement as a vector field: the colour shows the magnitude, and the arrows show the direction. The grid has 189 × 193 cells. The viewer shows the mean of blocks of cells (95 × 97).

    4. The mask force_mask (the colony) shows as a layer.
    5. The histogram of each component (x and y) is below the viewer.
    6. Most other sections are in Not for this data. The data is one field, without time and without groups.

    Refer to Scientific data.

    4. Build the spec¶

    1. Open Build and click New spec. The editor opens with the geo MMM template.
    2. Set the Editor view to YAML.
    3. Open the Insert panel on the right.
    4. Open the Datasets section and select pytfm_colony_ko04. The toast "Dataset set" shows.
    5. Type traction in the search field. Under Models, select Cell forces from gel movement (sci.traction_force@^1). The toast "Model set" shows.
    6. Select all the text in the editor and paste the full spec below. The template has other blocks (a pipeline, a split and the MMM plots), so the spec must replace all of it.
    7. Click Validate. The status shows valid.
    8. Click Save spec. The spec gets its name from metadata.name.

    This is the full spec:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata:
  name: pytfm-colony-traction
  project: biophysics
data:
  dataset: dataset://biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04@latest
model:
  use: sci.traction_force@^1
  settings:
    grid_um: 2.117341064232256
    young_pa: 49000.0
    poisson: 0.49
    young_cv: 0.0
    prior: matern
    noise_um: 0.003
    traction_pa: 1000.0
    lengthscale_um: 20.0
    field_draws: 200
    force_mask: true
  fit:
    mode: train_only
    seed: 60
    tune: 1000
    draws: 1000
    chains: 4
outputs:
  plots:
    - core.convergence
    - core.posterior_forest
    - sci.vector_field

    The settings come from challenges_settings() (the --spec file of the export has the same values). This table tells what each one means for the colony:

    Setting Value Meaning for this question
    grid_um 2.117 The PIV step in µm (20 µm windows that move by 2.1 µm). It sets the wavenumbers
    young_pa 49000 Young's modulus of the gel. The tractions scale exactly with it
    poisson 0.49 The Poisson ratio of the gel (almost incompressible)
    young_cv 0 The relative error of young_pa. 0 gives the notebook row "E known". Step 6.4 uses 0.15
    prior matern The prior spectrum of the tractions. Section 7 of the notebook selects it. white is Tikhonov and smooth is a squared exponential
    noise_um 0.003 The prior median of the noise sd: 3 nm. The notebook found 3.4 nm
    traction_pa 1000 The prior median of the traction scale in Pa
    lengthscale_um 20 The prior median of the Matérn length scale, near the PIV window. The notebook found 23 µm
    field_draws 200 The number of traction fields that each score draws. The notebook also uses 200
    force_mask true Sum the strain energy and the contractility inside force_mask only, as pyTFM does
    • fit.mode: train_only fits on all of the data. The model needs the full raster, so it has no split. Without this line, the validator shows the note SPLIT_INVALID ("there is no holdout for the evaluation").
    • The spec has no evaluate block. Without a split, the fit skips the metrics rmse and mae ("no data.split is declared").
    • The model has no pointwise log likelihood, so elpd_loo is not available.

    The plots

    The model declares core.convergence, core.posterior_forest and sci.vector_field. The fit makes only the plots in outputs.plots, so the spec names them. The Plots section of the Insert panel adds a plot for you.

    Do not add sci.field_map. For the traction field, that recipe needs the settings y and x, and the fit skips it with the warning PLOT_SKIPPED. The Fields section of the Model Explorer shows the same maps.

    5. Fit and watch the run¶

    1. Click Fit. The app opens the run page.
    2. The badge shows Live while the run sends events.
    3. The Progress card shows the NUTS progress. The Events card shows the stages of the run and, at the end, the diagnostics. The Logs card stays empty when the model writes no output.
    4. When the run is done, the Result card shows Inspect model://biophysics/pytfm-colony-traction@v1. Click it.

    The fit samples only 4 numbers (noise_sd, traction_sd, lengthscale and smoothness). The model integrates the traction field out, with one closed-form likelihood for each of the 72,952 Fourier modes. Each gradient uses all modes, so the fit takes some minutes. After the fit, the run calculates the plots and the explorer views. For these it draws 200 traction fields.

    You can also find the run under Runs. Filter it with the kind fit.

    6. Read the result¶

    On the model version page, the tabs are Overview, Diagnostics, Plots, Data tree, Preprocessing and Provenance. Click Explore to open the Model Explorer.

    6.1 Can you trust the fit?¶

    Open Diagnostics in the Model Explorer (group "Can you trust it?").

    • R-hat and ESS are for the 4 hyperparameters. Our fit had a largest R-hat of 1.003, a smallest bulk ESS of 1,205 and no divergent transitions.
    • Expect the warning NOISE_SPECTRUM_MISMATCH. The plain message is "The noise in the displacements is not the same at all scales. The ranges of the tractions are possibly too narrow."

    The warning is correct, and the notebook finds the same problem. Neighbour PIV vectors come from windows that overlap by 90%. Thus their errors are correlated, and the finest Fourier modes have much less power than white noise gives. The diagnostic compares the two in the finest quarter of the modes. A value below 0.5 starts the warning. Our fit gave 0.33. The consequence: the ranges with E known are too narrow. Use them only to compare priors, not as the error bar of the colony.

    Fit vs actual compares displacement_hat with the PIV field. The fit is almost exact. A small residual is not a sign of a good model here, because many force maps give the same displacement.

    6.2 Which prior do the data support?¶

    Open Parameters (group "What it learned").

    Parameter Mean 89% interval Notebook (ML-II)
    noise_sd (µm) 0.00335 0.00331 to 0.00339 3.4 nm
    traction_sd (Pa) 1,799 1,495 to 2,191 not given
    lengthscale (µm) 23.8 18.9 to 30.0 23
    smoothness (α) 0.937 0.920 to 0.955 0.94

    The intervals are the 5.5% and 94.5% quantiles of the 4,000 draws, as in the notebook. The Model Explorer shows the interval that you select in Explore with options.

    The notebook finds the Matérn spectrum with ML-II: ℓ = 23 µm, α = 0.94 and 3.4 nm of noise. Its log marginal likelihood is 2,157 above the squared exponential and 14,104 above the white prior. The platform does not show the marginal likelihood (see section 10). To compare the priors, fit the spec again with prior: white and prior: smooth, and compare:

    • noise_sd. With the white prior, the notebook finds that the noise goes to zero, that is no regularisation. The noise_um prior of the spec keeps the noise sd above zero. A noise sd much smaller than 3 nm tells you that the prior explains the noise as force.
    • The NOISE_SPECTRUM_MISMATCH value on Diagnostics.
    • The strain energy (step 6.4). It moves with the prior by more than its range.

    6.3 The traction maps¶

    Open Fields (group "What it learned"). The text says "The model has 2 outputs on a grid." Select traction in Output.

    • Statistic Mean shows the posterior mean of the traction length. Select Show the arrows to see the direction. The colony pulls at its edge, with the largest tractions (about 1 kPa) at its left, top and lower right edges. The cells at the edges of the image show too.
    • Std. dev. is almost flat. A stationary prior gives the same uncertainty at each pixel. This map does not show where the model is wrong.
    • Mean ÷ sd is the notebook's right panel. Inside the colony, the tractions are many sd from zero.
    • Draws and Play the draws show 200 possible force maps that agree with the data.
    • Artefacts along the image edges come from the periodic FFT. They are outside the force mask.

    Move the pointer over the map to read the values of one pixel.

    On the model version page, open Plots:

    Plot What to look for
    core.convergence R-hat near 1 and a high ESS for the 4 hyperparameters
    core.posterior_forest Narrow intervals. With 72,952 modes, the data fix the hyperparameters well
    sci.vector_field The heatmap of the traction length with arrows of the mean vector, and its sd. Compare it with the pyTFM map in notebook section 8

    6.4 How hard does the colony pull?¶

    The strain energy and the contractility are outputs of a score. Do step 7 to get them. These are the results:

    Output Our fit: mean, 89% interval Notebook: Matérn posterior, E known, 89% pyTFM
    strain_energy (fJ) 271.3, 270.9 to 271.6 271.0 to 271.6 246 (252 with the notebook formula)
    contractility (µN) 2.173, 2.168 to 2.179 2.167 to 2.180 2.13

    The contractility agrees with pyTFM to within 2%. The strain energy is about 8% higher than pyTFM. pyTFM smooths its tractions with a Gaussian filter, and a smoother field has less energy. The web app shows hdi_90, so its interval is a little wider than the 89% interval here.

    Then do the stiffness part of the decision:

    1. Open the spec in Build.
    2. Change young_cv: 0.0 to young_cv: 0.15.
    3. Click Save revision, then Fit.
    4. Score the new version as in step 7.

    young_cv gives each posterior draw a lognormal draw of Young's modulus. The displacements carry no information about it, so its posterior is its prior. Each draw scales its traction field, its strain energy and its contractility.

    Output Our fit: mean, 89% interval Notebook: E ± 15%, 89%
    strain_energy (fJ) 271.0, 211.4 to 340.8 213 to 338
    contractility (µN) 2.171, 1.689 to 2.733 1.71 to 2.70
    young_pa (Pa, in Parameters) 49,379, 38,331 to 62,518 (the prior)

    The hyperparameters do not change: the same seed gives the same draws, and only the scale of each field changes.

    The decision. The data support the Matérn prior. With the stiffness known, the ranges are less than 1% wide. With a 15% stiffness error, they are about 100 to 200 times wider. Thus report the second set of ranges for the colony. In a real study, the gel stiffness causes most of the uncertainty, then the selection of the prior, and last the displacement noise.

    7. Score the model¶

    Each score draws new traction fields from the posterior. A score needs a dataset, but the model does not use its values: the field is the posterior for the fitted data. The dataset only selects the pixels. Send the dataset that you fitted.

    1. On the model version page, click Score.
    2. In What do you want to do?, select Predict.
    3. In Values to score, select Use a dataset. Select pytfm_colony_ko04.
    4. Open Advanced. Set Seed (optional) to 60. Type strain_energy, contractility in Variables (optional).
    5. Click Predict.

    The Results card shows the mean and the 90% interval (hdi_90) of each output.

    No new data

    The page does not show No new data for this model. The model cannot forecast, so a request without a dataset has no forecast horizon, and the server refuses it. A note under Values to score gives this reason.

    This is the request that the page sends (POST /v1/models/{model_id}/versions/{v}:score):

    {
  "apiVersion": "mapi/v1",
  "kind": "ScoreRequest",
  "model": "model://biophysics/pytfm-colony-traction@latest",
  "mode": "predict",
  "data": "dataset://biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04@latest",
  "output": {
    "kind": "expected",
    "summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"],
    "vars": ["strain_energy", "contractility"]
  },
  "seed": 60
}
    • To get the map of one area, add "select": {"row": [90, 91, 92], "col": [100, 101]} and "vars": ["traction"].
    • "kind": "predictive" adds white noise with sd noise_sd to displacement_hat. It does not change traction.
    • The model has no simulate mode. To make new data from known forces, use traction_data.simulate_traction() in Python.

    If you promote the version to an endpoint, Integrations → the endpoint → Schema and Try it sends the same request.

    8. Run a workflow recipe¶

    The recipe bayes_workflow_audit (Bayesian workflow audit) does the checks of a Bayesian workflow on any model with draws. It fits the model with prior predictive draws. A convergence gate needs the largest R-hat below rhat_max (1.01) and the smallest bulk ESS at or above min_ess (400). Only then does it make the posterior predictive check and run the governance checks sensitivity.prior and recovery.simulated. Then it writes a report with the plots.

    1. Set the header to Guided and open Recipes. In Expert mode, open Workflows and then the Templates tab.
    2. Find the card Bayesian workflow audit and click Run on my data.
    3. In 1. Your data, select pytfm_colony_ko04 in Dataset.
    4. In 2. What the recipe needs to know, type sci.traction_force@^1 in Model.
    5. Open Fine-tune. In Settings, type the settings of step 4 as JSON: {"grid_um": 2.117341064232256, "young_pa": 49000.0, "poisson": 0.49, "young_cv": 0.0, "prior": "matern", "noise_um": 0.003, "traction_pa": 1000.0, "lengthscale_um": 20.0, "field_draws": 200, "force_mask": true}. If you do not, the model uses young_pa: 10000, and the forces are 4.9 times too small. Set N sims to 5 (see the note below).
    6. Click Run this recipe.

    The monitor of the run has the tabs Graph, Steps, Decisions, Outputs and Events. Decisions shows the convergence gate and the value that it read. Outputs shows the plots and the report. The model version link opens the Model Explorer.

    The audit takes a long time on the full colony

    The checks after the gate refit the model. sensitivity.prior fits it one time for each value in Prior scales (two by default), and recovery.simulated fits it N sims times (20 by default). One fit of the full colony takes from 3 minutes to more than 40 minutes, as a function of the load of the machine. Thus decrease N sims before you start the audit.

    The image recipe sci_image_model_check is for models with images of a scalar field. It does not apply to this model.

    9. The same with the CLI and the Python client¶

    Save the spec of step 4 as pytfm-colony-traction.yaml.

    mapi use <tenant>/biophysics
mapi datasets upload pytfm_colony.nc --name pytfm_colony_ko04
mapi check pytfm-colony-traction.yaml --remote @     # the server's static validation
mapi fit pytfm-colony-traction.yaml --remote @       # create or revise the spec, fit, follow
mapi explore model pytfm-colony-traction -s params -s diagnostics

cat > traction-score.yaml <<'EOF'
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: model://biophysics/pytfm-colony-traction@latest
mode: predict
data: dataset://biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04@latest
output: {kind: expected, summary: [mean, hdi_90], vars: [strain_energy, contractility]}
seed: 60
EOF
mapi score traction-score.yaml --remote @ --model <model id> --version 1 -o traction.zarr.zip

mapi workflows init bayes_workflow_audit --name traction-audit
mapi workflows apply traction-audit.yaml -m "E60 audit"
mapi workflows run traction-audit -i data=dataset://biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04@latest \
  -p model=sci.traction_force@^1 -p n_sims=5 \
  -p 'settings={"grid_um": 2.117341064232256, "young_pa": 49000.0, "poisson": 0.49, "young_cv": 0.0, "prior": "matern", "noise_um": 0.003, "traction_pa": 1000.0, "lengthscale_um": 20.0, "field_draws": 200, "force_mask": true}' \
  --watch

    The CLI has no models command. Take the model id from the run (mapi runs get <run>) or from Models in the web app.

    The file pytfm-colony-traction.yaml holds the spec of step 4.

    from mapi_client import Client

api = Client(project="<tenant>/biophysics")
version = api.datasets.upload("pytfm_colony.nc", dataset="pytfm_colony_ko04")
print(version.ref, version.status.value)

with open("pytfm-colony-traction.yaml", encoding="utf-8") as fh:
    spec_yaml = fh.read()
revision = api.specs.upsert(spec_yaml)
run = api.runs.fit(revision.spec_id, revision.revision)
run = api.runs.wait(run.id, raise_on_failure=True)

model = next(m for m in api.models.list() if m.name == "pytfm-colony-traction")
result = api.models.score(
    model.id,
    model.latest_version,
    {
        "model": "model://biophysics/pytfm-colony-traction@latest",
        "mode": "predict",
        "data": "dataset://biophysics/pytfm_colony_ko04@latest",
        "output": {"summary": ["mean", "hdi_90"], "vars": ["strain_energy", "contractility"]},
        "seed": 60,
    },
)
summary = result.tree["summary"]  # an xr.DataTree (needs the `xarray` extra)
print(summary["strain_energy"].values, summary["contractility"].values)

    To compare with pyTFM's own map and numbers, load them with mapi_plugin_science.traction_data.challenges_reference("<path>/data").

    10. Differences from the notebook¶

    The port keeps the physics and the model of notebook sections 7 and 8. It leaves out these parts:

    • The simulated cell (sections 1 to 6). The --synthetic data come from the model's own Matérn prior. They are not the notebook's cell with 16 focal adhesions.
    • The L-curve and the discrepancy principle (section 3). The platform fits the hyperparameters with NUTS and does not offer a scan of λ.
    • The marginal likelihood as a number. The notebook compares the priors by log marginal likelihood. The platform does not show it, and elpd_loo is not valid for this model. Compare the priors with the noise sd, the diagnostic and the strain energy (step 6.2).
    • The radial power spectrum plot (section 7). The diagnostic NOISE_SPECTRUM_MISMATCH keeps one number from it: the power ratio in the finest modes.
    • The footprint and horseshoe priors (section 6). The model has only stationary priors. The forces are not limited to the cells, and sharp adhesions become smooth.
    • A finite gel thickness. The model uses an infinite half-space, as the notebook does. pyTFM uses a finite thickness. The difference is only in the longest wavelengths.
    • A model of the correlated PIV noise. The noise is white, so the ranges with E known are too narrow (notebook "Try it yourself", item 2).
    • pyTFM's map. The web app does not show pyTFM's tractions. Use challenges_reference() in Python.
