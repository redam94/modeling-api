    Guided for analysts · exact for data scientists · honest uncertainty · one safe platform

    Trustworthy models,
    from spreadsheet to endpoint.¶

    modeling-api fits Bayesian and classical models of data with named dimensions (market × channel × week, store × product × day, household × trip) and answers questions with them in the units your business uses. An analyst uploads a spreadsheet and gets a report, a sanity scorecard and instant what-if sliders. A data scientist writes a small spec or a few lines of Python and gets the same model behind a versioned endpoint, with the fitted preprocessing replayed exactly on every call. Whole analyses - tests, decisions, models, benchmarks - run as versioned **workflows**, and a model only goes live after it has beaten **shuffled data, fake drivers and a naive forecast**. Every run happens in a fresh sandbox, and every result says how sure it is.

    v0.5: workflows, governance, explorers, mmm-framework and statsmodels

    Workflows chain unit-root tests, differencing decisions, AR/MA order searches, rolling-origin backtests against naive forecasts, model selection and governance into one versioned, schedulable graph. Governance policies hold every model to null tests - it must beat itself fitted on shuffled results and give fake noise drivers no credit - with a second person's approval and an evidence pack. Data and Model Explorers, mmm-framework marketing-mix models with budget optimisation, twelve statsmodels time-series models, and an About card on everything with Help me choose. What's new in 0.5.

    Two ways in, one model¶

    Analysts¶

    Upload → report → scenario in minutes, with honest uncertainty.

    • Pick what you want to learn; answer "which column is revenue?" (the platform suggests the answer and says why).
    • Say what you already believe ("TV returns 1.5 to 4 per euro") or enter a lift test.
    • Read a report in plain words, with a sanity scorecard that tells you whether to trust it.
    • Drag what-if sliders: answers in your browser, with a range that stays honest when several channels move at once. "Run exact" asks the full model.
    • Share a live chart or put the model in a Google Sheet.
    • Not sure which model? Help me choose asks a few questions and explains its recommendations; every option has an About card in plain words.

    Your first analysis in Guided mode

    Data scientists¶

    Any PyMC, scikit-learn or forecasting model. Preprocessing replayed exactly. Endpoints in one click.

    • A PyMC model is one build() method, an sklearn model one estimator(); built-in marketing-mix, forecasting, demand and choice models are ready to use.
    • Steps learn on the training data only and are replayed, never refitted, at score time: forecasts, new markets, counterfactuals.
    • Uncertainty is first-class: draws, quantiles or intervals, with the aggregations that are valid for each.
    • Promote a version to an endpoint through the scorecard gate; copy a snippet for Python, TypeScript, curl, R, Apps Script or a notebook.
    • Automate the statistics: workflows of tests, branches, searches, backtests and governance checks, run live from mapi workflows run --watch or api.workflows.

    How it works¶

    flowchart LR
    D["Your data<br/>CSV · Parquet · MFF ·<br/>Meridian"] --> I["Sandboxed ingest<br/>checked, profiled,<br/>dims and roles"]
    Q["Guided answers<br/>or a YAML spec"] --> V{{"Static checks<br/>before any compute"}}
    I --> V
    V --> F["Fit in a fresh sandbox<br/>preprocessing learned<br/>on train only"]
    F --> B[("Signed bundle<br/>states · posterior ·<br/>scorecard · grids")]
    W["Workflow<br/>tests → decisions →<br/>search → backtest"] -.-> F
    B --> R["Report, explorers,<br/>what-if, budget plans"]
    B --> G{{"Governance<br/>null tests · benchmarks ·<br/>second-person approval"}}
    G --> E["Endpoint<br/>gated on the scorecard<br/>and the validation"]
    E --> S["Sheets · BI embeds ·<br/>webhooks ·<br/>Python, TS, R"]
    1. Your data stays labelled. The upload becomes an xr.Dataset with named dims, roles and units; nothing downstream is positional.
    2. Mistakes fail early. A spec that would leak the holdout, a forecast past what the model supports, a market the model has never seen: typed errors, before any compute.
    3. Fitting learns, scoring replays. Every preprocessing step stores what it learned, so a forecast or a new market goes through exactly the same transformations, then back into your units.
    4. Results are checked twice. Outputs are verified against the storage's own checksums and signed; the scorecard checks convergence, holdout error and plausibility.
    5. Models earn their place. A governance policy holds each version to null tests and benchmarks, records the evidence and, if you ask, waits for a second person; endpoints can refuse anything that is not validated.
    6. Other tools connect to an endpoint, not a file. Promote a better model and every integration uses it; roll back with one call.
    7. Whole analyses repeat themselves. A workflow re-runs the tests, decisions, fits and checks every Monday or whenever the data changes, reusing every step whose inputs did not change.

    Your first MMM in Guided mode¶

    The whole journey of a geo marketing-mix model, from a spreadsheet of weekly spend and revenue by region to a budget scenario. The times are what an automated "tester" took on a laptop, almost all of it the platform working: 135 seconds from upload to scenario.

    1. Choose "Geo marketing mix" and drop your CSV on the page. Each column is profiled in your browser; the platform suggests which column is revenue, which are spend, and why. (5 s)
    2. Say what you believe, if anything: an ROI range per channel, a lift test. Preview what this means shows it in business units before any fitting. (22 s, optional)
    3. Fit the model. Plain-language progress; the model is tested on weeks it has not seen, then refitted on everything. (80 s)
    4. Read the results: ROI and share per channel with 90% ranges, and the sanity scorecard, each check with what to do next. (instant)
    5. Ask "what if": move search +20% and see the change in revenue with its range at once; Run exact confirms it with the full model. (30 s with the exact run and a forecast)
    6. Share it: put the model live as an endpoint, or share a chart by link.

    Measured on an 8-core laptop against the whole stack, with real fits in the sandboxed runner. The v0.5 journeys, on the data and settings of M3.5 verification:

    37–40 sthe canonical workflow, 19 nodes from unit-root tests to governance, through the orchestrator and the sandboxed runner
    p = 0.7–0.95an ARIMAX fitted on pure noise against 19 shuffled refits: validation fails, promotion answers NOT_VALIDATED
    0.1 %of the result credited to three fake noise drivers by a model fitted on real signal
    26 sa validation with the null tests: 19 shuffled refits plus 3 fake drivers
    10–16 san mmm-framework budget plan with a 5-point efficient frontier, in the sandbox

    The core idea: fit with preprocessing, score with the same preprocessing¶

    The same pipeline object runs at fit time and at score time. At fit time each step learns its state from the training data; at score time it replays that state. Steps that look back in time store a small per-group tail, so a forecast sees exactly the history the model was fitted on.

    flowchart LR
    subgraph FIT["Fit (once)"]
        direction LR
        A["Daily spend & revenue<br/>local currency"] --> B["fx_convert<br/>(side table)"]
        B --> C["resample<br/>daily → weekly"]
        C --> S{{"split<br/>last 8 weeks"}}
        S --> D["holidays<br/><i>pinned side table</i>"]
        D --> M["model.fit<br/><i>keeps the fitted history<br/>for carry-over</i>"]
    end
    M --> BUN[("Bundle<br/>states · context tails · posterior<br/>hashes · no pickle")]
    subgraph SCORE["Score (any time)"]
        direction LR
        F["Next quarter's plan<br/>or a new market"] --> G["same steps,<br/><b>replayed</b> state"]
        G --> H["model.score"]
        H --> I["inverse → weekly EUR<br/>summaries · your names"]
    end
    BUN --> G
    pipeline:
  - {id: to_eur, use: core.fx_convert@^1, with: {vars: [y, spend], table: fx, ...}}
  - {id: weekly, use: core.resample@^1,   with: {dim: time, freq: W-SUN, how: sum}}
  - {id: hols,   use: core.holidays@^1,   with: {table: holidays, along: time}}
model:
  use: mmm.bayesian@^1                 # mmm-framework: scaling and carry-over inside
  settings: {hierarchy: {pool_over: [geo]}, new_levels: hyperprior}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit}
    1. Checks the whole spec statically: names, dims, settings, units at every stage, where the split happens and whether any step would learn from the holdout (LEAKAGE_RISK).
    2. Fits each stateful step on the training weeks only, fits the model, then scores the holdout through the same path a forecast uses and records the metrics.
    3. Refits on all weeks so forecasts start from the latest data, and writes a bundle with every state, context tail and hash.
    • Forecasts in weekly EUR, not "model units": the model reports its own units, and they stop at the weekly resample because daily values cannot be recovered from weekly sums. The output says so in its output_space (model-owned) and units attributes.
    • A new market drawn from the fitted hyperprior, honestly wider than a known one.
    • What-if deltas that share their noise with the baseline (common random numbers), so the delta reflects the change, not sampling noise.

    The same model in plain Python, without any server; every snippet on this site is executed by the test suite:

    from mapi_sdk import fit_spec

bundle = fit_spec(spec, data, registry=registry, raw_schema=schema)
print(bundle.metrics.values)  # holdout metrics, in units sold: {'rmse': ..., 'mape': ..., ...}
print(bundle.metrics.n_holdout)  # 48 cells: 3 stores x 2 skus x 8 weeks

    from mapi_spec import ErrorCode, MapiError


def try_score(request_yaml: str, payload: xr.Dataset) -> ErrorCode:
    try:
        score_bundle(bundle, request_yaml, payload, registry=registry)
    except MapiError as err:
        print(err.code, "-", err)
        return err.code
    raise AssertionError("expected a typed error")


FORECAST = """
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://quickstart/fruit-demand
mode: forecast
horizon: {dim: time, periods: 4}
"""

# 1. a forecast without next month's promotions: promo is future_required
code_1 = try_score(FORECAST, plan.drop_vars("promo"))

# 2. quantiles are per cell, so "total over all stores" would be a made-up number
code_2 = try_score(FORECAST + "output: {aggregate: {over: [store]}}\n", plan)

# 3. a product outside the fitted range: sku is a fixed dim
code_3 = try_score(FORECAST, plan.assign_coords(sku=["apples", "kiwis"]))

    Secure by design¶

    The platform is built to run models and data from people it does not trust. The controls that matter most are not settings you can forget to turn on: they are how it works. The security model says what each one stops, and what is still to be verified in the cloud.

    • A fresh sandbox per run

      Every ingest, fit, score and render runs in its own sandbox (a microVM on Cloud Run Jobs), with no secrets and no network but signed URLs to its own objects. Placement by trust tier is enforced in code.

    • Tenants never see each other

      PostgreSQL row-level security on every table, pinned per transaction and failing closed; roles checked at every request; operators manage the platform without reading tenant data.

    • Outputs are claims

      A run's outputs are checked against the store's own hashes and generations, copied where runs cannot write, and signed. Download links are minted only after the signature verifies.

    • Integrations that cannot be turned against you

      Webhooks are HMAC-signed and sent through an SSRF guard that pins the checked address; embed links show platform recipes only, carry no credential and can be revoked; snippets never contain a secret.

    • Compute you can see and cap

      Quotas in vCPU-seconds per token, tenant and platform, per-token budgets for endpoint callers, a cost on every run, a global spend cap and an audited kill switch.

    • No pickle, anywhere

      Bundles are single zarr zips plus JSON sidecars; native models are skops (allow-listed types) or xgboost JSON. Every file carries CRC32C, MD5 and SHA-256, and reading verifies them.

    Run it for many teams¶

    One deployment serves many organisations. People sign up or sign in with their own identity provider, work in the projects they have a role in, and see what their compute costs; operators look after the platform without ever reading a tenant's data.
