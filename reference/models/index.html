    Built-in models¶

    These model plugins ship with the platform (tier T0). They register through the mapi.models entry point, so default_registry() and the control-plane catalog list them without any configuration. Every one passes the contract kit (mapi_sdk.testing.check_model) and all but mmm.bayesinsight have a conformance fixture (see Conformance corpus); the BayesInsight model is exercised end to end by the import test of the Guided data path instead.

    Calibration (M3, the spec's calibration block, Guided task 3) is declared per plugin in Capabilities.calibration; a spec with calibration data for a plugin that does not declare it fails static validation with CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED:

    Plugin Business priors Lift tests
    mmm.hierarchical_geo yes - beta_channel and intercept priors yes - with carry-over inside the test window
    mmm.pymc_marketing yes - saturation_beta, intercept (and narrowed gamma_control/gamma_fourier) priors yes - pymc-marketing's add_lift_test_measurements
    mmm.bayesinsight no - its priors are already in KPI units: state them in model_def.json yes - the incremental KPI under the model's own likelihood

    The math of the translation and of each lift likelihood is in Guided modelling.

    Plugin Package Representation Score modes Corpus fixture
    mmm.hierarchical_geo@1 mapi-plugin-mmm draws predict, forecast, counterfactual, simulate, evaluate e33
    mmm.pymc_marketing@1 mapi-plugin-mmm draws predict, forecast, counterfactual, evaluate pymc_marketing
    mmm.bayesinsight@1 mapi-plugin-mmm draws predict, forecast, counterfactual, simulate, evaluate – (contract kit and the BayesInsight import test)
    ts.hsgp_trend@1 mapi-plugin-ts draws predict, forecast, evaluate c08
    ts.gaussian_hmm@1 mapi-plugin-ts draws predict, forecast, filter, evaluate e24
    tabular.hierarchical_glm@1 mapi-plugin-tabular draws predict, counterfactual, evaluate e02
    tabular.regressor@1 mapi-plugin-tabular quantiles predict, forecast, counterfactual, evaluate xgb_store_demand
    choice.multinomial_logit@1 mapi-plugin-tabular draws (Bayesian bootstrap) predict, forecast, counterfactual, evaluate e84_lite

    Plugins use canonical names for their inputs; bind your dataset's names onto them in the spec's data.bind block. Outputs come back under your names (reverse binding) unless they are new variables such as y_hat.

    The mapi-sklearn package also registers five general-purpose tabular plugins, sklearn.tabular_regressor, sklearn.tabular_quantiles, sklearn.tabular_bootstrap, sklearn.tabular_conformal and sklearn.tabular_classifier; see Tabular models.

    Every setting, generated

    The models catalog is generated from the installed manifests: capabilities, outputs, derived quantities, every setting with its default and Guided-mode level, and the input data contract.

    mmm.hierarchical_geo: geo marketing mix¶

    A hierarchical marketing-mix model: adstock over a core.lag_tensor spend tensor, logistic or Hill saturation, channel effects partially pooled across geos, a hierarchical intercept and controls. New geos are drawn from the fitted hyperprior. Contributions are additive and, with outputs.derived, the fit caches contributions, per-draw response curves and marginal costs in original units. It declares a decision objective for budget optimisation.

    Inputs. y(geo, time), spend(geo, channel, time, lag) from core.lag_tensor (the adstock.l_max setting must equal the step's l_max), and any other numeric variable along time as a control. geo is new_levels (hyperprior), channel is fixed, time extends.

    Outputs. y_hat(geo, time) and contributions(component, geo, time) (one component per channel, baseline and one per control; they sum to y_hat).

    Read the marketing-mix guide for the model, its priors and a complete spec.

    mmm.pymc_marketing: the pymc-marketing adapter¶

    Runs pymc_marketing.mmm.MMM behind the platform. The model scales data, carries spend over and reports original units itself, so the plugin declares owns: [scaling, carryover, inverse_units] and the platform skips its own versions of those. Bundles store the posterior, pymc-marketing's fit_data and its JSON model description, never a pickle. Markets are a fixed dim: pymc-marketing cannot score a market it has not seen. See the adapter section.

    Priors and calibration. settings.priors hands prior overrides to pymc-marketing's model_config, in its scaled units (the KPI divided by its maximum): saturation_beta (the channel effect, one parameter per channel; saturation_b for saturation: none), intercept, gamma_control and gamma_fourier. Business priors are translated into them on prior draws of pymc-marketing's own model, so its scaling, adstock and curves are exactly the fit's: a log-normal saturation_beta per channel matches each ROI or contribution-share range at its credibility, a Normal intercept the baseline share, and the control and seasonality priors are narrowed when they alone spread the baseline share wider than the range (an info issue says by how much). A translate_priors run previews them; a fit without one translates them itself (learned.calibration.translated_priors). Lift tests go through pymc-marketing's add_lift_test_measurements (the saturation curve, no carry-over).

    mmm.bayesinsight: a BayesInsight model_def.json¶

    Fits a BayesInsight model exactly as its model_def.json defines it - the dedicated target of the BayesInsight import (POST /v1/guided/import/bayesinsight with "target": "mmm.bayesinsight"; see Guided mode). The settings carry model_def (typed by mapi_spec.bayesinsight_model), map every analytic variable name to its model-stage variable and list the panel dims (the model's row ids besides Period).

    Inputs. The KPI y(*panel, time) as stored (BayesInsight does not rescale it; priors are in its units), every media variable as a core.lag_tensor with pad: nan (the history before a series starts carries no media, BayesInsight's zero-padded convolution) and the controls along (*panel, time). Panel dims are new_levels (pooled); time extends.

    Model. eta = intercept + Σ media + Σ controls + Σ seasons + Σ trends:

    Feature model_def.json Semantics
    Likelihood exog.likelihood.type Normal, LogNormal (on eta), Poisson, NegativeBinomial, ZeroInflatedPoisson, ZeroInflatedNegativeBinomial (log link, exp(clip(eta, -20, 20))); none given means Poisson, as in BayesInsight. likelihood_params parametrise the dispersion prior, dispersion_dims vary it by row id
    Coefficients coeff_prior, intercept_prior Any of Normal, Laplace, StudentT, Uniform, HalfNormal, HalfCauchy, Exponential, LogNormal, Gamma, Beta with its parameters, in KPI units; defaults are BayesInsight's (LogNormal(log 0.05, log 1.3) for media, Normal(0, 3) otherwise)
    Effects per row id fixed_ind_coeff_dims, random_coeff_dims One coefficient per level combination of the fixed row ids, plus sigma * z per level combination of the random ones with sigma ~ HalfNormal(partial_pooling_sigma) (0, BayesInsight's default, pools nothing). New levels at score time get the pooled value; a coefficient estimated per level of a dim has none for a new level (UNSEEN_LEVELS)
    Signs sign Enforced (BayesInsight declares but does not enforce them): real priors are truncated at 0, positive priors negated for negative, random effects multiplicative so no level crosses zero. Media are always positive
    Media curve media_transform, media_transform_prior First the curve on transform(x) / index (index: each series' non-zero median or mean, index_to): hill (K ~ LogNormal, n = 1 + Exponential), linear, or the S-shaped curve of an SShaped prior, anchored so no spend has no effect
    Carry-over adstock, adstock_prior Then delayed (alpha^((l - theta)^2)), geometric (alpha^l), weibull (pymc-marketing's PDF or CDF weights, a Weibull prior) or none, over l_max lags, normalised to sum to one
    Controls deterministic_transform, normalization linear, log, exp, indexp, power; Global Standardize over the fit window (or the stored mean/std)
    Seasons season n_fourier cos/sin pairs of period, one coefficient each, on BayesInsight's time grid
    Local trends localtrend A B-spline basis (num_knots, order) with Gaussian-random-walk coefficients, per level of random_coeff_dims or grouped by grouping_map; forecasts hold the last fitted level

    Where BayesInsight's own code cannot run a feature it defines (geometric and Weibull carry-over, the S-shaped curve, linear media, the zero-inflated negative binomial's psi), the plugin's choice is documented on mapi_spec.bayesinsight_model and the import reports it as an info issue. MvNormal likelihoods, time transforms (moving averages) and event priors are not supported: the import falls back to BayesInsight's defaults with an IMPORT_UNSUPPORTED warning.

    Outputs. y_hat(*panel, time) - the expected KPI (eta for a Normal likelihood, exp(eta + sigma^2 / 2) for a log-normal, the count mean times the zero-inflation share) or, predictive, a draw of the likelihood with common random numbers where the family allows - and contributions(component, *panel, time): each media variable, baseline (the intercepts), each control, season and trend. They sum to y_hat: additive terms for a Normal likelihood, a log-mean Divisia split of E[y] - baseline for the others, exact for terms of either sign.

    Calibration. Lift tests (channel: a media variable's analytic or model-stage name, geo: a label of the first panel dim) observe the incremental KPI Σ E[y | x + dx] - E[y | x] over the test window under the model's own likelihood, with the extra spend spread in proportion to the actual spend and carried over. Business priors are not supported: BayesInsight's coefficient priors are already statements in KPI units.

    Response curves, marginal costs and the decision objective are not offered yet, so the Guided what-if sliders and the scorecard's ROI checks skip this model (they say so).

    ts.hsgp_trend — smooth trend with seasonality¶

    A Hilbert-space Gaussian process (HSGP) trend plus an optional linear drift and Fourier seasonality, for one regularly spaced series (Mauna-Loa style CO2, sales, traffic).

    Inputs. t (time, policy extend) and the target y(t). Missing months are allowed: replace sentinel codes with core.replace_sentinel and they are left out of the likelihood while the model still interpolates them. Put core.standardize on y; the priors are on the standardised scale and outputs are mapped back to your units.

    Forecast horizon. max_extension (default 24 periods) is part of the model's input schema. The HSGP domain is sized at fit time from the anticipated coordinates (the fitted range plus max_extension periods), so every forecast up to that horizon evaluates exactly the fitted functions. A longer horizon is rejected before any compute with HORIZON_EXCEEDS_FIT (HTTP 409): refit with a larger max_extension.

    Setting Default Meaning
    max_extension 24 Periods a forecast may reach past the fit without a refit
    kernel expquad expquad, matern52 or matern32
    m, c 30, 1.5 Basis functions and boundary factor of the approximation
    lengthscale_mu, lengthscale_sigma 0.4, 0.5 Log-normal lengthscale prior on the scaled axis [-1, 1]
    eta_sigma 1.0 Half-normal scale of the GP amplitude
    linear_trend true Add intercept + slope * t
    season_period, season_order 12, 3 Fourier seasonality (periods of t, order)
    noise_sigma 0.5 Half-normal scale of the observation noise

    Outputs. y_hat(t) (expected value or a predictive draw) and trend(t), the long-term component without seasonality, both in the target's units.

    data:
  bind: {dims: {t: date}, vars: {y: co2}}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: t, last: 12}
pipeline:
  - {id: clean, use: core.replace_sentinel@^1, with: {vars: [y], below: 0}}
  - {id: scale, use: core.standardize@^1, with: {vars: [y], over: [t]}}
model:
  use: ts.hsgp_trend@^1
  settings: {max_extension: 24, season_order: 2}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: pymc, seed: 11}

    ts.gaussian_hmm — hidden states with a terminal state¶

    A K-state Gaussian hidden Markov model over many sequences at once (animal tracks, machine regimes, customer phases). Hidden states are summed out with the forward algorithm, so sampling is fast and exact.

    Inputs. Padded layout: animal (the sequence dim, new_levels with strategy pooled), step (extend), y(animal, step) with NaN padding and a boolean mask(animal, step).

    Terminal state. After the fit the filtered state distribution at the last fitted step is stored as the bundle's terminal_state group, alpha_T(chain, draw, animal, state).

    • predict / evaluate use the one-step-ahead state distribution of the fit inside the fitted range and alpha_T propagated by the transition matrix after it; they never look at targets in the payload.
    • forecast propagates alpha_T over the horizon.
    • filter runs the forward filter over new observations that continue the fitted steps and returns an updated terminal_state group with the filtered state_prob.
    • New animals start from the initial distribution pi at the first fitted step.

    Draws are relabelled after sampling so that the state means increase (label switching); identification: ordered uses an ordered prior instead. Pointwise LOO is declared invalid (elpd_loo is rejected at validation): steps within a track are dependent.

    Setting Default Meaning
    n_states 2 Number of hidden states
    mu_center, mu_sigma 0.0, 2.0 Normal prior of the state means
    sigma_scale 1.0 Half-normal scale of the per-state sds
    stickiness 2.0 Extra Dirichlet weight on staying in a state
    pi_concentration 1.0 Dirichlet weight of the initial distribution
    identification relabel relabel (sort draws by mean) or ordered

    Outputs. y_hat(animal, step) in your units and state_prob(animal, step, state) (units probability, never inverse-transformed).

    tabular.hierarchical_glm — partial pooling in long layout¶

    Varying intercepts (and optionally slopes) by group with group-level predictors: the radon model and its many relatives (schools, stores, clinics).

    Inputs. Long layout: rows along obs (extend), a group dim (group_dim, default county, new_levels with strategy hyperprior), the index <group_dim>_idx(obs), the target y(obs), observation-level covariates and group-level group_covariates. Covariates are part of the input schema, so a spec naming a missing one fails validation.

    New groups. A county the model never saw is drawn from the fitted hyperprior for every posterior draw; its group-level covariates shift its prior mean exactly as for fitted counties. Score payloads may list counties in any order: the platform remaps county_idx.

    Setting Default Meaning
    group_dim county The grouping dim
    family normal normal, student_t, poisson (log link) or bernoulli (logit)
    covariates [] Predictors over obs
    varying_slopes [] Covariates whose slope varies by group
    group_covariates [] Predictors over the group dim
    centered false Centred group effects

    tabular.regressor — quantile regression for panels¶

    The plan's store-demand model: rows are stacked from labelled dims (store × sku × week), the coordinates become categorical features, and an allow-listed estimator predicts conditional quantiles. Its estimator setting accepts exactly the quantile-capable estimators of the registry (xgboost, scikit-learn's gradient boosting and histogram boosting, and quantile regression), so other estimators are rejected at validation.

    • Defaults: xgboost (300 trees, depth 5, learning rate 0.05) and quantiles [0.1, 0.5, 0.9].
    • New stores are pooled (new_levels: pooled); forecasting is direct, so future covariates marked future_required must be supplied (MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE otherwise).
    • Quantiles are per cell: aggregating them across stores is refused (CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED).
    • The manifest lists core.fit_vs_actual, core.residuals_over_time and ml.feature_importance.

    All other settings are those of mapi_sklearn (see Tabular models).

    choice.multinomial_logit — which product customers pick¶

    The built-in model of the Guided customer choice template (E84-style household panels): on every shopping trip a household picks one product, and price and promotions shift the choice.

    Inputs. A dense hh × trip panel - hh (categorical, new_levels with strategy hyperprior), trip (index, extend) - with the alternatives along product (categorical, fixed). The target y(hh, trip) is text: the product chosen, empty where a household made no trip (ragged panels are fine). Covariates are alternative-specific variables along product, such as price(hh, trip, product) and promo(hh, trip, product); by default every numeric variable with the product dim is one. A product whose covariates are missing on a trip was not available on that trip (off the shelf).

    Model. A conditional logit with household-specific product intercepts: U[h, t, j] = asc[j] + Σ_k beta[k]·x_k[h, t, j] + u[h, j], the choice probabilities a softmax over the available products, u[h, j] ~ Normal(0, effect_sd) (a random-intercept logit). It is fitted by penalised maximum likelihood, the posterior mode of that model. Uncertainty comes from the Bayesian bootstrap: each of fit.draws draws refits with Dirichlet weights on households, so every household keeps its own effect in every draw and the draws are joint (draws representation: shares, counterfactual deltas and sums over trips are valid). There is no pointwise log-likelihood, so LOO is not offered.

    Scoring. y_hat(hh, trip, product) is the choice probability (expected) or one sampled choice per draw (predictive, one-hot). A household never seen at fit draws its preferences from Normal(0, sd) per draw, sd being the spread of the fitted effects in that draw. Trips never made have probability 0 for every product, so sums over trips count expected purchases. Price scenarios are interventions, e.g. {var: price, sel: {product: cola}, multiply: 1.1} in a counterfactual request. Choice values match product labels after lower-casing and replacing punctuation with _.

    Setting Default Meaning
    covariates every numeric variable along product Alternative-specific covariates
    random_effects [hh] Household product intercepts ([]: none)
    effect_sd 1.0 Prior sd of the household effects (smaller pools more)
    coef_sd 10.0 Prior sd of the product intercepts and coefficients
    reference the first product The product whose intercept is fixed at 0
    max_iter 500 Iterations of each L-BFGS fit

    fit.draws (default 100, at most 2000) sets the bootstrap draws and fit.seed makes them reproducible. The in-sample hit rate is reported as the CHOICE_HIT_RATE diagnostic.

    Holdout metrics. A text target scored as one probability per product gets the categorical metrics of evaluate.metrics: accuracy (the hit rate of the most likely product), accuracy_naive (always guessing the holdout's most common product), accuracy_lift (their difference), log_score (mean log probability of the product bought) and brier (multi-class Brier score). The Guided customer_choice template evaluates all five on each household's last trip and its scorecard runs holdout.hit_rate; numeric metrics such as mape are skipped for a text target.
