    Built-in models¶

    These model plugins come with the platform (tier T0). They register through the mapi.models entry point. Thus default_registry() and the control-plane catalog show them without configuration. Each plugin passes the contract kit (mapi_sdk.testing.check_model). Each plugin also has a conformance fixture (see Conformance corpus). From v0.5, the marketing-mix models run on mmm-framework. This is the only marketing-mix engine of the platform.

    Calibration (M3, the calibration block of the spec, Guided task 3): each plugin declares it in Capabilities.calibration. A spec can have calibration data for a plugin that does not declare it. Such a spec fails static validation with CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED:

    Plugin Business priors Lift tests

    mmm.frequentist no no
    mmm.nested no yes - the total effect of a channel (marginal ROAS or contribution)
    mmm.multivariate no no

    mmm.bayesian: marketing mix on mmm-framework¶

    This plugin uses the BayesianMMM of mmm-framework. It has these parts:

    • Adstock (geometric, delayed, Weibull) and saturation (logistic, Hill, Michaelis-Menten, tanh, root) for each channel.
    • A trend (none, linear, piecewise, spline, GP).
    • Fourier seasonality.
    • Baselines with partial pooling across geos and products. Channel effects for each geo are optional.
    • Channel effects as returns on spend.
    • Channel effects that change over time (time_varying) v0.12.
    • The likelihoods normal, student_t, poisson, negative_binomial (counts) and beta (shares) v0.12.

    The model owns scaling, carry-over and units (owns: [scaling, carryover, inverse_units]). Thus the pipeline only converts currencies and resamples. With outputs.derived, the fit caches contributions, response curves for each draw and marginal costs. The mmm-framework planning optimises budgets (POST …:optimize). The model declares a decision objective.

    Inputs. y(*panel, time), spend(*panel, channel, time), and other numeric variables over time as controls. The panel dims are hierarchy.pool_over (geo, product). New panel levels follow new_levels (pooled by default). channel is fixed. time extends.

    Outputs. y_hat(*panel, time) and contributions(component, *panel, time). The components are one for each channel, baseline, trend, seasonality and one for each control. Their sum is y_hat.

    Marketing-mix models on mmm-framework tells how the settings map to mmm-framework. It also tells about priors, lift tests and optimisation. For a complete analysis, read the marketing-mix guide.

    mmm.nested: marketing mix with an intermediate measure v0.12¶

    This plugin uses the NestedMMM of mmm-framework (mmm_extensions). Channels act on one or more mediators (for example visits or awareness), and the mediators act on the KPI. The channels can also act on the KPI directly. A measured mediator can have empty periods. A latent mediator has no data.

    Inputs. National data only: y(time), spend(channel, time) and mediators(mediator, time) (missing values allowed). There are no control variables. channel and mediator are fixed. time extends.

    Outputs. y_hat(time), contributions(component, time) (each channel with its total effect, baseline, trend, seasonality; their sum is y_hat) and mediator_hat(mediator, time). Derived: contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost, adstock and mediation (direct, indirect and total effect of each channel). Lift tests calibrate the total effect of a channel.

    mmm.multivariate: marketing mix for several results v0.12¶

    This plugin uses the MultivariateMMM of mmm-framework (mmm_extensions). Each outcome has its own equation. The noise of the outcomes is correlated (LKJ prior). Cross-effects (halo, cannibalization, unconstrained) link the outcomes. They are associations, not causes. Scores and budget plans use the reduced form. Thus, a channel that moves one outcome also moves the outcomes that it links to.

    Inputs. National data only: y(outcome, time) and spend(channel, time). There are no control variables. channel and outcome are fixed. time extends.

    Outputs. y_hat(outcome, time) and contributions(component, outcome, time). Their sum is y_hat for each outcome. Derived: contributions, response_curves (the weighted total of the outcomes, and each outcome), marginal_cost, adstock and cross_effects. There is no lift-test calibration.

    Marketing-mix models on mmm-framework gives the settings of the two models.

    mmm.frequentist: fast marketing mix¶

    This plugin uses the ridge estimator of mmm-framework. An out-of-sample search selects the adstock and saturation. The channel effects are not negative. The bootstrap gives confidence intervals (the replicates are the draws). The data contract and outputs are the same as mmm.bayesian. There are no priors, no lift tests and no convergence diagnostics. The same seed gives the same result, and a fit takes seconds.

    ts.hsgp_trend — smooth trend with seasonality¶

    This plugin has a Hilbert-space Gaussian process (HSGP) trend, an optional linear drift and Fourier seasonality. It is for one series with regular spaces between periods (CO2 as at Mauna Loa, sales, traffic).

    Inputs. t (time, policy extend) and the target y(t). Missing months are permitted. Replace sentinel codes with core.replace_sentinel. The likelihood then does not use these months, but the model interpolates them. Put core.standardize on y. The priors are on the standardised scale, and the model changes the outputs back to your units.

    Forecast horizon. max_extension (default 24 periods) is part of the input schema of the model. At fit time, the model sets the size of the HSGP domain from the anticipated coordinates. These are the fitted range plus max_extension periods. Thus each forecast up to that horizon evaluates exactly the fitted functions. The server rejects a longer horizon with HORIZON_EXCEEDS_FIT (HTTP 409) before it starts to compute. Fit again with a larger max_extension.

    Setting Default Meaning
    max_extension 24 The number of periods after the fit that a forecast can reach without a new fit
    kernel expquad expquad, matern52 or matern32
    m, c 30, 1.5 Basis functions and boundary factor of the approximation
    lengthscale_mu, lengthscale_sigma 0.4, 0.5 Log-normal lengthscale prior on the scaled axis [-1, 1]
    eta_sigma 1.0 Half-normal scale of the GP amplitude
    linear_trend true Adds intercept + slope * t
    season_period, season_order 12, 3 Fourier seasonality (periods of t, order)
    noise_sigma 0.5 Half-normal scale of the observation noise

    Outputs. y_hat(t) (the expected value or a predictive draw) and trend(t). trend(t) is the long-term component without seasonality. The two outputs are in the units of the target.

    data:
  bind: {dims: {t: date}, vars: {y: co2}}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: t, last: 12}
pipeline:
  - {id: clean, use: core.replace_sentinel@^1, with: {vars: [y], below: 0}}
  - {id: scale, use: core.standardize@^1, with: {vars: [y], over: [t]}}
model:
  use: ts.hsgp_trend@^1
  settings: {max_extension: 24, season_order: 2}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: pymc, seed: 11}

    ts.gaussian_hmm — hidden states with a terminal state¶

    This plugin is a Gaussian hidden Markov model with K states. It uses many sequences at the same time (animal tracks, machine regimes, customer phases). The forward algorithm sums out the hidden states. Thus the sampler is fast and exact.

    Inputs. A padded layout with these items: animal (the sequence dim, new_levels with strategy pooled), step (extend), y(animal, step) with NaN padding, and a boolean mask(animal, step).

    Terminal state. After the fit, the bundle keeps the filtered state distribution at the last fitted step. It is the terminal_state group of the bundle, alpha_T(chain, draw, animal, state).

    • predict / evaluate use the one-step-ahead state distribution of the fit in the fitted range. After the fitted range, they use alpha_T, propagated by the transition matrix. They do not use targets in the payload.
    • forecast propagates alpha_T over the horizon.
    • filter runs the forward filter over new observations that continue the fitted steps. It returns an updated terminal_state group with the filtered state_prob.
    • New animals start from the initial distribution pi at the first fitted step.

    After the sampler stops, the model gives new labels to the draws so that the state means increase (label switching). identification: ordered uses an ordered prior instead. The manifest declares pointwise LOO as not valid, because the steps in a track are dependent. Validation rejects elpd_loo.

    Setting Default Meaning
    n_states 2 Number of hidden states
    mu_center, mu_sigma 0.0, 2.0 Normal prior of the state means
    sigma_scale 1.0 Half-normal scale of the sds for each state
    stickiness 2.0 Additional Dirichlet weight to stay in the same state
    pi_concentration 1.0 Dirichlet weight of the initial distribution
    identification relabel relabel (sort the draws by mean) or ordered

    Outputs. y_hat(animal, step) in your units, and state_prob(animal, step, state). The units of state_prob are probability. The model does not inverse-transform it.

    ts.bsts and the causal models v0.13¶

    ts.bsts is a Bayesian structural time series on the state-space models of pymc-extras: a level, a trend, seasonal patterns and drivers with one effect for all periods. Like ts.gaussian_hmm, it keeps a terminal_state group. forecast starts from it and filter updates it. The manifest declares pointwise LOO as not valid.

    causal.impact (CausalImpact on the ts.bsts core), causal.synthetic_control (a Bayesian synthetic control for geo-lift tests) and causal.did (difference-in-differences and event study) write the effect of a change to the impact group. See Causal models and Bayesian structural time series for the inputs, the settings and the groups, and Measure the effect of a change for the procedures.

    tabular.hierarchical_glm — partial pooling in long layout¶

    This plugin has intercepts that change by group, and optionally slopes that change by group. It has group-level predictors. Examples are the radon model and many similar models (schools, stores, clinics).

    Inputs. A long layout with these items:

    • Rows along obs (extend).
    • A group dim (group_dim, default county, new_levels with strategy hyperprior).
    • The index <group_dim>_idx(obs).
    • The target y(obs).
    • Observation-level covariates and group-level group_covariates.

    Covariates are part of the input schema. Thus, if a spec names a covariate that is missing, the spec fails validation.

    New groups. For a county that the model did not see, the model draws its effect from the fitted hyperprior for each posterior draw. Its group-level covariates move its prior mean in the same way as for fitted counties. Score payloads can list counties in any sequence. The platform maps county_idx again.

    Setting Default Meaning
    group_dim county The grouping dim
    family normal normal, student_t, poisson (log link) or bernoulli (logit). From v0.14 (model version 1.1.0) also the families in the table below.
    n_categories none v0.14: the number of ordered categories (2 to 50). Necessary for ordinal.
    covariates [] Predictors over obs
    varying_slopes [] Covariates whose slope varies by group
    group_covariates [] Predictors over the group dim
    centered false Use centred group effects

    Families of v0.14. mu is the expected value of y.

    Family Link Extra parameter Data
    negative_binomial log alpha ~ HalfNormal (dispersion) Counts: whole numbers, 0 or more
    zero_inflated_poisson log psi ~ Beta(2, 2): the probability of the count process. mu = psi · exp(eta). Counts with many zeros
    zero_inflated_negative_binomial log alpha and psi Counts with many zeros and a large spread
    beta logit kappa ~ HalfNormal(50) (precision) Shares strictly between 0 and 1
    gamma log alpha ~ HalfNormal(5) (shape) Positive amounts, more than 0
    ordinal cumulative logit cutpoints: n_categories − 1 sorted values. The intercept is 0. mu = Σ k · p_k. Whole numbers from 0 to n_categories − 1

    If the target does not agree with the family (for example a negative count, or a share of exactly 0 or 1 for beta), the fit stops with a clear message.

    tabular.image_logistic — classify small images v0.14¶

    This plugin classifies small images, for example the images of an image folder upload. It is a Bayesian multinomial logistic regression on the main patterns of the pixels.

    Inputs. pixels(image, y, x, channel) with the dtype uint8 (role covariate) and the target label(image) as text (role target, missing values allowed). image is categorical with new_levels (strategy pooled). y and x are integer dims and channel is a categorical dim, all fixed.

    Model. The pixels are divided by 255. With downsample more than 1, the model takes the mean of blocks of pixels. With features: pca (the default), the model calculates the first n_components principal components of the training images and uses their standardised scores. With features: pixels, it uses the pixels (a maximum of max_features values). Then: alpha ~ ZeroSumNormal(2) over the classes, beta ~ ZeroSumNormal(prior_sigma) over the features and the classes, and label ~ Categorical(softmax(alpha + Z · beta)). The default engine is nutpie.

    Outputs. y_hat(chain, draw, image, class): the probability of each class (a maximum of 500 draws), with the attribute meaning: class_probability. The bundle keeps the PCA basis in the group features. The fit logs the params image.features, image.n_components, image.height and image.width, the metric explained_variance, and the images pca_components and class_means (the Media tab). The plots are core.roc_pr, core.confusion and core.convergence. loo is not valid for this model.

    Setting Default Meaning
    pixels, target, image_dim pixels, label, image The names of the inputs
    features pca pca or pixels
    n_components 16 The number of components (2 to 64, a maximum of the training images − 1)
    downsample 1 The size of the pixel blocks (1 to 16)
    max_features 256 pixels: the maximum of (height / downsample) × (width / downsample) × channels
    prior_sigma 1.0 The prior scale of beta (more than 0, a maximum of 10)
    class_order none The classes in a fixed sequence (a maximum of 50)
    input pixels v0.15: pixels or features (image features such as embedding(image, feature))
    feature_dim feature v0.15: the feature dim when input is features

    mapi_plugin_tabular.image_data.shapes_dataset() makes a small example dataset (circles, squares and crosses, 16 × 16 pixels, with a split coordinate).

    Features input (v1.1, v0.15). With input: features, the model reads image features, for example embedding(image, feature) from the step dl.embed. Set pixels to the name of the feature variable and feature_dim (default feature) to its dim. The model standardises the features. It uses PCA only when there are more than max_features features. See Deep learning: image features and a simple head.

    Deep learning (mapi-jax) v0.15¶

    The package mapi-jax has three models and one step. They use JAX, Flax (nnx) and Optax. The models train with the shared trainer of mapi_jax.trainer. All have the capabilities: inference custom, representation draws (chain = 1), score modes predict and evaluate, checkpointable, deterministic_given_seed, and loo_valid: false ("a network trained by gradient descent has no pointwise log likelihood").

    choice.multinomial_logit — which product customers pick¶

    This plugin is the built-in model of the Guided customer choice template (household panels as in E84). On each shopping trip, a household selects one product. Price and promotions change the choice.

    Inputs. A dense hh × trip panel, with the alternatives along product (categorical, fixed). hh is categorical, with new_levels with strategy hyperprior. trip is index and extend. The target y(hh, trip) is text: the product that the household selected. It is empty when a household made no trip (ragged panels are permitted). Covariates are variables for each alternative along product, for example price(hh, trip, product) and promo(hh, trip, product). By default, each numeric variable with the product dim is a covariate. If the covariates of a product are missing on a trip, that product was not available on that trip (not on the shelf).

    Model. A conditional logit with product intercepts for each household: U[h, t, j] = asc[j] + Σ_k beta[k]·x_k[h, t, j] + u[h, j]. The choice probabilities are a softmax over the available products. u[h, j] ~ Normal(0, effect_sd) (a random-intercept logit). The fit uses penalised maximum likelihood, that is, the posterior mode of that model.

    Uncertainty comes from the Bayesian bootstrap. Each of the fit.draws draws is a new fit with Dirichlet weights on households. Thus each household keeps its own effect in each draw, and the draws are joint. In the draws representation, shares, counterfactual deltas and sums over trips are valid. There is no pointwise log-likelihood, so LOO is not available.

    Scoring. y_hat(hh, trip, product) is the choice probability (expected), or one sampled choice for each draw (predictive, one-hot). The fit did not see some households. For each draw, the model draws their preferences from Normal(0, sd). sd is the spread of the fitted effects in that draw. A trip that the household did not make has probability 0 for all products. Thus sums over trips count the expected purchases.

    Price scenarios are interventions, for example {var: price, sel: {product: cola}, multiply: 1.1} in a counterfactual request. To match choice values to product labels, the model changes them to lower case and replaces punctuation with _.

    Setting Default Meaning
    covariates each numeric variable along product Covariates for each alternative
    random_effects [hh] Household product intercepts ([]: none)
    effect_sd 1.0 Prior sd of the household effects (a smaller value pools more)
    coef_sd 10.0 Prior sd of the product intercepts and coefficients
    reference the first product The product whose intercept is fixed at 0
    max_iter 500 Iterations of each L-BFGS fit

    fit.draws (default 100, maximum 2000) sets the number of bootstrap draws. With fit.seed, you get the same draws each time. The CHOICE_HIT_RATE diagnostic gives the in-sample hit rate.

    Holdout metrics. A text target with one score probability for each product gets the categorical metrics of evaluate.metrics:

    • accuracy: the hit rate of the most likely product.
    • accuracy_naive: the hit rate if you always select the most common product of the holdout.
    • accuracy_lift: the difference between the two.
    • log_score: the mean log probability of the product that the household bought.
    • brier: the multi-class Brier score.

    The Guided customer_choice template evaluates all five on the last trip of each household. Its scorecard runs holdout.hit_rate. For a text target, the server skips numeric metrics, for example mape.

    sci.areal_bym2 — disease risk on a map of areas (E18)¶

    Tutorial: E18 from start to end.

    A disease map for counts in areas (districts, counties). The count of each area is Poisson around its expected count times a relative risk rr. The log of rr is an intercept, plus a covariate effect, plus an area effect u. The kind setting selects the prior of u:

    kind Area effect u
    poisson No area effect
    iid Independent effects with sd sigma
    icar An intrinsic CAR (ICAR) on the graph of neighbours: each effect is close to the mean of its neighbours
    car A proper CAR with alpha ~ Uniform(0, 1). alpha is often near 1, and then the intercept mixes slowly
    bym2 A scaled ICAR plus independent effects. sigma is the total sd and rho is the share that follows the map

    For bym2, the model divides the ICAR by the square root of the scaling factor s of the graph (the geometric mean of the diagonal of the generalised inverse of the graph Laplacian). Thus sigma and rho have the same meaning on every map. The fit always keeps the log-likelihood of each area, so elpd_loo compares the kinds. Many Pareto k values are high, because each area has its own effect.

    Inputs. area and area_ (categorical, fixed: the same areas twice), vertex (integer, fixed), the target cases(area) (missing values are permitted), expected(area) (the expected count, for example age-standardised), one covariate(area), adjacency(area, area_) (1 for neighbours) and the polygon outlines ring_x(area, vertex) and ring_y(area, vertex) (NaN padded; a NaN inside a row starts a new ring).

    Islands. An area without neighbours makes the BYM2 scaling factor undefined, and under an ICAR its effect gets no information from other areas. The islands setting selects what happens:

    • link_nearest (default): the model links each such area to the area with the closest polygon vertex. Then it links each remaining separate piece of the graph in the same way. The links are in learned["links"].
    • isolate: the graph stays as it is. Only iid and icar accept this.
    • error: a graph with more than one piece stops the fit with SCHEMA_INVALID.

    Outputs. cases(area) (the expected count expected × rr, or a Poisson draw), rr(area) (the relative risk), p_exceed(area) (1 when rr is more than exceed_threshold in a draw, so its mean is the probability) and effect(area) (u). The bundle keeps the map for the plots in constant_data: ring_x, ring_y, adjacency (the graph that the model used, with the links), centroid(area, xy) and smr(area) (observed ÷ expected). Thus sci.area_map draws the maps (use observed: smr for the raw ratio) and net.graph draws the graph at the centroids.

    Score modes. A score needs only the area labels. expected and covariate come from the fit when the payload does not give them. A payload can change them (for example, a different covariate). simulate draws a new map of area effects for each draw from the fitted sigma, rho and alpha, and then new counts. A hand-written sampler draws the ICAR, because pm.ICAR cannot draw values. Areas that are not in the fit stop the score with UNSEEN_LEVELS.

    Leave-areas-out. A mask_block split over area removes the counts of the held-out areas from the likelihood, but the areas stay in the graph. The model then predicts them from their neighbours.

    Setting Default Meaning
    kind bym2 poisson, iid, icar, car or bym2
    islands link_nearest link_nearest, isolate or error
    covariate_scale 10 The model divides the centred covariate by this value. b1 is the effect of one unit
    center_covariate true Subtract the mean of the covariate
    intercept_sd, slope_sd 1, 1 Prior sd of b0 and b1
    sigma_scale 1 Half-normal scale of sigma
    rho_alpha, rho_beta 1, 1 Beta prior of rho (bym2)
    exceed_threshold 1 The threshold of p_exceed

    from_challenges reads scotland_lip.geojson: the 56 districts of Scotland with the lip cancer cases from 1975 to 1980, the expected cases and aff (the percentage of workers in agriculture, fishing and forestry). The adjacency is queen contiguity from the polygons (117 edges), as in the notebook. Orkney, Shetland and the Western Isles have no neighbours, and link_nearest links them to Caithness, Orkney and Ross-Cromarty. Use graph="rook" for shared border segments, or graph="published" for the hand-edited WinBUGS list of scotland_lip_adjacency.csv. With kind: bym2 the AFF effect b1 is 0.41 (0.67 with iid), rho is about 0.8, and 19 districts have P(rr > 1) > 0.95. The fit takes a few seconds.

    Typed errors: SETTINGS_INVALID for bym2 or car on a graph with more than one piece, and for simulate of an ICAR with isolated areas. SCHEMA_INVALID for counts that are not whole non-negative numbers, for an expected count that is not more than zero, and for a graph with islands when islands is error.

    sci.heat_plate — temperature of a heated plate (E51)¶

    Tutorial: E51 from start to end.

    The 2-D steady heat equation of a thin plate. Heat moves in the plane of the plate (conductivity k) and goes out through the two faces (h) and the outer edges (h_edge). A part eta of the heater power goes into the plate. The model divides the plate into square cells (finite volumes). This gives a linear system A(θ) u = eta q for the rise u above the room. The model solves the system for each run in the model graph (pytensor.tensor.linalg.solve). The readings are Normal around T_room + u with the noise sigma.

    The field is linear in the heater powers. Thus one fit predicts the field of any new heater layout. To do this, score a payload with new run labels and their heater_power.

With use_insulation: true, the data also has a bool insulation(y, x): True where the back face of the cell has insulation.

    Outputs. field(run, y, x) (a field with a frame for each run, in °C) and the scalar spread_length (sqrt(k δ / 2h), cm). predict gives the steady field. simulate gives simulated sensor readings in all cells (the field plus the noise). The pointwise log-likelihood has one value for each reading. Thus elpd_loo works when the spec keeps it (keep: {log_likelihood: true}).

    room_temperature 20 Temperature of the room (°C)
    use_insulation false The data has insulation(y, x)

    k_median, k_log_sd 50, 0.5 Log-normal prior of the conductivity k (W/m/K)

    sci.turing_pattern — Turing patterns from early images (E61)¶

    Tutorial: E61 from start to end.

    The Schnakenberg activator-substrate system makes spots or stripes (a Turing pattern). The model gets its rates from images of the activator that you take while the pattern starts to grow. In this phase the growth is linear. Thus each Fourier mode of the images is a complex Gaussian, and the modes are independent. The model groups the modes by their integer |k|² and uses a Woodbury identity. Thus the likelihood needs only 2 × 2 algebra for each wavenumber, and it is exact for the linear dynamics. The model finds the mode first, and NUTS samples near that mode with a Laplace preconditioner.

    The units have no dimensions. Lengths are in units where the activator diffuses at a rate of 1. The parameters are the supply s = a + b, the activator share rho = a / s, the diffusion ratio d, the reaction scale g, the initial noise s0 and the camera noise sigma. The priors are those of the notebook.

    Inputs. t (float, extend: the time since the start of the pattern, t > 0), y and x (integer, fixed) and the target activator(t, y, x). The raster must be square, with an even number of pixels on each side, and must have no missing pixels. The model treats the square as periodic.

    Outputs.

    • pattern(t, y, x): a field with a frame for each time. In predict and evaluate, it is a draw of the activator at the requested times, given the fitted images. In simulate, it is a new pattern from the nonlinear spectral solver, with camera noise.
    • settled(y, x): only in simulate. The pattern of each run at settle_time.
    • growth(wavenumber): the dispersion relation (the growth rate of each wavenumber).
    • p_turing: 1 when the draw meets the Turing conditions, else 0. Its mean is the probability.
    • wavelength: the wavelength 2π / k_m of the fastest-growing mode.
    • turing_space(b_supply, a_supply): a field. Its mean is P(Turing) for each pair of supply rates.

    The posterior also has a, b, wavelength, max_growth and p_turing. With fit.keep.log_likelihood: true, the bundle keeps the log-likelihood of each Fourier mode (log_likelihood/activator(chain, draw, mode)). The modes are independent, thus elpd_loo over the modes is valid.

    Setting Default Meaning
    box_length 100 The side of the square in the length unit of the model
    k2_max 1.5 Use only the Fourier modes with a squared wavenumber less than this value
    max_extension 2 Image times after the fit that a prediction can go to

    sci.storm_localisation — molecule positions in STORM frames (E58)¶

    Tutorial: E58 from start to end.

    Single-molecule localisation microscopy (STORM, PALM, dSTORM). The data are the raw frames of an EMCCD camera. The model finds isolated spots and gives the position of each molecule with an error bar in nm. Detection, camera calibration and the patches use NumPy and scipy.ndimage at fit time. Then NUTS fits all patches in one model. Each patch has one emitter with its own position, photons, background and PSF sd. The image of the emitter is a Gaussian PSF that is integrated over each pixel (erf). The counts are Poisson.

    Camera. An EMCCD at high gain doubles the variance of the photon counts. The effective counts z = round((ADU - offset_adu) / scale_adu) are Poisson with a mean of half the photons. scale_adu is twice the EM gain. Set offset_adu and scale_adu together. If you do not set them, the model fits the photon-transfer curve of the frames: the robust temporal variance of each pixel against its median, without the brightest 10 % of the pixels. This calibration needs pixels with a range of background brightness. A small crop over one bright structure is not sufficient. For such data, calibrate on a larger field and set the two values.

    Detection. The model subtracts the per-pixel time median from each frame, smooths with a Gaussian (smooth_px) and keeps the connected regions above threshold_sd robust noise sds. A blob is isolated when no other blob is in its patch_px × patch_px patch and it has min_blob_px to max_blob_px pixels. The fit uses max_spots isolated blobs, evenly spaced in frame order.

    Inputs. frame, row and col (integer, fixed; rows and columns are consecutive pixel numbers) and the target frames(frame, row, col) (int32, ADU). The frames must have no missing values. Thus the model has no split: evaluate it in sample.

    Outputs.

    • x, y (spot): the position in nm. It is the column (row) number times pixel_nm.
    • photons, background (photons per pixel) and psf_sd (nm) of each spot.
    • render(render_y, render_x): the super-resolution image (a field). Each draw puts each spot at its drawn position as a small Gaussian. The mean of the draws is the usual image where each localisation is a Gaussian with its own error.
    • patch_fit(spot, py, px): the effective counts of each patch (a field with a frame for each spot). With kind: predictive, these are Poisson draws.
    • frames_hat(frame, row, col): the camera image in ADU. Inside a fitted patch it is offset_adu + scale_adu × patch_fit. Outside the patches it is the per-pixel time median. evaluate compares it with the frames. Spots that the model did not fit count as misfit.

The bundle keeps predictions/render (score_draws draws) as well, so sci.point_pattern (the positions with error ellipses over render) and sci.field_map draw the super-resolved image from the fit alone. A whole long movie, such as the 2,000 real frames, is too large for the in-sample evaluate (see PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE below).

    sci.ibp_features — shared parts of small images (E31)¶

    Tutorial: E31 from start to end.

    A latent feature model for small images, for example handwritten digits. Each image is the average image plus the features that it owns, plus Gaussian noise: x = mu + z A + e. Each z is 0 or 1. The prior of z is an Indian buffet process (stick-breaking, truncated at K = n_features): alpha ~ Gamma, nu_k ~ Beta(alpha, 1), pi_k = nu_1 ... nu_k. The model sums z out exactly over the 2^K patterns of each image (a pm.Potential). Thus NUTS samples only the feature images A, the sticks, alpha and sigma_A. Each extra feature doubles the time, so n_features is 10 or less.

    Inputs. digit (categorical, fixed), y and x (integer, fixed, row 0 at the top) and the target ink(digit, y, x) from 0 (blank) to 1 (full ink). An empty value is a pixel that the model does not see. With shown_mask: true, the data also has a bool shown(digit, y, x). The fit does not use a pixel where shown is false, but the bundle keeps its value for comparison. The average image mu is the mean of the images with no hidden pixels.

    Outputs. ink_hat(digit, y, x) (a field for each digit, grey scale): the expected image given the seen pixels. Where the model did not see a pixel, this is the completion. The predictive kind draws a pattern and the noise. features(feature, y, x) (a field for each feature, diverging scale) gives the change of ink that each feature adds to the average image. feature_prob(feature) gives pi_k, and owns(digit, feature) gives the probability that each image owns each feature. A score uses only the pixels that the bundle keeps, never the target values in the score data. Thus a held-out pixel cannot get into its own prediction.

    sci.colour_naming — how the speakers of a language name colours (E77)¶

    Tutorial: E77 from start to end.

    The World Color Survey asked the speakers of 110 languages to name 330 colour chips. This model fits the naming of one language. The probability that a speaker gives term t to chip c is a softmax over the terms. The logit of each term is a quadratic surface in CIELAB colour space: a multinomial logistic regression on 10 standardised features (1, L, a, b, L², a², b², ab, La, Lb). The weights W are zero-sum over the terms (ZeroSumNormal(w_sd)).

    • kind: pooled gives all speakers the same naming. The likelihood uses the chip × term counts.
    • kind: hierarchical gives each speaker an intercept and linear weights (L, a, b) of their own, around the weights of the language: W_s = W + sd z_s, with sd ~ HalfNormal(sd_scale) and z ~ ZeroSumNormal(1). A term that only some speakers use gets a large sd on its intercept.
    • features: grid_line and features: grid_circle use the place of the chip on the printed palette (row and hue column) instead of its colour. grid_line treats the hue as a line, and grid_circle treats it as a circle. Fit the variants with elpd_loo in a workflow to compare them.

    Inputs. speaker (categorical, policy new_levels, strategy hyperprior), chip (integer, fixed) and the target naming(speaker, chip). The target holds the term labels as text. An empty cell is a speaker who gave no answer. The chip variables are lab_l, lab_a, lab_b (the CIELAB colour) and grid_row, grid_col (the place on the palette; WCS rows A to J are 0 to 9 and the greys are in column 0). The model orders the terms from the most used to the least used.

    Outputs.

    • p_name(speaker, chip, term): the probability that the speaker gives the term to the chip. A fitted speaker keeps their own effects. A new speaker gets new effects in each draw, so the mean over the draws is the prediction for a new speaker. With kind: predictive, each draw holds one sampled term (one-hot). The evaluate mode always uses the probabilities.
    • p_term(term, lightness, hue): the share of the speakers of the language who give the term to the chip, on the palette grid (a field with one frame for each term). The fit averages new_speakers simulated speakers in each draw. A grid cell without a chip is empty in the fitted model and 0 in a score.
    • mode_term_rgb(chip, rgb): each chip in the display colour of its most probable term in the draw. The display colour of a term is the CIELAB mean of the chips where the term is most probable. The row and col coords put the chips on the palette for sci.swatches. The observed panel paints each chip with the term that most speakers gave.
    • p_top(chip): the share of speakers who give the chip its most probable term.
    • k_eff_speaker and k_eff_language: the effective number of colour categories of one speaker and of the language, exp of the mutual information between chip and term.

    sci.cmb_field — the CMB sky behind a mask (E79)¶

    Tutorial: E79 from start to end.

    Field-level inference of a temperature map of the cosmic microwave background (CMB). Each pixel of the sky is a parameter. The model samples the sky together with the power spectrum in bins (bandpowers). Masked pixels have no data term, and each pixel has its own noise level. Thus the model needs no correction for the mask, as a simple spectrum estimate does.

    The sky is a Gaussian random field on a flat, periodic torus. The torus is the map plus a border of border pixels without data on each side, because a real patch does not repeat at its edges. The prior power of each Fourier (Hartley) mode is the reference spectrum after the beam, the pixel windows and aliasing, times exp(q_b) for bin b. q_b has a flat prior.

    The sampler is not NUTS. It is the Wandelt-Eriksen Gibbs sampler of the notebook, in NumPy:

    1. A constrained realisation of the sky for the current bandpowers. Preconditioned conjugate gradients (CG) solve one linear system. The preconditioner is dense below ell_dense and diagonal above it.
    2. A draw of each bandpower from its inverse-gamma distribution, given the sky.
    3. A rescaling move for each bin (Metropolis). It helps in the bins where the noise is larger than the signal.

    tune sets the burn-in iterations and draws the kept iterations of each chain. All chains run together. The fit is deterministic for a given seed.

    Inputs. row and col (integer, fixed, row 0 at the bottom) and multipole (integer, fixed, 0, 1, 2, ...). The target temperature(row, col) is in µK, with NaN where the mask removes a pixel. noise_sd(row, col) is the noise sd of each pixel (µK). reference_dl(multipole) is the reference spectrum D_ℓ = ℓ(ℓ+1)C_ℓ/2π in µK². With pixel_window: true, the data also has pixel_window(multipole), the pixel window of the source map (for example HEALPix).

    Outputs. sky(row, col) and temperature_hat(row, col) are fields (diverging colours). sky is a sky map for each of field_draws posterior draws. temperature_hat is the same map, plus noise with noise_sd for kind: predictive. bandpower(bin) (µK²) and bandpower_ratio(bin) have the coords ell, ell_low and ell_high on bin. The scalars log_amplitude and tilt compare the bandpowers with the reference: they come from the exact Gaussian posterior of q_b = log A + tilt · log(ℓ_b / tilt_pivot), with the bins below tilt_max_ell. The fit keeps the mean and the sd of each pixel over all kept Gibbs draws as the derived group sky_moments. In simulate mode, the model makes skies of a new patch with the spectrum of each posterior draw, plus noise. The mean of a new patch comes from the spectrum too (a few µK), not from the vague mean_prior_sd prior of the fitted patch's mean.

    sci.network_latent_space — who works with whom (E21)¶

    Tutorial: E21 from start to end.

    A model of an undirected graph. Each pair of nodes i < j has a tie or not (Bernoulli, logit link). The log odds have three parts:

    • alpha + x_ij beta: the pair covariates (homophily), for example the same office.
    • s_i + s_j: the sociality of the two nodes (how many ties each node has).
    • -||z_i - z_j||: the distance between the latent positions of the two nodes.

    The kind setting selects the parts: covariates (the first part), sociality (the first two) or latent_space (all three). With covariates: false, the model has no pair covariates. kind: covariates with covariates: false is the Erdős-Rényi graph.

    The distances do not change when the positions turn, flip or move together. Thus the raw positions z_raw do not converge, but each distance does. After the fit, the model aligns each draw to one reference with Procrustes analysis. The aligned draws are the posterior variable position. The reference is constant_data/position_ref. The fit then drops the raw draws z_raw and s_raw from the posterior (position and sociality hold them, with the same distances). Thus the convergence diagnostics and the plots core.convergence and core.posterior_forest use only identified quantities: alpha, beta, sigma_s, sigma_z, sociality, position and tie_prob, in that order.

    Inputs. node and node_ (the same labels, fixed) and the target tie(node, node_): 1 for a tie, 0 for no tie, and a missing value when you do not know. The matrix must be symmetric. The likelihood uses a pair only when its two cells are not missing. With covariates: true, the data has dyad_cov(node, node_, cov) (cov is categorical, fixed). With node_groups: true, the data has node_group(node), an integer code for the colours of the plots.

    Outputs.

    • tie_prob(node, node_): the probability of a tie (a field, 0 on the diagonal).
    • tie(node, node_): the probability, or a simulated graph with kind: predictive and in simulate mode. The graphs are symmetric. The random numbers belong to the pair, so a subset of the nodes gets the same ties.
    • degree(node): the ties of each node with the scored nodes (the node_ labels of the score data), expected or in a simulated graph. Score all the nodes for the degree in the graph: on a subset, degree counts only the ties inside the subset.
    • sociality(node) and position(node, latent_dim), when the kind has them.
    • graph_stat(statistic): statistics of each simulated graph (density, sd_degree, max_degree, isolates, triangles, transitivity, esp0, mean_geodesic, unreachable). esp0 is the number of ties whose two nodes have no shared neighbour. Compare them with the statistics of the data (posterior predictive checks). The statistics need simulated graphs of all the nodes of the fit. A request that names graph_stat in output.vars for a subset of the nodes, or for expected probabilities, fails with CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED.

    The posterior also keeps tie_prob (float32) for the plots of the fit, and constant_data/adjacency keeps the observed matrix. The fit always keeps the log-likelihood of each observed pair (dim dyad; the pairs are in constant_data/dyad_i and dyad_j). Thus elpd_loo is leave-one-pair-out, and the kinds of a workflow ladder use the same pairs in the same order. A mask_block split over [node, node_] holds out random cells.     sci.graphical_model — direct links between variables (E19)¶

    Tutorial: E19 from start to end.

    A Gaussian graphical model. The model measures the partial correlation of each pair of variables: the correlation that stays when all the other variables are held constant. A zero partial correlation means that the two variables have no direct link. A link (an edge) is present when |partial correlation| > threshold, and the model gives the probability of each edge. The model centres and scales each variable with the mean and the sd of the fit data.

    There are two kinds:

    • dense (default): one multivariate normal with an LKJ prior on the correlations (eta = 2) and an Exponential(1) prior on the sds. The precision matrix comes from a triangular solve. This kind also predicts and simulates new samples.
    • neighbourhood: a sparse (regularised horseshoe) regression of each variable on all the others, in one model. The edge_rule setting makes the two coefficients of a pair into one edge: and (both pass the threshold), or (one passes) or rho (their symmetric combination). This kind conditions on the other variables of each sample. Thus it does not predict, simulate or evaluate new samples, and a spec with this kind has no split.

    Inputs. cell (integer, policy new_levels with the pooled strategy: the samples are exchangeable, so new samples can be scored), var (categorical, fixed: the variables) and the target value(cell, var). With reference: true, the data also has a known network reference(var, var_) (1 = edge, constant). The fit keeps it in constant_data, and net.graph draws it below the learned edges.

    Outputs. pcor(var, var_) (the partial correlations, a field with a diverging colour scale), edge_prob(var, var_) (the edge indicator of each draw: its mean is the edge probability) and, for kind dense, value(cell, var). With kind expected, value is the mean of each variable. With kind predictive or in simulate mode, it is a new sample from the model. The fit also keeps the posterior precision(var, var_) and the learned values edges (the pairs with a probability above 0.5), undecided_pairs (between 0.2 and 0.8) and pcor_sd_mean.

    Setting Default Meaning
    kind dense dense (LKJ multivariate normal) or neighbourhood (horseshoe regressions)
    threshold 0.1 An edge is a partial correlation larger than this value (in absolute value)
    reference false The data has a known network reference(var, var_)
    standardise true Centre and scale each variable with the fit data
    log_likelihood auto The log-likelihood that the fit keeps for elpd_loo: joint (dense only) or conditional. auto is joint for dense and conditional for neighbourhood
    lkj_eta, sd_rate, mean_sd 2, 1, 1 dense: the LKJ shape, the rate of the sd prior and the sd of the mean prior
    expected_neighbours 2 neighbourhood: the prior guess of the links of each variable
    slab_alpha, slab_beta 2, 2 neighbourhood: the slab prior c² ~ InvGamma(alpha, beta)
    edge_rule and neighbourhood: and, or or rho

    The fit keeps one log-likelihood value for each sample when fit.keep.log_likelihood is true. To compare a dense fit and a neighbourhood fit with elpd_loo, set log_likelihood: conditional on both. Then both values are the sum of the log densities of each variable given the other variables of the same sample.

    Typed errors: SETTINGS_INVALID for log_likelihood: joint with kind neighbourhood (when the settings are parsed), or expected_neighbours that is not smaller than the number of other variables; SCHEMA_INVALID for score data without a cell dim, missing values, a variable with no variation, fewer than two variables, or reference: true without a reference variable; MODE_NOT_SUPPORTED for simulate, evaluate or predictive outputs with kind neighbourhood, and for forecast and counterfactual.

    from_challenges reads the Sachs et al. (2005) flow cytometry data: the 853 cells with only the CD3/CD28 stimulus and 11 proteins (log of the intensity + 10). When sachs2005_consensus.txt is in the same directory, it adds the 20 edges of the consensus network as consensus. With observational=False, it reads all 7466 cells of the nine conditions (the notebook shows why you must not pool them). On the 853 cells, the dense model finds 8 edges with a probability above 0.5. Seven of them are in the consensus network. The fit takes approximately 40 seconds with nutpie. The neighbourhood model finds 6 edges, all in the consensus network, in approximately 2 minutes.

    sci.phylo_regression — traits of related species on a tree (E23)¶

    Tutorial: E23 from start to end.

    A regression of one trait of species on other traits. Related species are alike because they share ancestors, so the residuals are correlated by a phylogeny (a tree with branch lengths). The model reads the correlation from the shared branch length of two species. On standardised log values it is trait ~ MvNormal(intercept + X slope, K). The kind setting selects K:

    kind Residual correlation
    iid None: sigma² I (the tree is not used)
    brownian Brownian motion along the branches: sigma² R (R = shared length ÷ tree depth)
    lambda Pagel's lambda: sigma² (lam R + (1 - lam) I), a tree part and a species part
    ou Ornstein-Uhlenbeck: eta2 exp(-rho D / 2T) on the distance D through the tree (T = tree depth)

    For ou, the data identify only the Brownian rate rate = eta2 rho. Thus the default prior is on rate (ou_parameterisation: rate). This removes the ridge of the (eta2, rho) posterior.

    Inputs. species (categorical, fixed) and node (integer, fixed). The target is trait(species); missing values are permitted. Each name in predictors is a variable on species with no missing values. The tree is four variables on node: parent (the position of the parent node, -1 at the root), branch_length (the branch above the node), is_tip and tip_label (the species name of each tip). Each species label must be a tip label. The tree can have unary nodes.

    Outputs. trait(species): the trait of each species, predicted from the other species by Gaussian conditioning, in the units of the target. For a fitted species, the prediction leaves that species out. For a species without a trait value, it uses all fitted species. With log_target, the expected value is the conditional median. slope(predictor): the slopes. If the score data changes a fitted species' predictors, its prediction moves by the change in its regression mean. The fit also keeps posterior_predictive/trait(species) (the same predictions as conditional means, beside observed_data/trait for the sci.phylogeny plot) and the tree in constant_data.

    Score modes. predict and evaluate predict each requested species from its relatives. simulate draws new traits for the requested species from the fitted parameters, without the observed traits. Use a mask_block split on species to hold out species: the model predicts them from the species that remain.

    LOO. log_likelihood/trait holds the leave-one-out conditional density of each observed species, log p(y_i | y_-i), from Q = K⁻¹. Thus elpd_loo compares the four kinds correctly (a workflow can fit each kind and compare them). The marginal density of each species gives the opposite ranking.

    Setting Default Meaning
    kind lambda iid, brownian, lambda or ou
    predictors [body, group_size] The predictor variables on species
    log_target, log_predictors true, true Take the log first (the values must be positive)
    standardise true Centre and scale by the fitted species
    intercept_sd, slope_sd 1, 0.5 Normal prior sd of the intercept and of each slope
    sigma_rate 1 Rate of the exponential prior of sigma
    lambda_alpha, lambda_beta 1, 1 Beta prior of lam
    ou_parameterisation rate rate: prior on eta2 rho; variance: priors on eta2 and rho
    rate_sd, rho_sd, eta2_sd 1, 5, 5 Half-normal prior sd for the ou parameters
    jitter 1e-6 ou: added to the diagonal of K

    from_challenges reads primates301.csv and the tree primates301_tree.rda (an R ape::phylo object). It reads the R file without R. read_tree also reads Newick files. The dataset has the 186 species with a body mass and a group size; 151 of them have a brain volume. The tree is pruned to these species. With the notebook settings (4 chains of 1000 draws), the lambda fit gives a group-size slope of 0.027 ± 0.020 and lam = 0.954. The iid fit gives 0.123. elpd_loo is about 130 for lambda, 91 for brownian and ou, and 15 for iid.

    Typed errors. SCHEMA_INVALID when a species has no tip of the same label, the tree has no single root or has a cycle, a predictor has missing values, a value is not positive for a log, or fewer than 3 species have a trait value. UNSEEN_LEVELS when a score requests a species that the fit did not see.

    sci.circular_regression — directions and times of day (E81)¶

    Tutorial: E81 from start to end.

    The data are angles: the direction that a wind comes from, or the time of day of a photograph. 359 degrees is next to 1 degree, and 23:59 is next to 00:01. Thus the model uses densities on the circle, as the E81 case study does. It has four kinds:

    • von_mises (default). The hour of the day and the day of the year are features: an intercept, cos and sin of k 2π h / 24 and of k 2π d / 365.25, and the products of the first daily harmonics with the first annual harmonic. The defaults give 19 features F. The natural parameters of a von Mises are η = F B (the canonical link). The mean direction is atan2(η₂, η₁) and the concentration is |η|. This model has one mode. It has no wrapped prior and no seam at ±π.
    • projected_normal. The angle of a vector z ~ N(F B, I).
    • regime_mixture. Two von Mises regimes (for example the easterly night wind and the westerly sea breeze). Each regime uses fewer features. A logistic gate on all features gives the probability of the second regime. The prior directions of the regimes keep their labels.
    • activity. A log-Fourier density for each group (species): log f(θ) = Σ aₖ cos kθ + bₖ sin kθ − log Z, with the prior aₖ, bₖ ~ N(0, (τ/k)²). The model uses only the sums of cos kθ and sin kθ for each group.

    The priors of von_mises and projected_normal are N(0, intercept_sd = 2) on the intercepts and N(0, coef_sd = 1) on the other coefficients. regime_mixture does not use intercept_sd, as in the notebook. The intercept of regime r has the prior N(regime_strength (cos μᵣ, sin μᵣ), 1), with μᵣ from regime_directions_deg. The gate intercept has the prior N(0, gate_intercept_sd = 1.5). The other coefficients of the regimes and of the gate have the prior N(0, coef_sd). NUTS samples the coefficients. With fit.keep.log_likelihood: true, the bundle keeps the log density of each row (per radian). Thus elpd_loo compares the kinds on the same rows, for example in a workflow ladder von_mises → projected_normal → regime_mixture.

    Inputs. obs (integer, policy extend, one row for each report) and the target direction(obs) in units. The regression kinds also need hour(obs) (hours since midnight, 0 to 24) and day(obs) (days since 1 January). The kind activity needs a categorical dim (group_dim, fixed) and the index <group_dim>_idx(obs). Remove rows with no direction (a calm wind has no direction) before the fit.

    Outputs.

    • direction_hat(obs): the mean direction of the row, in units from 0 to one turn. With kind: predictive and in simulate mode, it is one simulated angle.
    • resultant(obs): the mean resultant length of the row (1: one direction, 0: no preferred direction).
    • density(frame, angle): the density per unit of units on angle_points angles (a field: frames on the rows, angles on the columns). For the regression kinds, a frame is a block of 24 / frames hours. Its density is the mean of the densities of up to rows_per_frame fit rows in that block (the rose diagram of the model). For activity, a frame is a group.
    • mean_direction(frame) and resultant_length(frame): the mean resultant vector of each frame.
    • activity only: activity_level(frame) (1 / (2π max f), the part of the day that the group is active) and overlap(frame, frame_) (the area under the lower of two densities, a field).

    The fit also keeps the fields density(frame, angle) and, for activity, overlap(frame, frame_) in the posterior. They have the same values as in a score. Thus sci.field_map and sci.profile_bands show the densities (and, with var: overlap, the overlaps) in the plots of a fit run.

    With fit.keep.posterior_predictive, the bundle keeps simulated angles theta (radians, 0 to 2π) and the observed theta in observed_data. sci.rose uses them in its default settings: it shows the observed rose with the range of the model. The default settings use the orientation of mathematics: 0 is at the right (east) and the angles increase anticlockwise. Thus a wind from the west (270°) is at the bottom. For a compass or a clock (0 at the top, clockwise), pass clockwise: true to sci.rose in a plot.render step or in the plots request. For the density of each frame, use sci.rose with var: density, frame: frame and units set to the units of the data. The default rose of a score result reads direction_hat, which is in the units of the data, as radians. Pass var: direction_hat and units set to the units of the data.

    Setting Default Meaning
    kind von_mises von_mises, projected_normal, regime_mixture or activity
    units degrees Units of the direction: degrees, radians or hours
    daily_harmonics, annual_harmonics 3, 2 Harmonics of the hour and of the day of the year
    interaction_harmonics 2 Daily harmonics multiplied by the first annual harmonic
    intercept_sd, coef_sd 2, 1 Prior sd of the intercepts (not for regime_mixture) and of the other coefficients
    regime_directions_deg, regime_names [90, 250], [easterly, westerly] regime_mixture: prior directions and names of the two regimes
    regime_daily_harmonics, regime_annual_harmonics 2, 1 regime_mixture: features of each regime
    regime_strength 2 regime_mixture: prior mean concentration of each regime (the length of the prior mean of its intercept)
    gate_intercept_sd 1.5 regime_mixture: prior sd of the gate intercept
    group_dim group activity: the dim of the groups
    harmonics, tau_sd, grid_points 16, 2, 256 activity: Fourier terms, prior scale of τ, angles of the sum for Z
    frames, angle_points, rows_per_frame 8, 72, 200 Blocks of the day, angles and fit rows of the density output
    score_draws 400 Posterior draws for each score

    from_challenges reads the hourly wind reports of Santa Barbara airport (2021–2023). It removes the calm reports and uses Pacific standard time (UTC − 8). ksba_spec_yaml(ds) holds out 2023 as the notebook does. On the 12,023 reports of 2021–2022, elpd_loo is −17,385 (the notebook: −17,385). The mean log score of 2023 is −1.517 for each report (the notebook: −1.517). from_challenges_activity reads the camera-trap times of Barro Colorado Island for the ocelot and five species that it hunts. With BCI_SPEC_YAML, the overlap of the ocelot and the agouti is 0.30 (the notebook: 0.30).

    direction_hat and mean_direction are circular outputs (OutputDecl.circular, with their period attr: 360, 2π or 24). Their score summaries are on the circle: the circular mean, and intervals centred on it in [0, period). The holdout metrics of evaluate are circular: the point is the circular mean of the draws, mae, rmse and bias use the error wrapped to half a turn (359° against 1° is an error of 2°), coverage_<p> and interval_width_<p> use central intervals of the draws around the circular mean (an interval may cross 0), and crps uses the arc distance. mape, smape, wape and r2 are skipped. For a proper score of the whole density, use elpd_loo, or CircularRegressionModel().holdout_log_score(bundle.model, ds) for the log score of held-out rows. Errors: SCHEMA_INVALID for a missing direction, an hour out of range or a bad group index; MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE when the score data have no hour or day; SETTINGS_INVALID for more interaction harmonics than daily harmonics.
