    Forecasting and sequential models¶

    Time is where most modelling platforms quietly cut corners: a forecast that silently runs past what the model can represent, a state-space model that restarts from scratch at every score, a holdout scored without the history it depends on. modeling-api makes each of these explicit and checks them before any compute is spent.

    Horizons are part of the contract¶

    A time dim declares its policy extend and, optionally, a max_extension: how far past the fitted range a forecast may go without a refit. The control plane checks every score request against it from metadata alone:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://climate/mauna-loa-co2
mode: forecast
horizon: {dim: t, periods: 30}

    {"code": "HORIZON_EXCEEDS_FIT", "status": 409,
 "detail": "horizon of 30 exceeds the 24 periods this fit supports along 't'; a refit is required"}

    Models that need a bounded domain take it from the same place. ts.hsgp_trend sizes its Hilbert-space GP from ctx.anticipated_coords (the fitted labels plus max_extension future labels), so the basis it evaluates at a forecast month is exactly the basis it was fitted with:

    dom = HSGPDomain.from_positions(positions_of(ctx.anticipated_coords["t"]), s.c)
phi = hsgp_basis(dom.scale(ctx.position("t")), s.m, dom.L)   # same at fit and at score

    Score payloads must also continue the fitted range without gaps (NON_CONTIGUOUS_EXTENSION) and may not start before it (COORD_NOT_ALLOWED).

    Evaluate the way you will forecast¶

    With fit.mode: evaluate_then_refit (the default for promotable models) the platform fits on the training window, scores the holdout through the score path (with the context and state from training only), records the metrics, then refits on everything so forecasts start from the latest data. The production bundle links the evaluation bundle and its metrics.

    Sequential models keep a terminal state¶

    Hidden Markov models, Kalman filters and particle filters summarise the past in a state. They store it in a plugin-owned terminal_state group and read it at score time instead of replaying history. ts.gaussian_hmm stores the filtered state distribution at the last fitted step, alpha_T(chain, draw, animal, state):

    • forecast propagates it: P(z_{T+h}) = alpha_T · A^h, per posterior draw;
    • filter continues the forward filter over new observations and returns an updated terminal_state group, so filtering six new steps at once or in two batches of three gives the same state.
    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://ecology/animal-tracks-hmm
mode: filter
data: dataset://ecology/tracks_live@v1

    The result has predictions (one-step-ahead y_hat and filtered state_prob) and a terminal_state group with attrs.step set to the last filtered step.

    Missing values and sentinels¶

    Raw series often encode gaps as numbers (-99.99). Declare the code in the schema (missing_sentinel) and turn it into NaN with core.replace_sentinel; models that support gaps (such as ts.hsgp_trend) leave those periods out of the likelihood and still predict them.

    See Built-in models for every setting, and the Conformance corpus for the fixtures (c08, e24) that keep this behaviour tested.
