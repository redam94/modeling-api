    Validate models with governance policies v0.5¶

    A model that informs decisions should prove it learned something real. This guide shows how to say which checks a model must pass (a policy), run them on a model version (a validation), have a second person approve it, export the evidence pack for an auditor, and make endpoints refuse models that are not validated. The concepts and the full state machine are in Governance; every operation is in the HTTP API reference.

    Everything here can also be done in the web app: see In the web app. Why the null tests matter, in plain words and in statistics, is in Can you trust this model?. From Python, use api.governance and api.validations (client guide); from a terminal, mapi governance, mapi validate model, mapi validations and mapi approve (CLI guide):

    mapi governance checks                        # what a policy can require
mapi governance checks --about null.permutation_target --expert
mapi governance policies ls                   # in force here: tenant-wide first
mapi governance policies create forecast-policy.yaml
mapi validate model model://growth/wf-weekly-readiness-fit@v1   # runs the checks, waits
mapi validations ls --queue                   # reviewers: what waits for a decision
mapi approve "$VALIDATION" --comment "Null tests convincing; beats naive by 25%"
mapi validations pack "$VALIDATION" --out evidence.html          # for the auditor

    The examples below use curl with an API token (see Endpoints and webhooks for the api helper):

    api() { curl -sS -H "Authorization: Bearer $MAPI_TOKEN" -H "X-Mapi-Project: $MAPI_PROJECT" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' "$@"; }

    What the checks mean, in plain words¶

    Every check has an About card (GET /v1/catalog/governance-checks); the governance checks reference lists each one with its card, metric, default limits, requirements and parameters. In short:

    Check It asks How it is measured
    null.permutation_target Does the model beat versions of itself fitted on shuffled data? A model with drivers keeps its result and gets its drivers shuffled together in blocks of time, so trend, season and controls cannot pass on their own; a model without drivers (or one that ignores them) gets the period-to-period changes of its result shuffled and added up again from the same start (the plain values for a series that does not wander), which keeps how much it wanders but removes anything predictable. The model is refitted n_permutations times and scored on its held-out skill against the null forecast (the last value, or the mean), which does not depend on the level a shuffle lands on. The p-value is the share of shuffled fits that did as well as the real one, (1 + #as good) / (n + 1). With 20 shuffles the smallest possible p is 0.048, so use at least 19 for a 5% test.
    null.noise_regressors Does the model give fake drivers made of pure noise almost no credit? n_noise placebo drivers are added - a real driver shuffled in time, or Gaussian noise with its mean and spread - as new channels of a marketing-mix model or new variables of any other model, and the model is refitted. Placebos get the model's default prior: the real channels' priors and calibration (business priors, lift tests) are not copied. Each placebo's share of the real drivers' total effect is measured by switching it off and scoring again (of the result when the drivers' effect cannot be measured). A Bayesian model is flagged when a placebo's share is above the warn limit with at least 90% posterior probability - not when its interval misses zero, which a marketing mix model's positive-only effects always do; frequentist models' placebo p-values are judged with Holm's correction for the number of placebos.
    null.placebo_time_shift Do the effects vanish when the drivers are moved months away? The drivers are shifted (shifts, default ±26 periods, wrapped around) and the model refitted; the check reports the placebo effect as a share of the real one. A model that still finds most of the effect is picking up trend or season, not the drivers.
    baseline.beats_naive Is the model better than simple benchmarks on held-out periods? The train-only model is scored on the held-out periods next to "same as last period", "same as last season", the mean or the drift line, forecast from the training history. Skill is 1 - error(model) / error(best benchmark); MASE is reported too.
    residuals.whiteness Is there no pattern left in the errors? Ljung-Box on the in-sample residuals of every series (the smallest p-value counts; ARMA orders reduce the degrees of freedom).
    stability.rolling_params Are the key estimates stable over time? The model is refitted on rolling windows of history; the check reports the largest coefficient of variation of the key estimates across windows.
    plausibility.signs Do effects point the expected way? By default media and spend must raise the result (switch a channel off and the prediction must fall, per posterior draw for Bayesian models). List other expectations in expected, e.g. {"price": "negative"}.
    sensitivity.prior Bayesian models: are the answers driven by the data, not the assumptions? Every prior width the model uses - including the plugin's defaults that the settings leave out (for mmm.bayesian: the default ROI prior LogNormal(0, 1), seasonality.prior_sigma, trend.growth_prior_sigma, hierarchy.media_geo_sigma) and business priors translated at fit time - is multiplied by each of scales (sigma, scale, sd, tau under a prior… key, and keys named …prior_sigma or …_sigma; never Beta/Gamma beta or b parameters, which are not widths); the check reports the largest relative move of a key posterior mean.
    recovery.simulated Does the model find known answers? Data is simulated from the fit (its expected values plus block-bootstrapped residuals), the model is refitted n_sims times, and the check counts how often the 90% intervals contain the fitted values (target 90%, a parametric-bootstrap version of simulation-based calibration).
    convergence Did the fit finish properly? Bayesian: the largest split R-hat (divergences and effective sample size as evidence; any divergence makes a pass a warning). Others: the optimiser's converged flag.
    holdout.accuracy Is the error on unseen data below your limit? The fit's holdout metric (mape by default, as a fraction). For rmse or mae set thresholds in the KPI's units.
    data.quality Is the data good enough? The Data Explorer's quality warnings that are not just information: missing shares above max_missing, fewer than min_history_periods, constant drivers, negative spend, values outside declared bounds, duplicated periods, long gaps, collinear drivers.

    Every result carries the measured value, the threshold, a plain-language message_plain, a suggested_action when it did not pass, and evidence (tables, numbers) for the auditor.

    Model families¶

    Checks adapt to how a model is estimated (mapi_spec.governance.model_kind):

    • Bayesian (MCMC, VI, SMC): refits use a budget of at most 200 draws and 200 tuning steps on 2 chains (RefitBudget), intervals are highest-density intervals, and sensitivity.prior applies.
    • Frequentist (statsmodels, MAP, Kalman, ridge): refits run as configured; intervals are confidence intervals from standard errors; convergence reads the optimiser flag.
    • Machine learning (scikit-learn, xgboost, quantile and conformal models): no parameter intervals, so recovery.simulated and stability.rolling_params are skipped when the model has no coefficients; the null tests and benchmarks apply unchanged.

    A check that does not apply is reported as skipped with the reason (for example "the model has no test period (add a data split and refit)"); a check that could not be computed is error, which blocks validation when the check is blocking. A governance run refits at most max_refits times (120 by default).

    The default policies¶

    The first time a project uses governance the platform seeds three tenant-wide policies. They decide what a validation checks, but they do not gate promotion and do not require approval: an admin turns those on.

    Policy Families Blocking Warning Information
    Marketing mix models mmm data quality, convergence, holdout accuracy (MAPE ≤ 15% warn / 30% fail), shuffled results (20), fake drivers (3), effect signs beats benchmarks, time-shift placebo, prior sensitivity residual whiteness
    Forecasting models ts, forecast, ts_multivariate, regime data quality, convergence, beats benchmarks, holdout accuracy residual whiteness, shuffled results parameter stability
    Regression and machine learning tabular, choice, sklearn data quality, holdout accuracy, shuffled results fake drivers convergence

    The thresholds are the catalog defaults: shuffled-result p-value ≤ 0.05 passes (≤ 0.10 warns), fake-driver share ≤ 2% passes (≤ 5% warns), placebo effect ≤ 25% passes (≤ 50% warns), benchmark skill ≥ 0.05 passes (≥ 0 warns), Ljung-Box p ≥ 0.05 passes (≥ 0.01 warns), rolling variation ≤ 25% (≤ 50%), wrong-sign share ≤ 10% (≤ 25%), prior sensitivity ≤ 20% (≤ 50%), interval coverage within 10 points of 90% (25 points warns), R-hat ≤ 1.01 (≤ 1.05), holdout MAPE ≤ 15% (≤ 30%), and no data problems (up to 2 warns).

    Write a policy¶

    Project admins add policies to a project; tenant admins add tenant-wide ones, which every project inherits as a floor (a project can tighten a threshold, never loosen it):

    api -X POST "$API/v1/governance/policies" -d '{
  "name": "Forecasts we publish",
  "applies_to": {"families": ["ts"]},
  "checks": [
    {"check": "baseline.beats_naive"},
    {"check": "null.permutation_target", "params": {"n_permutations": 20}},
    {"check": "residuals.whiteness", "severity": "warning"},
    {"check": "holdout.accuracy", "thresholds": {"warn": 0.10, "fail": 0.20}}
  ],
  "require_approval": true,
  "approvals_required": 1,
  "require_validated_for_promotion": true
}'

    PUT …/policies/{policy_id} saves the next version (send expected_version to refuse a concurrent edit with 409); GET …/versions lists every version; DELETE retires the policy but keeps its versions for audit. GET /v1/governance/policies lists the policies in force in the project, tenant-wide first.

    Validate a model version¶

    api -X POST "$API/v1/models/$MODEL_ID/versions/7/validations" -d '{"note": "Q4 refresh"}'

    The answer (202) is a validation in checks_running with the checks it is held to and the governance run (workflow_run_id) that measures them in the sandbox. A validation is always held to every policy in force for the model (the tenant-wide floor and the project's own); policy_ids can only add more active policies on top of them, never leave one out. The version is locked while a request is checked, so two simultaneous requests (or a request and a workflow's submission) make one validation; the other gets 409 VALIDATION_TRANSITION_INVALID. Follow the run like any other (GET /v1/runs/{run_id}, or its event stream). When it finishes the validation moves on by itself:

    checks_running -> checks_passed -> validated              (no approval required)
checks_running -> checks_passed -> awaiting_approval      (approval required)
checks_running -> checks_failed                           (a blocking check failed or errored)

    GET /v1/validations/{validation_id} shows the status, every result and the history; GET …/evidence pages through the results with their evidence. After a failure (or a rejection) fix the model or the data and POST /v1/validations/{id}:resubmit: a new round starts under the policies in force now. :cancel stops running checks (back to draft).

    Approve as a second person¶

    When a policy requires approval, someone other than the submitter who holds the reviewer capability (or is a project admin) decides. Grant it on the membership:

    api -X PATCH "$API/v1/tenants/acme/projects/growth/members/$USER_ID" \
  -d '{"capabilities": ["reviewer"]}'

    Reviewers find their queue at GET /v1/validations?status=awaiting_approval and decide in a signed-in session (API tokens can never approve):

    curl -X POST "$API/v1/validations/$VALIDATION_ID/approvals" -d '{"decision": "approve",
  "comment": "Null tests are convincing; benchmarks beaten by 30%."}'

    The submitter is refused (403 SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN); each reviewer decides once per round; approvals_required approvals validate the version; one rejection (which needs a comment) rejects it. A validated version that should no longer be used is retired with :deprecate and a reason.

    Promotion requires validation¶

    When any policy in force says require_validated_for_promotion, promoting a model version to an endpoint needs its latest validation to be validated and to have covered every such policy as it stands now: either it names the policy at its current version, or it was held to each of the policy's checks at least as strictly (severity, limits and settings) and had the reviewers the policy asks for. A version validated before a gating policy was added or made stricter is reported as validated under other or older policies; request a new validation (allowed in that case). Anything else is refused with 409 NOT_VALIDATED, and no administrator override changes that; the refusal is audited as endpoint.promote_not_validated.

    Export the evidence pack¶

    api -X POST "$API/v1/validations/$VALIDATION_ID/evidence-pack" -d '{"format": "html"}'

    The answer is the pack as JSON (format: mapi.evidence-pack/v1): the validation with every result, its evidence, approvals and history, the policies at the versions it was held to, the model's lineage and sanity scorecard, and a digest (sha256 of the canonical content). With format: html it also carries download, a signed link to a static, self-contained HTML rendering: inline styles only, no scripts, no external assets (a Content-Security-Policy forbids them), every value escaped. Exports are audited (model.evidence_export).

    In the web app¶

    Governance in the sidebar (G then V) has three tabs: In force here (the tenant-wide policies first, then the project's own, with what each covers, its number of checks, whether it needs a reviewer and whether it gates promotion), Tenant-wide and the Check catalog, where every check has its About card, what it measures and its default limits.

    Editing a policy¶

    Open a policy (project admins edit project policies, tenant admins tenant-wide ones; everyone else sees them read-only) or press New policy:

    • Covers: model families, model ids and Guided templates; empty lists match every model.
    • Required checks: add a check from the catalog - its About card shows before you add it
    • then set its severity (Blocking, Warning, Information), switch on Custom limits to set its warn and fail values (the page refuses a fail limit that is looser than the warn limit, as the server does) and adjust its settings, a form generated from the check's parameters (for example the number of shuffles).
    • Sign-off and promotion: Require approval by a reviewer (and how many), Only validated models can be promoted to an endpoint, and Active.

    Save version N stores the next version; a concurrent edit is refused and kept on screen. Versions lists every version and compares two side by side. Retire policy keeps its versions for audit.

    Validating a model version¶

    A model version's page has a Validation button. It lists the version's validations and requests a new one: the policies it is held to (every policy in force always applies; ticking others adds them), optionally the data to check against, and a note. The validation page then shows:

    • Status: where the validation is on its path (draft → checks running → checks passed → awaiting approval → validated, or checks failed, rejected, deprecated) and the history of every transition with who made it and their note.
    • Checks: failures first, each with its plain-language message, What to do when it did not pass, the measured value against its limit and its evidence: small tables, numbers and, for the shuffled-result test, the distribution of the shuffled fits' errors with the real model's error marked (drawn in the plot sandbox).
    • Approvals, Evidence pack and Held to (the policy versions).

    Depending on its state the page offers Cancel checks, Resubmit (after failed checks or a rejection) and Deprecate (with a reason, for a validated version; project admins). Export evidence pack saves the readable HTML page or the JSON as a file; the HTML is never opened inside the app.

    Reviewing¶

    Reviewers find Review queue on the Governance page (with the number waiting), or Validations filtered by Awaiting approval. The approval form appears only to members who may approve: project admins, and editors or runners who hold the reviewer capability. Rejecting needs a comment. On your own submission the form says that a second person has to review it and its buttons are disabled; the server refuses it anyway (SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN).

    Project admins grant and revoke the capability in the Reviewer column of Members (admins hold it implicitly; viewers cannot hold it).

    Promotion in the app¶

    When a policy gates promotion, promoting a version that is not validated from Integrations is refused with A governance policy requires a validated model, the version's current validation status, and links to its validation (or to request one). No override is offered: none passes this rule.

    Audit and webhooks¶

    Every policy change (policy.create, policy.update, policy.delete), request (model.validation_request), transition (model.validation_transition, with from, to and the actor), decision (model.approve, model.reject) and deprecation is in the audit log. Subscribe webhooks to model.validation.checks_passed, model.validation.checks_failed, model.validation.awaiting_approval, model.validated, model.rejected and model.deprecated to drive review queues and release pipelines.
