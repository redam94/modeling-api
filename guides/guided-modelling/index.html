    Guided modelling: priors, lift tests and the scorecard v0.4¶

    This guide explains what happens behind the Guided-mode tasks of Guided mode when the model is a marketing-mix model (mmm.hierarchical_geo): how beliefs in business units become model-space priors, how experiment results calibrate a fit, how the sanity scorecard is measured, and which views the what-if sliders, forecasts and counterfactuals read. Everything that touches data runs in the sandbox; the control plane only validates JSON.

    Task Where it runs What it writes
    Business priors → model-space priors a translate_priors run priors.json (PriorTranslationResult), plots.json
    Lift-test calibration inside the fit the posterior (lift_tests__model), learned.calibration
    Sanity scorecard at the end of the fit scorecard.json (Scorecard), stored on the model version
    What-if sliders at the end of the fit ui/grids/response_curves.json (ResponseGridView)
    Forecast a forecast score run ui/guided/forecast.json (ForecastView) and a fan chart with the split line
    Counterfactual a counterfactual score run ui/guided/counterfactual.json (CounterfactualView)

    Business priors¶

    A business prior states a belief about one channel in the units people think in:

    calibration:
  business_priors:
    channels:
      tv:     {roi: [1.5, 4.0], credibility: 0.8}           # "80% sure each euro returns 1.5 to 4"
      search: {contribution_share: [0.05, 0.15], credibility: 0.9}
    baseline_share: {share: [0.5, 0.8], credibility: 0.8}   # KPI without any media

    From beliefs to priors: the math¶

    The model's contribution of channel c over the fit window, in KPI units, is

    C[c] = sum over geos g and periods t of  kpi_scale[g, t] * beta[g, c] * S[g, c, t]

    where kpi_scale converts model units to KPI units (the pipeline's fitted scaling, read through its inverse), beta[g, c] is the channel effect in geo g and S the carried-over, saturated spend, a function of the adstock and saturation parameters. The two business quantities are

    ROI[c]   = C[c] / spend[c]       spend[c]: the channel's total spend over the window
share[c] = C[c] / Y              Y:        the observed KPI total over the window

    The translation gives the median channel effect a log-normal prior, beta_channel[c] = exp(mu[c] + sigma[c] * eps[c]). With partial pooling beta[g, c] = beta_channel[c] * exp(beta_sigma[c] * z[g, c]), so

    log ROI[c] = mu[c] + sigma[c] * eps[c] + log K[c] - log spend[c]
K[c]       = sum over g, t of  kpi_scale[g, t] * exp(beta_sigma[c] * z[g, c]) * S[g, c, t]

    (Without pooling every geo draws its own effect and the log-sum runs over geos; with complete pooling z = 0.) Two facts make the solve exact up to Monte Carlo error:

    • mu[c] only shifts log ROI[c]: every term is proportional to exp(mu[c]);
    • K[c] depends only on the other priors (adstock, saturation, pooling spread), which are independent of beta_channel, so it is sampled once, with common random numbers.

    The stated range [L, H] held with credibility p fixes the q = (1 - p) / 2 and 1 - q quantiles of log ROI[c]. Write R(sigma) for log ROI[c] with mu[c] = 0. Then

    1. sigma[c] solves Q[1 - q](R(sigma)) - Q[q](R(sigma)) = log(H / L) by bisection, vectorised over channels (the interval widens with sigma);
    2. mu[c] = (log L + log H) / 2 - (Q[q] + Q[1 - q])(R(sigma[c])) / 2 centres it.

    A share range works the same way with Y in place of spend[c]. Edge cases are reported as issues, never hidden:

    • if the other priors alone already make the interval wider than stated, sigma[c] stops at its floor (0.02) and a warning says so - the reported coverage shows by how much the range is missed;
    • a range starting at 0 is read as [H / 20, H] (a log-normal has no mass at zero);
    • a channel the data does not have, or a ROI for a channel without spend, is an error.

    Channels without a belief keep their prior, refitted as the log-normal with the same 5% and 95% quantiles, so beta_channel stays one per-channel log-normal.

    The baseline share (intercepts plus controls) is linear in the population mean of the geo intercepts m: B = a * (m + s * eps) + E, so the same two-step solve runs in linear space and gives intercept: {distribution: Normal, mu: m, sigma: s}. When the geo spread and the control effects alone are wider than the stated range, their priors (intercept_sigma, gamma_control) are narrowed by a common factor first, and an info issue says by how much.

    Amounts only translate through linear preprocessing (mean_scale, max_abs_scale, currency conversion). A log1p or standardize step on the KPI or spend refuses the translation with CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED.

    The prior-translation run¶

    Ask for a translation while drafting - from an unsaved document or a saved revision:

    curl -X POST "$MAPI/v1/guided/prior-translations" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $MAPI_TOKEN" -H "X-Mapi-Project: acme/growth" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"spec_id": "spc_…", "revision": 2, "draws": 500,
       "business_priors": {"channels": {"tv": {"roi": [1.5, 4.0], "credibility": 0.8}}}}'

    The API checks the beliefs statically first (channels against the dataset's levels, shares that cannot add up, BUSINESS_PRIORS_INVALID; a model without the business_priors capability, CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED) and answers 202 with the queued run. The run fits the pipeline's preprocessing on all of the data (the scales a production refit learns), solves, and samples the translated priors afresh for the preview:

    GET /v1/guided/prior-translations/{run_id} returns, once done, the PriorTranslationResult:

    • priors: the model-space priors in the settings.priors form, to write at apply_at (/model/settings/priors; mapi_spec.calibration.apply_prior_translation does it and records the run in calibration.prior_translation_run);
    • preview: ROI and contribution-share distributions per channel, the baseline share and a KPI fan in business units against the observed KPI;
    • coverage: the prior mass inside each stated range - close to the stated credibility when the translation is faithful;
    • issues: everything above in plain words, plus lift tests whose implied ROI falls outside the prior's 90% range.

    The run's plots (GET /v1/runs/{run_id}/plots) are mmm.prior_beliefs (each channel's prior interval over the range you stated) and core.prior_predictive (the KPI the priors can generate, against your data).

    A spec that carries business_priors without a recorded translation run is translated at fit time instead, with the same solve over the training data's scales; the priors used are in the model's learned.calibration.translated_priors.

    Business priors for mmm.pymc_marketing¶

    The pymc-marketing adapter takes business priors too. pymc-marketing scales the KPI by its maximum (target_scale) and each channel by its maximum, and every curve it offers here is beta * g(adstock(x)) (or adstock(beta * g(x)) with adstock_first: false), beta being its saturation_beta prior over (*dims, channel). A channel's contribution in KPI units is therefore

    C_c = target_scale * sum over panel cells k of beta[k, c] * A[k, c],
A[k, c] = sum over periods t of g(...)[t, k, c]

    and A depends on the adstock and curve priors only. The translation draws A from pymc-marketing's own model - prior draws of channel_contribution / beta - so its scaling, carry-over padding and curves are exactly the fit's, and then solves as above for a log-normal saturation_beta with one mu and sigma per channel (drawn independently per panel cell, as pymc-marketing draws it): a bisection on sigma matches the range's width at its credibility, mu centres it. The baseline share is linear in the intercept (intercept ~ Normal(m, s) per cell, B = a (m + s zbar) + E with a = target_scale * T * cells / Y and E the controls' and seasonality's share). pymc-marketing's default seasonality prior (Laplace(0, 1) on the scaled KPI) is wide, so when E alone spreads the baseline share over more than 80% of the range, the gamma_control and gamma_fourier priors are narrowed by a common factor first - their contributions are linear in them, so the spread shrinks by the same factor - and an info issue says by how much.

    The priors land in the adapter's settings.priors (saturation_beta, intercept, gamma_control, gamma_fourier, in pymc-marketing's scaled units), which it hands to pymc-marketing as model_config priors; the preview, coverage and plots are the same as for the geo MMM.

    mmm.bayesinsight: no business priors¶

    The BayesInsight model's coefficient priors are already statements in the KPI's own units (coeff_prior in model_def.json), so it does not declare business priors: state the ROI you believe directly as the media coefficient's prior. It does take lift tests (below).

    Lift tests¶

    A lift test is calibration data: extra spend on one channel (optionally in one geo) during a window, and the incremental KPI it caused, with its uncertainty:

    calibration:
  lift_tests:
    - id: tv-holdout-2026q2
      channel: tv
      geo: DE
      start: 2026-04-06
      end: 2026-05-03
      spend_delta: -120000      # TV off: the spend removed
      kpi_delta: -260000        # the revenue lost
      ci: [-380000, -140000]    # or se: 73000
      ci_level: 0.9

    The fit adds one observation per test,

    kpi_delta ~ Normal(lift, se)
lift      = sum over the test's geos g and periods t in W of
            kpi_scale[g, t] * beta[g, c] * (S[g, c, t](x + dx) - S[g, c, t](x))

    where W are the model periods whose label lies in the window and dx is the extra spend in model units, spread over the window's cells in proportion to the channel's actual spend there (evenly when there was none) and carried over through the lag tensor within the window. Carry-over after the window is not counted: tests report the lift measured during the test. (pymc-marketing's add_lift_test_measurements uses the saturation curve alone; this version also carries the extra spend over.) The model's implied lift per draw is kept as lift_tests__model(lift_test); the KPI likelihood alone stays in log_likelihood, so elpd_loo is unchanged in meaning.

    Tests are checked three times, each time where the information is:

    1. at submit, against the dataset version's summary: the channel and geo exist and the window lies inside the data (SCHEMA_INVALID at /calibration/lift_tests/<i>/…);
    2. in the sandbox, before fitting, against the raw data itself;
    3. at the model stage: the window must cover at least one model period, and a holdout (negative spend_delta) may remove at most the spend the data shows.

    During evaluate_then_refit, a test whose window lies after the training periods, or that the end of the training periods cuts, is skipped by the evaluation fit (logged) and used by the production refit.

    mmm.pymc_marketing accepts lift tests too, through pymc-marketing's own add_lift_test_measurements: each test becomes rows of its window's average spend, extra spend, lift and sigma per period, conditioned on the saturation curve (a Gamma on the absolute lift, pymc-marketing's model). With panel dims, a test must name its geo.

    mmm.bayesinsight observes each test under its own likelihood: channel names a media variable (its analytic name, TV_Total_Total_Total, or its model-stage name), geo a label of the first panel dim (the test then covers every level of the other panel dims there), and

    lift = sum over the test's cells p and periods t in W of
       mean(eta[p, t] + d_eta[p, t]) - mean(eta[p, t]),
d_eta[p, t] = coef[p] * sum over lags l of w[l] * (f(z + dz)[p, t, l] - f(z)[p, t, l])

    with mean the likelihood's mean function (the identity for a Normal KPI, exp and the zero-inflation share for counts, exp(. + sigma^2 / 2) for a log-normal), f the media curve and z the indexed media - so the extra spend goes through BayesInsight's curve, then its carry-over, then the link. The extra spend is spread and carried over within the window as above; the implied lift is kept as lift_tests__model.

    The sanity scorecard¶

    At the end of every fit the runner measures each check of the catalog it can answer and writes scorecard.json; the finalizer stores it on the model version (GET /v1/models/{id}/versions/{v} → scorecard) and the promotion gate reads it. A guided spec (metadata.labels.guided: <template>) runs its template's checks with its thresholds; any other spec runs the whole catalog.

    Metric Measured on
    r_hat_max, ess_bulk_min the production posterior's free parameters (the plugin's convergence diagnostics)
    divergence_rate sample_stats.diverging, as a share of all draws
    mape, coverage_90 the holdout, through the score path (needs them in evaluate.metrics). metrics.json reports mape in percent (1.4 is 1.4%); the check compares it as a fraction (0.014 against 0.10/0.25)
    accuracy_lift choice models: the holdout hit rate (the most likely product is the one bought) minus that of always guessing the holdout's most common product (accuracy, accuracy_naive; the customer_choice template evaluates them)
    negative_roi_share, roi_max the response_curves grid at the actual spend: per channel the share of draws with a negative return, and the largest posterior-mean ROI
    max_channel_share, baseline_share a channel's (or the baseline's) share of baseline + every channel, posterior mean
    prior_overlap_max the overlap of prior and posterior draws of the model's media parameters (PyMCModel.prior_overlap_vars, forward-sampled at fit time; cheap)
    missing_fraction the raw dataset's numeric variables
    history_years the model stage's time dim, in years: its periods over the periods in a year at the dim's frequency (or the one its dates follow), so 105 days and 15 weeks both count as about 0.3 years
    spend_cv_min the raw spend per channel over time

    A check that cannot be measured - no holdout split, no response curves, a non-MCMC model - is listed in skipped with the reason. Every item carries message_plain, message_expert (what was measured, on which parameter or channel), a suggested_action when it is not a pass and a docs_anchor back to the catalog.

    What-if grids and "Run exact"¶

    Fits with response_curves write ui/grids/response_curves.json: per thinned draw (up to outputs.ui_draws, at most 500), channel and spend multiplier, the channel's total contribution over the fit window in KPI units, plus the spend at every grid point, the actual spend, the baseline (intercepts and controls) and the per-geo grids when they fit the size cap. The sliders interpolate it per draw (the contract).

    "Run exact" compiles the scenario into a counterfactual score request with one multiply intervention per changed channel. At grid points the two agree per draw to float precision: the grid is computed from the same fitted curves, and linear preprocessing passes the multiplier through unchanged (packages/runner/tests/test_guided.py checks it).

    Future covariates¶

    A forecast needs the future values of its covariates - spend, prices. Guided forecasts do not ask for an upload: ScoreRequest.future_covariates says how the runner extends the ones the payload lacks, per series, from the fitted history (the server passes the model's fit dataset to the score run as its history input):

    Policy Future values
    supplied (default for API callers) from the request's data or scenario; missing ones are MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE
    carry_last (default in Guided mode) the last observed value of each series
    seasonal_naive the value one year earlier by date (52 weeks, 12 months, 365 days...), so the last observed year repeats even after a gap; a missing value steps back another year, and one missing in every year (or a history shorter than a year) falls back to carry_last with a warning
    zero zero - "no spend planned"

    Values the payload does supply are never replaced, and interventions apply after the fill, so budget × k over a horizon scales the carried-forward spend. The score result's issues name what was filled.

    Forecast and counterfactual views¶

    A forecast score run writes ui/guided/forecast.json (mapi.forecast/v1): the actual KPI over the fitted history (fan.observed), then the forecast quantiles after split_at (fan.values), summed over geos per draw before the quantiles are taken, and the KPI total over the horizon. Its core.ppc_fan plot shows the history and the dashed split line.

    A counterfactual score run - for example a preset from mapi_spec.whatif.counterfactual_request such as channel off or budget × k - writes ui/guided/counterfactual.json (mapi.counterfactual/v1): the scenario, baseline and change totals, the change as a share of the baseline and a per-period band of the change. Scenario and baseline share their noise (common random numbers), so the change is precise even when the totals are uncertain.

    Both views exist only for models whose outputs are posterior draws; other representations cannot be summed across geos.
