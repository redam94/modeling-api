    Guided modelling: priors, lift tests and the scorecard v0.4¶

    This guide tells what occurs behind the Guided-mode tasks of Guided mode when the model is a marketing-mix model (mmm.bayesian, on mmm-framework). It tells these things:

    • How beliefs in business units become priors in model space.
    • How experiment results calibrate a fit.
    • How the platform measures the sanity scorecard.
    • Which views the what-if sliders, forecasts and counterfactuals read.

    All the tasks that touch data run in the sandbox. The control plane only validates JSON.

    Task Where it runs What it writes
    Business priors → model-space priors a translate_priors run priors.json (PriorTranslationResult), plots.json
    Lift-test calibration in the fit mmm-framework experiment likelihoods, learned.calibration
    Sanity scorecard at the end of the fit scorecard.json (Scorecard), stored on the model version
    What-if sliders at the end of the fit ui/grids/response_curves.json (ResponseGridView)
    Forecast a forecast score run ui/guided/forecast.json (ForecastView) and a fan chart with the split line
    Counterfactual a counterfactual score run ui/guided/counterfactual.json (CounterfactualView)

    The marketing-mix templates v0.12¶

    Three Guided templates make a marketing-mix model. Select the template that agrees with your data:

    Template Model Your data Tasks
    geo_mmm - Geo marketing mix mmm.bayesian The result, spend for each channel and optional controls, for each market what-if, forecast, counterfactual, business priors, lift tests
    mediated_mmm - Marketing mix with an intermediate measure mmm.nested One national result, spend for each channel and one or more intermediate measures (visits, searches, awareness) what-if, forecast, counterfactual, lift tests
    multi_kpi_mmm - Marketing mix for related products mmm.multivariate Two or more national results (one column for each) and spend for each channel what-if, forecast, counterfactual

    mediated_mmm asks for the result, the date, the spend columns and the intermediate measures. The measures become one variable along the mediator dim. A measure can have empty periods, for example a monthly survey. Role detection also suggests columns that are mostly empty for this question. The labels keep the words of each column (site_visits). The weekly resample takes the mean of each measure in a week and keeps a week without a value empty. The report shows mmm.mediation: the direct and the indirect effect of each channel.

    multi_kpi_mmm asks for two or more result columns. They become y along the outcome dim (sales_premium labels premium). Use the same units in each column. The what-if sliders and the budget plan use the total of the results. The report shows mmm.waterfall for each result. The template does not set effects between the results, because the answers do not tell which result acts on which. To add them, open the spec in the editor and set cross_effects of mmm.multivariate (then add the derived quantity cross_effects and the chart mmm.cross_effects). These effects are associations, not causes.

    The two templates fit national data only. They take no control variables, and they do not have business priors. All three templates keep adstock and show mmm.roi. They use the same scorecard checks.

    The causal templates v0.13¶

    Two Guided templates measure the effect of a change. They fit once on all periods (train_only), because the effect is the result of the fit:

    Template Model Your data Tasks
    campaign_impact - Effect of a campaign causal.impact One result over time, optional control columns, the start date and the optional end date of the change —
    geo_lift - Geo-lift test causal.synthetic_control The result for each market over time, the names of the test markets, the start and end dates of the test — (the report has a lift-test card)

    campaign_impact asks for the result, the date, the control columns, the start date and the end date. The control columns are optional. Each control column becomes its own variable, and the spec lists them in controls. Leave the end date empty if the change did not stop. The template adds a yearly pattern (3 harmonics) for weekly data, and a weekly pattern for daily data.

    geo_lift asks for the result, the date, the market column, the test markets, the start date and the end date. Type the test markets as text, with commas between the names. The draft has the list of the names in treated. The other markets make the synthetic control.

    The two templates use the scorecard checks of convergence and of missing values. The report shows causal.impact first. The templates have no tasks, thus their steps do not include the scenarios step: Next on the results goes to Share. The share step offers the causal charts for embedding. See Measure the effect of a change.

    Business priors¶

    A business prior gives a belief about one channel, in the units that persons use:

    calibration:
  business_priors:
    channels:
      tv:     {roi: [1.5, 4.0], credibility: 0.8}           # "80% sure each euro returns 1.5 to 4"
      search: {contribution_share: [0.05, 0.15], credibility: 0.9}
    baseline_share: {share: [0.5, 0.8], credibility: 0.8}   # KPI without any media

    From beliefs to priors: the math¶

    mmm.bayesian gives the effect of each channel as its return on spend. A channel with an ROI prior samples:

    roi[c]  ~ LogNormal(mu[c], sigma[c])
beta[c] = roi[c] * spend[c] / (y_std * sum over observations of S[c](x))

    In this equation, S[c] is the carried-over, saturated spend of the channel. This is the adstock and saturation of mmm-framework, with the same parameters. y_std is the scale of the KPI. On the observed data, the contribution of the channel over the fit window, divided by its spend, is roi[c]. This is the quantity that a business ROI belief is about. Thus the translation has a closed form:

    • An ROI range [L, H] with credibility p is the log-normal that has this range as its central p interval. Thus mu = (log L + log H) / 2 and sigma = (log H - log L) / (2 z). Here, z is the (1 + p) / 2 quantile of the standard normal.
    • A contribution-share range [l, h] is the ROI range [l Y / spend[c], h Y / spend[c]]. The reason is that share[c] = roi[c] * spend[c] / Y. Here, Y is the observed KPI total over the window.
    • The fitted factors of the pipeline change business units to model units. The model does its own scaling, thus these factors are usually 1.

    The platform shows edge cases as issues. It never hides them:

    • A range that starts at 0 becomes [H / 20, H], because a log-normal has no mass at zero.
    • A range can be narrower or wider than a prior can usefully give. Then the platform clamps its spread to [0.02, 4] on the log scale. It gives a warning.
    • A channel that the data does not have is an error. A share for a channel without spend is also an error.

    Channels without a belief keep their prior (priors.roi_default, or the default of mmm-framework). The platform writes the result as priors.roi.<channel> = {mu, sigma}.

    The baseline share is the KPI that occurs without media: intercept, trend, seasonality, panel levels and controls. It is linear in the intercept Normal(m, s), in KPI standard deviations: B = a (m + s eps) + E. Here, a = n y_std / Y, and E is the rest of the baseline, drawn from the prior of the model in mmm-framework. The platform uses bisection to find s, so that the central interval of B has the given width. Then m puts the interval at the center. If the rest alone is already wider than the given range, s stops at its minimum and a warning tells this.

    The result is priors.intercept = {mu, sigma}.

    The preview then samples the prior predictive of the translated model. It uses the graph of mmm-framework, with a different seed. The preview gives ROI, contribution share, baseline share and a KPI fan in business units. It also gives the prior mass in each given range (coverage).

    Amounts translate only through linear preprocessing (currency conversion). The model does its own scaling. If a non-linear step applies to the KPI or the spend, the platform refuses the translation with CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED.

    The prior-translation run¶

    You can ask for a translation while you write a draft. Use an unsaved document or a saved revision:

    curl -X POST "$MAPI/v1/guided/prior-translations" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $MAPI_TOKEN" -H "X-Mapi-Project: acme/growth" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"spec_id": "spc_…", "revision": 2, "draws": 500,
       "business_priors": {"channels": {"tv": {"roi": [1.5, 4.0], "credibility": 0.8}}}}'

    First, the API checks the beliefs statically:

    • It checks the channels against the levels of the dataset, and finds shares that cannot add up (BUSINESS_PRIORS_INVALID).
    • It finds a model without the business_priors capability (CALIBRATION_UNSUPPORTED).

    Then it answers 202 with the queued run. The run fits the preprocessing of the pipeline on all the data. These are the scales that a production refit learns. The run solves the equations. Then it samples the translated priors again for the preview.

    When the run is done, GET /v1/guided/prior-translations/{run_id} gives the PriorTranslationResult:

    • priors: The model-space priors in the settings.priors form, to write at apply_at (/model/settings/priors). mapi_spec.calibration.apply_prior_translation writes them. It also records the run in calibration.prior_translation_run.
    • preview: The distributions of ROI and contribution share for each channel, and the baseline share. It also has a KPI fan in business units against the observed KPI.
    • coverage: The prior mass in each given range. If the translation is correct, this value is near the given credibility.
    • issues: All the items above in plain words. Also, the lift tests with an implied ROI that is outside the 90% range of the prior.

    The plots of the run (GET /v1/runs/{run_id}/plots) are these:

    • mmm.prior_beliefs: The prior interval of each channel over the range that you gave.
    • core.prior_predictive: The KPI that the priors can make, against your data.

    A spec can have business_priors without a recorded translation run. In this case, the platform translates the priors at fit time. It uses the same solve over the scales of the training data. The priors that the fit used are in learned.calibration.translated_priors of the model.

    Lift tests¶

    A lift test is calibration data. It gives the extra spend on one channel during a window, optionally in one geo. It also gives the incremental KPI that the spend caused, with its uncertainty:

    calibration:
  lift_tests:
    - id: tv-holdout-2026q2
      channel: tv
      geo: DE
      start: 2026-04-06
      end: 2026-05-03
      spend_delta: -120000      # TV off: the spend removed
      kpi_delta: -260000        # the revenue lost
      ci: [-380000, -140000]    # or se: 73000
      ci_level: 0.9

    The fit adds each test to mmm-framework as an experiment likelihood (BayesianMMM.add_experiment_calibration). This is a likelihood term on the estimand that the model implies. It updates the effect of the channel, its saturation curve and its adstock kernel together:

    The test mmm-framework measurement
    extra spend (spend_delta > 0) Marginal ROAS: the lift when the spend of the window increases by 100 x spend_delta / window spend percent, for each extra unit of spend, against kpi_delta / spend_delta
    a holdout that removes all (or almost all) of the spend of the channel in the window The contribution of the channel over the window, against -kpi_delta
    a partial cut (less than a full holdout) Skipped, with the reason. A partial cut measures the return below the current spend. mmm-framework can calibrate only on the return above the current spend (an increase) or on a full holdout. With diminishing returns, the two are different. Thus, if the platform used one for the other, the curve would have a bias

    geo limits the measurement to the observations of that geo. se is the standard error of the measurement. If you give ci, the standard error is the half-width of ci divided by the normal quantile. The fit does not count carry-over after the window, because tests give the lift measured during the test. Only the KPI likelihood stays in log_likelihood. Thus the meaning of elpd_loo does not change. learned.calibration.lift_tests tells which tests the fit used or skipped, and why.

    The platform checks tests three times. Each check occurs where the information is:

    1. At submit, against the summary of the dataset version. The channel and the geo must exist, and the window must be in the data (SCHEMA_INVALID at /calibration/lift_tests/<i>/…).
    2. In the sandbox, before the fit, against the raw data.
    3. At the model stage. The window must include a minimum of one model period. A holdout (negative spend_delta) can remove a maximum of the spend that the data shows. The time labels must be dates.

    During evaluate_then_refit, the evaluation fit skips some tests and logs them. These are tests with a window after the training periods, or a window that the end of the training periods cuts. The production refit uses these tests.

    A lift test from a geo-lift fit v0.13¶

    The geo_lift template fits causal.synthetic_control on the markets of a test. Its report has the card Lift test for your marketing mix. Type the channel and the spend change of the test. The card then shows the YAML entry for calibration.lift_tests: kpi_delta is the mean of the total effect, and se is its sd. With one test market, the entry has the geo of that market. With two or more test markets, the entry is national. The card uses the test markets of the revision that the last fit used, also when you changed them after that fit. Copy the entry into the spec of the marketing mix model. The workflow template geo_lift_mmm does the same steps in one run. See Measure the effect of a change.

    The sanity scorecard¶

    At the end of each fit, the runner measures each check of the catalog that it can answer. It writes scorecard.json. The finalizer stores the scorecard on the model version (GET /v1/models/{id}/versions/{v} → scorecard). The promotion gate reads it. A guided spec (metadata.labels.guided: <template>) runs the checks of its template with its thresholds. All other specs run the full catalog.

    Metric Measured on
    r_hat_max, ess_bulk_min The free parameters of the production posterior (the convergence diagnostics of the plugin)
    divergence_rate sample_stats.diverging, as a share of all draws
    mape, coverage_90 The holdout, through the score path. They must be in evaluate.metrics. metrics.json gives mape in percent (1.4 is 1.4%). The check compares it as a fraction (0.014 against 0.10/0.25)
    accuracy_lift Choice models: the holdout hit rate minus the hit rate of a guess that is always the most common product of the holdout. A hit is when the most probable product is the product bought (accuracy, accuracy_naive). The customer_choice template evaluates them
    negative_roi_share, roi_max The response_curves grid at the actual spend. For each channel: the share of draws with a negative return, and the largest posterior-mean ROI
    max_channel_share, baseline_share The share of a channel (or of the baseline) in the baseline plus all channels, posterior mean
    prior_overlap_max The overlap of the prior and posterior draws of the media parameters of the model (PyMCModel.prior_overlap_vars, forward-sampled at fit time, at a low cost)
    missing_fraction The numeric variables of the raw dataset that contain observations. The check does not count the variables with the role constant, index or mask (for example the padding of the outlines of a map). It also does not count the variables that the model declares in structural_missing (for example the cells without a sensor of sci.heat_plate). The expert message names the variables that the check does not count
    history_years The time dim of the model stage, in years. This is its periods divided by the periods in a year at the frequency of the dim (or the frequency of its dates). Thus 105 days and 15 weeks both count as about 0.3 years
    spend_cv_min The raw spend for each channel over time

    If the runner cannot measure a check, the check is in skipped with the reason. Examples are no holdout split, no response curves, or a model that is not MCMC. Each item has these fields:

    • message_plain.
    • message_expert: What the runner measured, and on which parameter or channel.
    • suggested_action, if the item is not a pass.
    • docs_anchor: A link back to the catalog.

    What-if grids and "Run exact"¶

    Fits with response_curves write ui/grids/response_curves.json. For each thinned draw, channel and spend multiplier, the file has the total contribution of the channel over the fit window, in KPI units. The number of draws is a maximum of outputs.ui_draws, and never more than 500. The file also has these items:

    • The spend at each grid point.
    • The actual spend.
    • The baseline (intercepts and controls).
    • The grids for each geo, if they are in the size cap.

    The sliders interpolate the grid for each draw (the contract).

    "Run exact" compiles the scenario into a counterfactual score request. The request has one multiply intervention for each changed channel. At grid points, the two results agree for each draw to float precision. The reason is that the grid comes from the same fitted curves. Also, linear preprocessing does not change the multiplier. packages/runner/tests/test_guided.py checks this.

    Future covariates¶

    A forecast needs the future values of its covariates, for example spend and prices. Guided forecasts do not ask for an upload. ScoreRequest.future_covariates tells how the runner extends the covariates that the payload does not have. It does this for each series, from the fitted history. The server gives the fit dataset of the model to the score run as its history input:

    Policy Future values
    supplied (default for API callers) From the data or the scenario of the request. Missing values get MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE
    carry_last (default in Guided mode) The last observed value of each series
    seasonal_naive The value one year before, by date (52 weeks, 12 months, 365 days, and so on). Thus the last observed year repeats, also after a gap. If a value is missing, the runner goes back one more year. If the value is missing in all years, or the history is shorter than one year, the runner uses carry_last with a warning
    zero Zero: "no spend planned"

    The runner never replaces values that the payload gives. Interventions apply after the fill. Thus budget × k over a horizon scales the carried-forward spend. The issues of the score result tell which values the runner filled.

    Forecast and counterfactual views¶

    A forecast score run writes ui/guided/forecast.json (mapi.forecast/v1). The file has these items:

    • The actual KPI over the fitted history (fan.observed).
    • The forecast quantiles after split_at (fan.values). The run adds the geos together for each draw before it calculates the quantiles.
    • The KPI total over the horizon.

    Its core.ppc_fan plot shows the history and the dashed split line.

    A counterfactual score run writes ui/guided/counterfactual.json (mapi.counterfactual/v1). An example is a preset from mapi_spec.whatif.counterfactual_request, such as channel off or budget × k. The file has these items:

    • The totals of the scenario, the baseline and the change.
    • The change as a share of the baseline.
    • A band of the change for each period.

    The scenario and the baseline share their noise (common random numbers). Thus the change is precise, also when the totals are uncertain.

    The two views exist only for models with outputs that are posterior draws. The platform cannot add other representations across geos.
