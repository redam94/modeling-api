    Marketing-mix models¶

    mapi-plugin-mmm contains the marketing-mix models (MMMs) of the platform. The four models use mmm-framework:

    Plugin What it is Use it when
    mmm.bayesian The Bayesian MMM of mmm-framework, national or hierarchical over geos and products You want the contribution and the return of each channel with honest ranges. You also want business priors and lift tests, what-if answers and budget plans
    mmm.frequentist The ridge estimator of mmm-framework with bootstrap intervals You need a first result in seconds, or you want to compare many set-ups quickly
    mmm.nested v0.12 The nested (mediated) model of mmm-framework, national Your channels act through an intermediate measure, such as visits or awareness, and you want to know how much of each effect goes through it
    mmm.multivariate v0.12 The multivariate-outcome model of mmm-framework, national One budget acts on two or more results, such as the sales of related products

    The plugins register through the mapi.models entry point. Thus a spec only gives their names: use: mmm.bayesian@^1. This guide shows a complete analysis. The model reference gives all the settings, and how each setting maps onto mmm-framework.

    From daily spend in local currency to weekly EUR decisions¶

    A typical European media dataset is daily, for each market and channel, in the currency of each market. mmm-framework does the scaling, the carry-over and the seasonality. Thus the pipeline of the platform only does these steps:

    • It changes the currencies.
    • It aggregates the data to weeks.
    • It adds a holiday control.

    The pipeline keeps each learned step. Thus a score does exactly the same work again:

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: eu-geo-mmm, project: growth}
data:
  dataset: dataset://growth/eu_media_daily@latest
  side_tables:
    fx:       {ref: dataset://growth/fx_daily@latest, score_policy: supplied}
    holidays: {ref: dataset://shared/holidays_eu@v3, score_policy: pinned}
  bind:
    dims: {geo: market, channel: channel, time: date}
    vars: {y: revenue_local, spend: spend_local}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: time, last: 8, after_step: weekly}   # weeks exist after `weekly`
pipeline:
  - id: to_eur
    use: core.fx_convert@^1
    with: {vars: [y, spend], table: fx, table_along: date, currency_dim: geo,
           currencies: {UK: GBP}, default_currency: EUR}
  - {id: weekly, use: core.resample@^1, with: {dim: time, freq: W-SUN, how: sum}}
  - {id: hols,   use: core.holidays@^1, with: {table: holidays, table_along: date, along: time}}
model:
  use: mmm.bayesian@^1
  settings:
    hierarchy: {pool_over: [geo]}       # one baseline per market, partially pooled
    new_levels: hyperprior              # a new market is drawn from the fitted spread
    adstock: {kind: geometric, l_max: 6}
    saturation: {kind: logistic}
    seasonality: {yearly_order: 3}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: nutpie, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, seed: 7}
outputs:
  derived: [contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost]
  plots: [core.ppc_fan, core.convergence, mmm.decomposition, mmm.response_curves]
evaluate: {metrics: [elpd_loo, coverage_90, mape]}

    The platform does these things with this spec. You do not write model code:

    • Binding. Your names (market, revenue_local) become the canonical names of the model (geo, y). The platform changes them back on each output.
    • Split at the right stage. The holdout is the last 8 weeks. The platform cuts the holdout after the resample. Thus no partial week goes into the training data.
    • Evaluate, then refit. The platform fits the model on the training weeks. It scores the holdout through the score path, which is the same code that a forecast uses. Then it fits the model again on all the weeks. Thus forecasts start from the latest data.
    • Owned scaling and carry-over. mmm-framework scales the data itself. It carries spend over with its own adstock kernel. The platform puts a score window on top of the stored fit history. Thus carry-over from the TV burst of last month gets to the sales of next month. Validation refuses a scaling or lag step on y or spend (CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED).
    • Weekly EUR. Predictions and contributions come back in the units of the model stage. Here, these are weekly EUR. The outputs tell this in their output_space (model-owned) and units.

    mapi_plugin_mmm.examples.E33_SPEC_YAML is a smaller version of this spec that you can run. It comes with a simulator (e33_dataset) with known true contributions.

    Settings at a glance¶

    Setting Level Default Meaning
    hierarchy.pool_over basic [] Panel dims of the data (geo, product). Each level gets its own baseline, partially pooled
    adstock.kind / l_max basic geometric, 8 Carry-over shape (geometric, delayed, weibull, none) and length
    saturation.kind basic logistic Diminishing returns (logistic, hill, michaelis_menten, tanh, root, none)
    trend.kind basic linear none, linear, piecewise, spline or gp
    seasonality.yearly_order basic 2 Yearly Fourier pairs
    channels.<c> advanced - Adstock/saturation overrides for each channel
    hierarchy.media_by_geo advanced false Channel effects for each geo, partially pooled
    media_prior advanced roi Channel effects given as returns on spend
    priors advanced - roi.<c>: {mu, sigma}, roi_default, intercept
    likelihood advanced normal student_t for heavy-tailed outliers; poisson, negative_binomial for counts; beta for shares
    media_effect_sigma advanced 0.5 The prior scale of the channel effects of a count or share likelihood
    time_varying advanced none channels, innovation_sigma (0.15) and forecast (random_walk) of time-varying channel effects
    new_levels advanced pooled How the model scores unseen geos/products
    controls advanced auto Control variables (default: all other numeric variables over time)
    grid advanced 0-2.5x in 26 steps The spend-multiplier grid of the response curves

    Guided mode shows only the basic settings. Each field has x-ui-level in the JSON Schema of the manifest. The models catalog lists all the settings with their defaults.

    Scoring¶

    Score requests for MMMs work as for all other models. MMM users need these requests most.

    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://growth/eu-geo-mmm-prod
mode: forecast
horizon: {dim: time, periods: 8}
side_tables: {fx: dataset://growth/fx_daily@latest}    # fx is `supplied` at score time
output: {kind: expected, summary: [mean, hdi_90], draws: 200}

    Send daily spend in local currency for the next 8 weeks. You get weekly EUR revenue.

    Add a market that the model did not see (FR). With new_levels: hyperprior, the model draws the baseline of the market from the fitted spread for each posterior draw. Thus its forecast is honest about what the model does not know. The forecast is wider than the forecast of a fitted market. The response has an UNSEEN_LEVELS notice.

    mode: forecast
horizon: {dim: time, periods: 8}
inputs_stage: weekly          # enter after fx conversion and resampling

    Planners send weekly EUR budgets. They do not need to invent daily spend in local currency. You do not need an FX table, because the platform skips the steps that use it. The forecast is the same as the forecast from the raw data for the same spend.

    mode: counterfactual
interventions: [{var: spend_local, sel: {channel: tv}, multiply: 1.2}]
output: {kind: predictive, draws: 200}

    The scenario and the baseline share their noise (common random numbers). Thus the delta group shows the effect of the change, not sampling noise. Only the contribution of the changed channel moves.

    Each score gives y_hat and contributions(component, geo, time). There is one component for each channel, baseline, trend, seasonality, and one component for each control. The components add up to y_hat.

    What a Guided report reads¶

    The fit calculates contributions, response_curves and marginal_cost (in outputs.derived) one time. It uses the first outputs.ui_draws posterior draws. These items read the values:

    • mmm.decomposition, mmm.response_curves and mmm.marginal_cost plot them.
    • The what-if sliders of Guided mode interpolate the response curves for each draw. The answer is instant, with joint uncertainty bands.
    • The plausibility checks of the scorecard read them: negative or absurd ROI, the share of a channel, and the baseline share.

    Business priors (ROI or share ranges) and lift tests calibrate the model through the calibration block of the spec. See Guided modelling and the model reference.

    Calibrate with a geo-lift test v0.13¶

    A geo-lift test changes the spend of one channel in some markets for some weeks. The result of the test is a good lift test for the model, because the test measures the effect directly.

    1. Fit causal.synthetic_control on the KPI of all markets around the test. The geo_lift Guided template does this for you.
    2. Get the lift test from the fit: the lift-test card of the Guided report, or the workflow node model.lift_test.
    3. Add the lift test to calibration.lift_tests of the MMM spec.
    4. Fit the MMM again.
    5. Look at the mmm.lift_tests chart. The estimate of the model must be near the test.
    calibration:
  lift_tests:
    - id: tv-geo-lift-2024-03
      channel: tv
      geo: geo_03            # one test market; null for two or more markets
      start: 2024-03-04
      end: 2024-04-14
      spend_delta: 50000
      kpi_delta: 4100        # the mean of the total effect
      se: 1100               # the sd of the total effect

    The workflow template geo_lift_mmm does all the steps in one run. See Measure the effect of a change.

    Effects that change over time v0.12¶

    Use this when the effect of a channel changes during the data, for example when an advertisement gets old.

    1. Put the channel in time_varying.channels. Remove its ROI prior, if it has one.
    2. Select the forecast policy after the fit window.
    3. Add effectiveness to outputs.derived and mmm.effectiveness to outputs.plots.
    model:
  use: mmm.bayesian@^1
  settings:
    adstock: {kind: geometric, l_max: 8}
    saturation: {kind: hill}
    time_varying: {channels: [tv], innovation_sigma: 0.15, forecast: hold_last}
outputs:
  derived: [contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost, adstock, effectiveness]
  plots: [core.ppc_fan, mmm.decomposition, mmm.effectiveness, mmm.roi]

    The chart shows the effect of one unit of spend in each period, with a range. A forecast with random_walk has a range that becomes wider with the horizon. Use hold_last if you think that the last effect continues. Response curves and budget plans use the average effect over the fit window. Refer to Effects that change over time.

    Counts and shares v0.12¶

    Use a count likelihood when the KPI is a number of items, such as orders, sign-ups or visits. Use beta when the KPI is a share between 0 and 1.

    1. Set likelihood to poisson, negative_binomial or beta.
    2. Remove priors.roi and priors.roi_default. These likelihoods use coefficient priors.
    3. Remove the calibration block. Business priors and lift tests need a normal or Student-t likelihood.
    model:
  use: mmm.bayesian@^1
  settings:
    likelihood: negative_binomial   # use poisson only when the variance is near the mean
    media_effect_sigma: 0.5
    adstock: {kind: geometric, l_max: 8}
    saturation: {kind: hill}

    The contributions still add up to y_hat, in KPI units. The baseline then includes the trend, the seasonality and the controls. A predictive score gives whole numbers for a count and values between 0 and 1 for a share. Refer to Counts and shares.

    Effects through an intermediate measure v0.12¶

    Use mmm.nested when you measure a step between the spend and the KPI, for example site visits or brand awareness from a monthly survey. The Guided template Marketing mix with an intermediate measure (mediated_mmm) makes this spec from your columns.

    1. Put the measures in one variable along a mediator dim. Leave the periods without a value empty.
    2. Bind the dims channel, mediator and time, and bind the measures to mediators.
    3. Optionally, give each mediator its channels and signs in mediators.
    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: online-mmm, project: growth}
data:
  dataset: dataset://growth/online_weekly@latest
  bind:
    dims: {channel: channel, mediator: mediator, time: week}
    vars: {y: online_sales, spend: spend, mediators: measures}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: time, last: 8}
model:
  use: mmm.nested@^1
  settings:
    trend: {kind: linear}
    seasonality: {yearly_order: 2}
    mediators:
      - {name: awareness, channels: [tv, social], observation_sigma: 0.2}
      - {name: visits, outcome_sign: positive}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: nutpie, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, seed: 7}
outputs:
  derived: [contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost, adstock, mediation]
  plots: [core.ppc_fan, core.convergence, mmm.decomposition, mmm.mediation, mmm.roi]

    mmm.mediation shows the direct and the indirect effect of each channel. Lift tests calibrate the total effect of a channel, as for mmm.bayesian.

    Note

    The nested model uses national data only and takes no control variables. If price or promotions change your sales much, mmm.bayesian with controls is a better start.

    Several results v0.12¶

    Use mmm.multivariate when one budget acts on two or more results, for example the sales of a premium range and a basic range. The Guided template Marketing mix for related products (multi_kpi_mmm) makes a start of this spec from your columns, without weights and without cross-effects. Add them in the spec editor.

    1. Put the results in y along an outcome dim.
    2. Give each outcome a weight: the value of one unit of it in a budget plan.
    3. Add the cross-effects that you expect, with their kind.
    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: range-mmm, project: growth}
data:
  dataset: dataset://growth/ranges_weekly@latest
  bind:
    dims: {channel: channel, outcome: outcome, time: week}
    vars: {y: sales, spend: spend}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: time, last: 8}
model:
  use: mmm.multivariate@^1
  settings:
    trend: {kind: linear}
    seasonality: {yearly_order: 2}
    outcomes: [{name: premium, weight: 3.0}, {name: basic, weight: 1.0}]
    cross_effects: [{source: premium, target: basic, kind: halo}]
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: nutpie, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, seed: 7}
outputs:
  derived: [contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost, adstock, cross_effects]
  plots: [core.ppc_fan, core.convergence, mmm.decomposition, mmm.cross_effects, mmm.roi]

    Response curves, the ROI and budget plans use the weighted total of the outcomes. The decomposition and the waterfall show the primary outcome. This is also true for a score that gives the dataset's names, where the outcome dim has the name that you bound (for example, product). Set outcome on mmm.decomposition or mmm.waterfall to show a different one.

    Read cross-effects as associations

    Do not use a cross-effect as proof that one product takes sales from a different product. The model cannot separate it from the correlation of the noise. Use a test to measure a cause.

    Charts for a marketing team v0.12¶

    Add these recipes to outputs.plots. Each chart has a caption in plain words.

    Question Recipe Needs in outputs.derived
    Which channel returns the most for each unit of spend? mmm.roi response_curves (and marginal_cost for the marginal return)
    How long does the effect of spend continue? mmm.adstock adstock
    What adds up to the total result? mmm.waterfall contributions
    Does each channel get spend in proportion to its effect? mmm.spend_effect response_curves
    Do the lift tests agree with the model? mmm.lift_tests none (the fit keeps the lift_tests group)
    Does the effect of a channel change over time? mmm.effectiveness effectiveness
    Did the sampler explore the posterior well? core.energy none
    How much did the data change each prior? core.prior_posterior none

    Optimising budgets¶

    The platform optimises budgets with the planning module of mmm-framework. You do not need an external optimiser:

    import httpx

with httpx.Client(base_url=API, headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {token}",
                                         "X-Mapi-Project": "growth"}) as http:
    r = http.post(
        f"/v1/models/{model_id}/versions/3:optimize",
        json={"budget_change_pct": 10, "objective": "mean", "frontier": {"n_points": 8}},
        headers={"Prefer": "wait=60"},
    )
    plan = r.json()["result"]          # None while the run is still going (202)
for a in plan["allocation"]:
    print(a["channel"], round(a["current_spend"]), "->", round(a["optimal_spend"]))
print("uplift", plan["uplift"]["mean"], "chance it helps", plan["prob_positive_uplift"])

    See Optimise a budget for a walk-through. See the model reference for all the options. In Python, you can also use the decision objective of a fitted bundle directly. MMMBayesian().objective(bundle.model, budget, ScoreContext()) gives the value of a budget for each period, spend(channel) or spend(geo, channel). It gives this value at steady state, for each posterior draw.

    Live progress and cancel v0.11¶

    mmm.bayesian reports its progress while it samples. Before v0.11, the progress went from 10% to 90% in one step.

    • The progress bar moves with the draws of the slowest chain.
    • The run page shows one lane for each chain, with its draws, divergences and step size.
    • The fit logs the step metrics step_size, num_steps and divergences for each chain (label chain). With nuts_sampler: pymc, it also logs tree_depth. The Metrics tab shows them. See Live chains and step metrics.
    • If you cancel the run while it samples, the sampler stops. The run becomes cancelled and makes no model version.

    This applies to nuts_sampler: nutpie and nuts_sampler: pymc. The approximate methods (ADVI, Pathfinder) of mmm-framework do not send progress while they fit. From v0.12, mmm.nested and mmm.multivariate send the same progress and step metrics. Each MMM fit also logs summary metrics, such as the ROI of each channel (see What an MMM fit logs).

    Compare MMM fits v0.14¶

    Each MMM fit logs the return on spend of each channel and other summaries (see What an MMM fit logs). Use these values to compare fits:

    1. Put the fits of a study in an experiment, for example the fits with adstock lengths 4, 6 and 8: mapi fit spec.yaml --remote @ --experiment "adstock study".
    2. In the runs table, add the columns metric.roi{channel=tv} and metric.baseline_share.
    3. Select 2 to 10 runs and click Compare. The compare page shows the metrics, the params that are different and the plots of the runs side by side.

    See Compare runs and use experiments.

    The fast frequentist model¶

    mmm.frequentist takes the same data and settings, and answers in seconds:

    • It searches the transforms out of sample.
    • It solves the model with ridge regression, with non-negative channel effects.
    • The ranges are bootstrap confidence intervals.

    Use it for a first look. Then fit mmm.bayesian before you make decisions.

    Trust, but verify¶

    The four plugins pass the SDK contract kit (mapi_sdk.testing.check_model). The kit tests these items:

    • Bundle round trips.
    • Shuffled and subset markets.
    • New levels.
    • Typed errors.
    • The holdout scored through the same path as a forecast.
    • Additive contributions.

    The conformance corpus runs the plugins through the full spec -> fit -> score path (e33, guided_mmm, mmm_frequentist). The tests of the plugin check the forward pass against the forecaster and the graph of mmm-framework.
