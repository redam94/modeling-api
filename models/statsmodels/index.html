    statsmodels time-series models¶

    The mapi-plugin-statsmodels package brings twelve classical time-series models from statsmodels (BSD-3-Clause) into the platform. They cover the everyday forecasting workhorses (ARIMA, exponential smoothing, theta), models with drivers (ARIMAX, ARDL, error correction), systems of series that drive each other (VAR, VECM, VARMAX, dynamic factors) and regime switching. All of them fit in seconds, run in the time-series runner image (mapi/base-forecast) and pass the same contract kit as every other plugin.

    Which model should I use?¶

    Every model has an About card in the catalog: the web app's Help me choose, mapi choose and api.catalog.recommend() rank them for your goal and data (see Choosing a model). In short:

    Plugin In plain words Choose it when
    ts.sarimax Forecast with past values and drivers You forecast one series a few periods ahead and know future prices, promotions or holidays
    ts.arima Forecast from past values You have only the series itself
    ts.ets Exponential smoothing forecast You need a fast, robust baseline with trend and season
    ts.theta Simple strong baseline You want something simple that is hard to beat, to compare other models with
    ts.unobserved_components Trend, season and noise You want to see the underlying trend without the noise
    ts.ardl Effects that build up over time A driver's effect is spread over several periods
    ts.ecm Long-run and short-run effects The result and its drivers trend together; you want the immediate and the lasting effect
    ts.var Series that drive each other Several series affect each other; you want shocks and Granger tests
    ts.vecm Series tied together for good Trending series keep a stable long-run relationship
    ts.varmax Series that drive each other, with drivers Like VAR, with outside drivers known ahead
    ts.dynamic_factor Common movements across many series Many series follow a few hidden common forces
    ts.markov_switching Switching between regimes Behaviour jumps between distinct modes (normal / promotion-driven)

    Not sure the series is ready? The time-series readiness workflow template runs the unit-root tests, chooses the differencing and the AR/MA orders, backtests ts.sarimax against a naive forecast and only keeps it if it wins; see Workflows.

    Your data, in the model's names¶

    The models use canonical names; bind your dataset's names onto them in data.bind:

    Canonical name What it is
    t The time index: dates (default) or period numbers (settings.time_kind: integer). Policy extend: forecasts continue it.
    y The target. Univariate models: y(t), or y(<series...>, t) for panels. Multivariate models: y(variable, t).
    variable Multivariate models only: the series modelled jointly (realgdp, realcons, ...).
    <series...> Panel dims you name in settings.series_dims (geo, product). One model is fitted per series.
    <exog...> Drivers you name in settings.exog; (<series...>, t); their future values are required for forecasts.

    A table with one column per series (realgdp, realcons, realinv) becomes y(variable, t) with the ingest mapping's wide variables; see From a spreadsheet to a Guided draft.

    Panels are a per-series loop

    With series_dims: [geo] every geo gets its own model (its own orders, parameters and residual checks). Geos are a fixed dim: a forecast for a geo the model never saw is refused with UNSEEN_LEVELS. Multivariate models loop the same way: one joint VAR per geo, for example.

    An ARIMAX per geo¶

    from mapi_plugin_statsmodels.datasets import sales_dataset, sales_schema
from mapi_sdk.harness import fit_spec, score_bundle
from mapi_spec import load_score_request

SPEC = """
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: weekly-sales-arimax, project: retail}
data:
  dataset: dataset://retail/weekly_sales@latest
  bind:
    dims: {t: date}            # the model's time dim is called t
    vars: {y: sales}           # ... and its target y
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: t, last: 8}
model:
  use: ts.sarimax@^1
  settings:
    order: [1, 0, 0]           # one past value (AR 1), no differencing, no MA term
    exog: [price, promo]       # drivers you know ahead
    series_dims: [geo]         # one model per geo
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, seed: 1}
evaluate: {metrics: [rmse, mape, coverage_90]}
"""

history = sales_dataset(periods=104, geos=["north", "south"], seed=0)
bundle = fit_spec(SPEC, history, raw_schema=sales_schema(geo=True))
print(bundle.metrics.values)  # holdout metrics of the evaluation fit
params = bundle.model.groups["params"]
print(params["estimate"].sel(param=["price", "promo"]).round(2).values)
# [[-7.96 11.62] [-7.32 12.18]] - per geo, close to the true -8 and +12

    The bundle's params group is the parameter table: estimate, standard error, z/t statistic, p-value and 95% interval for every parameter of every series.

    Forecasts and the drivers' future¶

    plan = sales_dataset(periods=112, geos=["north", "south"], seed=0).isel(date=slice(104, None))
request = load_score_request("""
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://retail/weekly-sales-arimax
mode: forecast
horizon: {dim: t, periods: 8}
output: {summary: [median, q5, q95]}
""")
forecast = score_bundle(bundle, request, plan.drop_vars("sales"))
print(forecast["summary"].to_dataset()["y_hat"].sel(geo="north").round(1).values)
# rows median, q5, q95 - the 90% prediction interval around the median

    What a score returns, for every model:

    • inside the fitted range: the model's one-step-ahead predictions from the fitted history (the payload's targets and drivers are not used there);
    • after it: a multi-step forecast from the end of the fit, using the drivers in the payload. A missing driver - or a missing value of one - fails with MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE (422). With future_covariates: carry_last, seasonal_naive or zero the platform extends drivers you leave out (see Forecasting).

    A what-if on the future is just another payload: change the planned drivers (a promotion every week, a 5% price rise) and forecast again.

    Outputs are quantiles (y_hat(quantile, ..., t); the representation quantiles): by default 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, 95% and 97.5%, so you get the median and the 50/80/90/95% bands (settings.quantiles changes them; 5%, 50% and 95% are always included). They are prediction intervals - they include the noise of the next value - for both output kinds, because statsmodels has no separate interval for the expected value. Quantiles describe one cell each, so totals across geos or series are refused (CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED); sum the series in the pipeline and model the total instead.

    counterfactual, simulate and filter score modes are not offered (MODE_NOT_SUPPORTED).

    The models¶

    Each section starts with the model's About card (what the web app, mapi about and api.catalog.about() show; a docs test keeps the quotes identical to the catalog), then a minimal spec and, for models with drivers, a forecast from planned drivers. The specs all use this small wrapper - the data binding, a holdout of the last 8 periods scored before the model is refitted on everything - and synthetic or public data from mapi_plugin_statsmodels.datasets; the documentation tests run every one of them (docs/snippets/statsmodels_catalog.py):

    from mapi_plugin_statsmodels.datasets import (
    ECM_SCHEMA,
    GROWTH_SCHEMA,
    MACRO_SCHEMA,
    REGIME_SCHEMA,
    ecm_dataset,
    macro_dataset,
    macro_growth,
    regime_dataset,
    sales_dataset,
    sales_schema,
)
from mapi_sdk.harness import fit_spec, score_bundle
from mapi_spec import load_score_request


def spec(model: str, bind: str = "{dims: {t: date}, vars: {y: sales}}") -> str:
    """A complete ModelSpec around a ``model:`` block: the data binding and a holdout of the
    last 8 periods, scored before the model is refitted on everything."""
    return f"""
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {{name: example, project: growth}}
data:
  dataset: dataset://growth/example@latest
  bind: {bind}
  split: {{kind: time_holdout, dim: t, last: 8}}
model:
{model}
  fit: {{mode: evaluate_then_refit, seed: 1}}
evaluate: {{metrics: [rmse, mape, coverage_90]}}
"""


def forecast(bundle, future, periods: int = 8):  # type: ignore[no-untyped-def]
    """Forecast ``periods`` ahead from planned drivers (the future rows of ``future``)."""
    request = load_score_request(f"""
apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ScoreRequest
model: endpoint://growth/example
mode: forecast
horizon: {{dim: t, periods: {periods}}}
output: {{summary: [median, q5, q95]}}
""")
    return score_bundle(bundle, request, future)["summary"].to_dataset()


weekly = sales_dataset(periods=112, seed=0)  # sales(date) with price and promo
history, plan = weekly.isel(date=slice(0, 104)), weekly.isel(date=slice(104, None))
panel = sales_dataset(periods=104, geos=["north", "south"], seed=1)  # sales(geo, date)

    ts.sarimax - forecast with past values and drivers¶

    Forecasts a series from its own recent past, its seasonal pattern and outside drivers you know ahead, such as planned prices or holidays.

    Use it when you forecast one regular series a few periods ahead and known future drivers (prices, promotions, holidays) matter. Pick something else when you have less than about 50 periods, or several series drive each other (use ts.var).

    SARIMAX = """
  use: ts.sarimax@^1
  settings:
    order: [1, 0, 0]          # AR(1) errors, no differencing, no MA term
    exog: [price, promo]      # drivers you know ahead
"""
sarimax = fit_spec(spec(SARIMAX), history, raw_schema=sales_schema())
fan = forecast(sarimax, plan.drop_vars("sales"))  # next 8 weeks from the planned price/promo
print(fan["y_hat"].sel(stat="median").round(1).values)

    Seasonal ARIMA with drivers - a regression with ARIMA errors - estimated by exact maximum likelihood with the Kalman filter. Missing target values are allowed (the filter skips them). The forecast needs the drivers' planned future values (here plan without sales); a missing one fails with MISSING_FUTURE_COVARIATE unless the request says how to extend it.

    Setting Default Meaning
    order [1, 0, 0] (p, d, q): past values, times differenced, past errors
    seasonal_order [0, 0, 0, 0] (P, D, Q, s) for a pattern repeating every s periods
    trend auto n, c, t, ct; auto is a constant unless the series is differenced
    exog [] drivers
    series_dims [] panel dims: one model per series

    Not sure about order? The readiness workflow chooses it from unit-root tests and an information-criterion search, and keeps the model only if it beats a naive forecast.

    ts.arima - forecast from past values¶

    Forecasts a series from its own recent past and trend, without outside drivers.

    Use it when you have only the series itself and want a solid, well-known method. Pick something else when outside drivers explain most of the movement (use ts.sarimax).

    ARIMA = """
  use: ts.arima@^1
  settings: {order: [1, 1, 1], series_dims: [geo]}   # one ARIMA(1,1,1) per geo
"""
arima = fit_spec(spec(ARIMA), panel, raw_schema=sales_schema(geo=True))

    The same estimator as ts.sarimax without drivers; here one ARIMA(1,1,1) per geo of a panel. ts.arima refuses a constant together with differencing (use trend: t for a drift).

    ts.ets - exponential smoothing¶

    Forecasts a series by giving recent values more weight than older ones, with optional trend and seasonality, and is quick and robust.

    Use it when you need a fast, reliable baseline and the level, trend and season shift slowly. Pick something else when outside drivers explain most of the movement.

    ETS = """
  use: ts.ets@^1
  settings: {trend: add, damped_trend: true}
"""
ets = fit_spec(spec(ETS), history, raw_schema=sales_schema())  # drivers are not used

    The error-trend-season model (ETSModel): error, trend (add/mul), damped_trend, seasonal with seasonal_periods. Additive models have closed-form intervals; multiplicative ones are simulated (simulate_repetitions paths, seeded by the request's seed, so the same request gives the same quantiles).

    ts.theta - a simple, strong baseline¶

    Forecasts a series by combining a straight-line trend with smoothed recent values, a simple method that is hard to beat.

    Use it when you want a strong, simple baseline to compare other models against. Pick something else when outside drivers matter a lot or you need detailed explanations.

    THETA = """
  use: ts.theta@^1
  settings: {series_dims: [geo]}
"""
theta = fit_spec(spec(THETA), panel, raw_schema=sales_schema(geo=True))

    The theta method with optional seasonal adjustment (period, deseasonalize, method, theta). It is the benchmark to beat in a forecast bake-off.

    ts.unobserved_components - trend, season and noise¶

    Splits a series into a slowly changing level and trend, a seasonal pattern and noise, and forecasts it from those parts.

    Use it when you want to see the underlying trend without the noise, and the trend and season change slowly. Pick something else when you only need a quick baseline.

    UC = """
  use: ts.unobserved_components@^1
  settings: {level: local linear trend, exog: [price]}
"""
uc = fit_spec(spec(UC), history, raw_schema=sales_schema())
print(sorted(uc.model.groups["components"].data_vars))  # the smoothed level, trend, ...
uc_fan = forecast(uc, plan.drop_vars("sales"))

    A structural model: level (local level, local linear trend, smooth trend, ...), optional stochastic seasonal, cycle, autoregressive noise and drivers. The smoothed level, trend and season are stored in the bundle's components group and shown in the Model Explorer's decomposition; with drivers, forecasts need their planned values.

    ts.ardl - effects that build up over time¶

    Explains a series from its own past and the current and past values of drivers, so you can see how an effect builds up over several periods.

    Use it when a driver's effect is spread over several periods. Pick something else when effects are immediate only (a simple regression is enough).

    ARDL = """
  use: ts.ardl@^1
  settings: {lags: 2, order: 2, exog: [price, promo]}   # or auto: true to choose them
"""
ardl = fit_spec(spec(ARDL), history, raw_schema=sales_schema())
ardl_fan = forecast(ardl, plan.drop_vars("sales"))

    Least squares on lags of the result and order lags of each driver; auto: true chooses them per series by ic up to those maxima. The parameter table shows each lag's effect, so you can read how a price change works through over the following weeks.

    ts.ecm - long-run and short-run effects¶

    Separates the short-term effect of a driver from its long-term effect on one series, and tests whether a long-run relationship exists at all.

    Use it when one result and a few drivers trend together, and you want both the immediate and the lasting effect of a driver. Pick something else when neither the result nor the drivers trend.

    ECM = """
  use: ts.ecm@^1
  settings: {lags: 1, order: 1, exog: [income]}
"""
monthly = ecm_dataset(periods=126, seed=4)  # sales tied to income in the long run
ecm = fit_spec(
    spec(ECM, "{dims: {t: month}, vars: {y: sales}}"),
    monthly.isel(month=slice(0, 120)),
    raw_schema=ECM_SCHEMA,
)
group = ecm.model.groups["ecm"]  # speed of adjustment, half-life, the bounds test
print(round(float(group["half_life"]), 1), float(group["bounds_p_upper"]) < 0.05)
ecm_fan = forecast(ecm, monthly.isel(month=slice(120, None)).drop_vars("sales"), periods=6)

    The same model in error-correction form (statsmodels UECM): the coefficient on y.L1 is the speed of adjustment (its half-life is in the ecm group), the long_run.* rows of the parameter table are the long-run effects with delta-method standard errors, and the Pesaran-Shin-Smith bounds test (the case from the trend: c is case 3) says whether a long-run relationship exists - levels_relationship, none or inconclusive, also raised as a scorecard diagnostic (ECM_BOUNDS_TEST).

    ts.var - series that drive each other¶

    Models several series that influence each other over time, such as prices and sales of two brands, and forecasts them together.

    Use it when several series move together and affect each other, and you want to see how a shock to one spreads to the others. Pick something else when you have one series, or the series trend together for good (use ts.vecm).

    VAR = """
  use: ts.var@^1
  settings: {max_lags: 4, ic: aic}     # the lag order chosen by AIC
"""
MACRO_BIND = "{dims: {t: quarter, variable: series}, vars: {y: value}}"
var = fit_spec(spec(VAR, MACRO_BIND), macro_growth(quarters=120), raw_schema=GROWTH_SCHEMA)
print(sorted(var.model.groups))  # ... fevd, granger, irf, params, residuals ...

    Least squares; leave lags empty to choose the order by ic up to max_lags. The data holds the series along one dimension (y(variable, t)). The bundle carries orthogonalised impulse responses with asymptotic 95% bands (irf), the forecast-error variance decomposition (fevd) and pairwise Granger causality tests (granger).

    ts.vecm - series tied together for good¶

    Models series that wander but stay tied together in the long run, such as prices of close substitutes, and shows how fast they snap back after a gap opens.

    Use it when series trend but keep a stable long-run relationship. Pick something else when the series do not trend, or the long-run tie is not plausible.

    VECM = """
  use: ts.vecm@^1
  settings: {k_ar_diff: 2, deterministic: ci}   # the rank from the Johansen test
"""
vecm = fit_spec(spec(VECM, MACRO_BIND), macro_dataset(quarters=160), raw_schema=MACRO_SCHEMA)
print(int(vecm.model.groups["cointegration"]["rank"]))

    A Johansen VECM; leave coint_rank empty and the Johansen rank_test (trace or max-eigenvalue) at rank_signif chooses it. A rank of 0 (no long-run relationship: model the changes with ts.var) or of the number of series (they are stationary: use ts.var on the levels) is refused with a plain-language SETTINGS_INVALID. The cointegration group holds the Johansen table, the rank, alpha (speed of adjustment) and beta (the long-run relations, normalised on the first series). The VAR/VECM recipe chooses between the two for you.

    ts.varmax - series that drive each other, with drivers¶

    Models several series that influence each other and also respond to outside drivers you know ahead, and forecasts them together.

    Use it when several series affect each other and outside drivers matter too. Pick something else when you have one series, or many series and little history.

    VARMAX = """
  use: ts.varmax@^1
  settings: {order: [1, 0]}            # VARX(1): q = 0
"""
varmax = fit_spec(spec(VARMAX, MACRO_BIND), macro_growth(quarters=90), raw_schema=GROWTH_SCHEMA)

    VARMA(p, q) with drivers in state-space form (VARX when q = 0); exog drivers need their planned values to forecast, as with ts.sarimax.

    ts.dynamic_factor - common movements across many series¶

    Finds a few hidden forces that move many series together, such as the overall economy behind sales in many categories.

    Use it when you have many related series and want their shared drivers. Pick something else when you have one or two series.

    DFM = """
  use: ts.dynamic_factor@^1
  settings: {k_factors: 1}
"""
dfm = fit_spec(spec(DFM, MACRO_BIND), macro_growth(quarters=90), raw_schema=GROWTH_SCHEMA)
print(sorted(dfm.model.groups["factors"].data_vars))

    A few hidden factors following a VAR drive many series (state space). The smoothed factors and loadings are in the factors group. Standardise the series first (core.standardize), as factor models assume comparable scales.

    ts.markov_switching - switching between regimes¶

    Detects whether a series switches between regimes, such as high-growth and low-growth periods, and estimates the behaviour in each.

    Use it when behaviour seems to jump between distinct modes and you want the probability of being in each mode now. Pick something else when changes are gradual or you have little history.

    MARKOV = """
  use: ts.markov_switching@^1
  settings: {k_regimes: 2, time_kind: integer}   # weeks numbered 0, 1, 2, ...
"""
regimes = regime_dataset(periods=150, seed=3).drop_vars("regime")
markov = fit_spec(
    spec(MARKOV, "{dims: {t: week}, vars: {y: demand}}"), regimes, raw_schema=REGIME_SCHEMA
)
print(markov.model.groups["regimes"]["mean_level"].round(1).values)  # about 10 and 16

    Hamilton's regime-switching regression (order: 0) or autoregression (order > 0): the level, driver effects and noise can switch between k_regimes hidden regimes. The regimes group holds the smoothed and filtered probability of each regime per period, the transition matrix, expected durations and each regime's mean level. Forecasts are regime mixtures: analytic for regressions, simulated (simulations seeded paths) for autoregressions. Start values come from a fit on the standardised series, which avoids the failures of statsmodels' default start values on series far from zero.

    Forecasting through an endpoint, the client and the CLI¶

    Once a version is promoted, the same forecast comes from the endpoint, with the drivers' plan sent inline (an xr.Dataset with the future rows):

    from mapi_client import Client

api = Client()
fan = api.endpoint("weekly-sales").score(plan_ds, mode="forecast", horizon={"dim": "t", "periods": 8})
print(fan["y_hat"].sel(stat="median").values)

    mapi endpoints score weekly-sales --data plan.json --periods 8 -o forecast.zarr.zip
mapi explore model model://growth/weekly-sales@v3 -s forecast -s residuals --horizon 8

    What is stored, and why there is no pickle¶

    A statsmodels results object is never stored: pickles are not allowed across the sandbox boundary. A bundle keeps

    • the model-stage training data (observed_data, and the drivers in constant_data);
    • every estimated parameter (params), with the order the model needs in learned;
    • fit statistics (fit_stats: AIC, BIC, HQIC, log-likelihood, observations, noise variance, whether the optimiser converged), residuals and residual tests (Ljung-Box, Jarque-Bera, ARCH-LM);
    • the model-specific groups above.

    Scoring rebuilds the results: state-space models (SARIMAX, ARIMA, UnobservedComponents, VARMAX, DynamicFactor) and the ETS and Markov-switching models apply the stored parameters to the stored data with their own filter (model.filter(params), smooth(params)); least-squares and closed-form models (VAR, VECM, ARDL/ECM, Theta) are re-estimated on the stored data with the stored orders and checked against the stored parameters - a mismatch fails with INTEGRITY_FAILURE.

    The Model Explorer¶

    StatsmodelsModel.explorer_views(fitted) turns a bundle into Model Explorer sections (mapi_spec.views DTOs): params (the table with 95% intervals and p-values), diagnostics (fit statistics, Granger, Johansen and bounds tests), residuals (residuals over time, their autocorrelation, Q-Q points and the residual tests per series), irf, fevd and decomposition. Panels appear as facets. views.forecast_view(predictions, observed=...) turns a forecast score run's quantiles into the forecast section: one fan per series (with the recent history), because quantiles of different series cannot be added into one fan.

    How it is tested¶

    • The contract kit (check_model) passes for every plugin with About cards required.
    • Parameters, in-sample predictions and forecast intervals equal direct statsmodels calls on the same data (SARIMAX, ARIMA, UnobservedComponents, ETS, Theta, ARDL, UECM, VAR, VECM, VARMAX, DynamicFactor, Markov switching); the UECM is also checked on Johansen and Juselius' Danish money-demand data, and the VAR(2) on US macro growth reproduces the coefficient table of the statsmodels documentation.
    • Conformance fixtures sm_arimax (an ARIMAX panel with drivers) and sm_vecm_macro (a VECM on statsmodels' public-domain macrodata) run the whole fit, score and evaluate path; see Conformance corpus.
