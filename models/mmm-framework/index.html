    Marketing-mix models on mmm-framework¶

    From v0.5 every marketing-mix model (MMM) on the platform runs on mmm-framework (PyPI mmm-framework==1.5.0, Apache-2.0), a Bayesian MMM framework built on PyMC. mapi-plugin-mmm wraps it behind the platform contract as two built-in models:

    Model In plain words Engine
    mmm.bayesian Measures how much each marketing channel adds to your sales, in total or per market, and what would happen if you moved budget between channels. mmm-framework BayesianMMM: NUTS (nutpie, PyMC or NumPyro), or fast approximations (MAP, ADVI, Pathfinder)
    mmm.frequentist A first estimate in seconds, with simpler ranges from resampling the data instead of a full Bayesian model's uncertainty. mmm-framework's ridge estimator with bootstrap intervals

    Both carry a plain-language About card (what the model is for, when not to use it, what data it needs, what you get out); the web app's Help me choose recommends them for "explain my marketing" and "plan my budget" goals (mapi choose -a goal=plan_budget, see Choosing a model). mmm-framework is the platform's only marketing-mix engine, and budgets are optimised by the platform itself (below).

    What the data looks like¶

    mmm-framework owns scaling, carry-over and the conversion back to your units (owns: [scaling, carryover, inverse_units]), so the pipeline only converts currencies, aggregates to weeks and adds controls. A scaling or lag step on the KPI or the spend is refused when the spec is validated (CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED), so nothing is scaled twice.

    Canonical names (bind your dataset's names onto them in data.bind):

    Name Dims Role
    time - the period dim (extend: forecasts run past the data)
    channel - one label per media channel (fixed)
    geo, product - the panel dims listed in hierarchy.pool_over (none for one national series)
    y (*panel, time) the KPI
    spend (*panel, channel, time) spend per channel (future_required: a forecast needs it)
    any other numeric variable over time (*panel?, time, ...) a control (or list them in controls); extra dims such as Fourier modes become one feature per label 
    apiVersion: mapi/v1
kind: ModelSpec
metadata: {name: eu-geo-mmm, project: growth}
data:
  dataset: dataset://growth/eu_media_daily@latest
  side_tables:
    fx:       {ref: dataset://growth/fx_daily@latest, score_policy: supplied}
    holidays: {ref: dataset://shared/holidays_eu@v3, score_policy: pinned}
  bind:
    dims: {geo: market, channel: channel, time: date}
    vars: {y: revenue_local, spend: spend_local}
  split: {kind: time_holdout, dim: time, last: 8, after_step: weekly}
pipeline:
  - {id: to_eur, use: core.fx_convert@^1,
     with: {vars: [y, spend], table: fx, table_along: date, currency_dim: geo,
            currencies: {UK: GBP}, default_currency: EUR}}
  - {id: weekly, use: core.resample@^1, with: {dim: time, freq: W-SUN, how: sum, full_periods_only: true}}
  - {id: hols,   use: core.holidays@^1, with: {table: holidays, table_along: date, along: time}}
model:
  use: mmm.bayesian@^1
  settings:
    hierarchy: {pool_over: [geo]}
    new_levels: hyperprior
    adstock: {kind: geometric, l_max: 6}
    saturation: {kind: hill}
    trend: {kind: linear}
    seasonality: {yearly_order: 3}
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, engine: nutpie, tune: 1000, draws: 1000, chains: 4, seed: 7}
outputs:
  derived: [contributions, response_curves, marginal_cost]
  plots: [core.ppc_fan, core.convergence, mmm.decomposition, mmm.response_curves]
evaluate: {metrics: [elpd_loo, coverage_90, mape]}

    A runnable, scaled-down version of this spec ships as mapi_plugin_mmm.examples.E33_SPEC_YAML, with a simulator (e33_dataset) whose true contributions are known; NATIONAL_SPEC_YAML with national_dataset() is the smallest national example.

    MFF data¶

    mmm-framework's native format is the Master Flat File (MFF): one row per Period, Geography, Product, Campaign, Outlet, Creative and VariableName, the value in VariableValue. Upload it with the mff ingest preset (or as a Guided upload): every variable becomes its own data variable, and the key columns follow mmm-framework's MFF semantics. Geography and Product become dims when they have more than one level; a variable given only in total (a blank, National or Total label - national TV next to regional sales) is spread across the levels like MFFLoader disaggregates it (geographies equally, products by the KPI's share; MffOptions.allocation changes it), and a variable may not mix per-level and total rows. Campaign, Outlet and Creative are summed up to the modelled dims unless MffOptions.split keeps them. The Guided geo template stacks the media variables you pick into spend(channel). The plugin hands the panel to mmm-framework in exactly the layout its own MFFLoader builds - observations period-major, then geography, then product - and tests check that an MFF file ingested by the platform and the same file loaded with mmm_framework.load_mff give the same panel, national media and campaign rows included.

    How the spec maps onto mmm-framework¶

    Spec mmm-framework
    y, spend, controls PanelDataset (y, X_media, X_controls) with MFFConfig (KPIConfig, MediaChannelConfig per channel, ControlVariableConfig per control feature)
    hierarchy.pool_over: [geo, product] the KPI's Geography/Product dimensions; HierarchicalConfig.pool_across_geo/product (a baseline per level, partially pooled)
    hierarchy.media_by_geo HierarchicalConfig.vary_media_by_geo (channel effects per geo, partially pooled)
    adstock.kind, l_max, normalize AdstockConfig (geometric, delayed, weibull, none) on every channel
    saturation.kind SaturationConfig (logistic, hill, michaelis_menten, tanh, root, none)
    channels.<c>.adstock/saturation that channel's configs
    trend.kind TrendConfig (none, linear, piecewise, spline, gp)
    seasonality.yearly_order ... SeasonalityConfig.yearly/monthly/weekly (Fourier pairs)
    media_prior: roi ModelConfig.media_prior_mode="roi": channel effects parameterised by their return on spend
    priors.roi.<c>: {mu, sigma} MediaChannelConfig.roi_prior_mu/roi_prior_sigma
    priors.intercept: {mu, sigma} ModelConfig.intercept_prior_mu/sigma
    likelihood: student_t LikelihoodConfig(family=student_t)
    fit.engine nutpie/pymc/numpyro: NUTS with that backend; map, advi, fullrank_advi, pathfinder, laplace, smc: BayesianMMM.fit(method=...)
    fit.tune, draws, chains, target_accept, seed the same fit() arguments

    frequency (W, D or M) is read from the time labels; integer labels need it set. New panel levels at score time follow new_levels: pooled (the overall level and the average channel effects), hyperprior (a level drawn from the fitted spread for every draw, so its range is honestly wider), zero (no channel effects) or error.

    What is stored, and how scoring works¶

    A fit stores, never as a pickle: mmm-framework's parameters (posterior), the sampler statistics, a thinned prior of the free parameters (the scorecard's prior/posterior overlap), the observed KPI and a fit_data group with the model-stage KPI, spend and control features. Scoring rebuilds the BayesianMMM from fit_data - the same data give the same scaling, Fourier features and trend basis - and replays the fitted model per posterior draw in NumPy: intercept, trend, seasonality, panel levels, every channel's adstock and saturation, every control, and the observation noise. The replay is the same forward pass as mmm-framework's own PosteriorForecaster; tests check it against both the forecaster and the graph's deterministics to floating-point precision.

    • Carry-over. The scored periods are written over the fitted history, so last month's TV burst reaches next month's sales and a four-week window scores exactly like the same weeks of the full series. Periods after the fit window must follow it without a gap.
    • Trends past the data. A linear trend is extrapolated; piecewise, spline and GP trends are held at their last fitted level (mmm-framework's forecasting policy), and the forecast says so - their intervals do not widen with the horizon.
    • Outputs. y_hat(*panel, time) and contributions(component, *panel, time): one component per channel, baseline (intercept and panel levels), trend, seasonality and one per control. They add up to y_hat. A predictive request adds noise from a seeded generator, so a counterfactual's scenario and baseline share it.

    Priors in business terms¶

    Channel effects are parameterised by their return on spend: under media_prior: roi (the default) each channel samples roi ~ LogNormal(mu, sigma) - the KPI it returns per unit of spend over the fit window - and mmm-framework derives the coefficient from it. So a prior is stated in the units a business owner uses:

    settings:
  priors:
    roi:
      tv:     {mu: 0.69, sigma: 0.4}    # median ROI e^0.69 = 2.0, 90% within about [1.0, 3.9]
      search: {mu: 1.1, sigma: 0.3}
    roi_default: {mu: 0.0, sigma: 1.0}  # channels not listed: median break-even
    intercept: {mu: 0.2, sigma: 0.3}    # the baseline, in KPI standard deviations

    Business priors (Guided task 3; calibration.business_priors, or a translate_priors run) translate into these in closed form:

    • an ROI range [low, high] held with credibility p is the log-normal whose central p interval it is;
    • a contribution-share range is an ROI range scaled by the KPI total over the channel's spend (share = roi x spend / KPI);
    • a range starting at 0 is read as [high / 20, high] (with an info issue);
    • the baseline share (what happens without media) sets the intercept prior, solved by bisection against mmm-framework's own prior of the trend, seasonality, levels and controls.

    The preview samples the translated model's prior predictive (mmm-framework's graph) and reports ROI, share, baseline share and a KPI fan in business units, plus the prior mass inside every stated range (coverage, close to the stated credibility).

    Lift tests¶

    calibration.lift_tests become mmm-framework experiment likelihoods (BayesianMMM.add_experiment_calibration): a likelihood term on the model-implied estimand that updates the channel's effect, its saturation curve and its carry-over jointly.

    Lift test mmm-framework measurement
    extra spend (spend_delta > 0) MROAS, spend_lift_pct = 100 x spend_delta / window spend, value kpi_delta / spend_delta
    a holdout removing (nearly) all the channel's spend (-spend_delta >= 95% of it) CONTRIBUTION of -kpi_delta
    a partial cut (less than 95% of the spend) skipped, with the reason in learned.calibration.lift_tests.skipped: it measures the slope below today's spend, (f(S) - f((1-c)S)) / cS, while MROAS is the slope above it (spend_lift_pct > 0 only); under diminishing returns they differ (by e^(c lam) for 1 - exp(-lam x)), so the mapping would bias the curve

    geo restricts the measurement to that geo. A test after the data, or cut by the fit window (a holdout fit that ends inside the test), is skipped and listed.

    Derived quantities¶

    Ask for them in outputs.derived; they are computed at fit over the first outputs.ui_draws draws and stored in the bundle:

    • contributions: channel_contribution(sample, channel, *panel, time), baseline, trend, seasonality and one variable per control;
    • response_curves: contribution(sample, channel, multiplier[, geo]), the total contribution over the fit window with the channel's spend scaled by the multiplier (0 to 2.5 x by default), the spend on each grid point, the current spend, the largest single-period spend and the baseline - what Guided-mode what-if sliders interpolate per draw and the scorecard's plausibility checks read;
    • marginal_cost: the extra spend per extra unit of KPI at current spend, and its inverse.

    Budget optimisation¶

    POST /v1/models/{model_id}/versions/{version}:optimize asks how to split a budget across channels (and optionally geos). A sandboxed optimize run builds the fitted model's response curves per posterior draw and hands them to mmm-framework's planning module: optimize_budget (greedy marginal allocation, or a constrained multi-start solver for risk objectives, groups and money bounds) and budget_frontier.

    curl -s -X POST "$API/v1/models/$MODEL/versions/3:optimize" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $MAPI_TOKEN" -H "X-Mapi-Project: growth" \
  -H "Prefer: wait=60" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{
    "total_budget": 1000000,
    "channels": {"tv": {"min_multiplier": 0.5, "max_multiplier": 1.5},
                 "search": {"min_spend": 50000}},
    "groups": [{"name": "digital", "channels": ["search", "social"], "min_share": 0.4}],
    "objective": "p10",
    "frontier": {"n_points": 10}
  }'
    Request mmm-framework
    total_budget / budget_change_pct the budget (default: today's total)
    mode: fixed / free spend the budget / fund each channel to break-even (value_per_kpi converts KPI to money)
    channels.<c>.min_multiplier/max_multiplier bounds (multiples of current spend)
    channels.<c>.min_spend/max_spend abs_bounds (money; they override multiples)
    channels.<c>.locked the multiplier pinned at 1
    groups portfolio constraints (shares and/or money)
    objective mean, p<q> (a cautious percentile) or cvar<a> (mean of the worst draws)
    by_geo allocate per (geo, channel) arm jointly
    horizon the plan's periods (curves rescaled from the fit window at its average spend)
    max_draws, n_steps, seed sampling controls

    The answer (200 when the run finished within Prefer: wait, else 202; poll GET /v1/optimizations/{run_id}) carries the allocation per channel with its expected contribution, marginal return and how stable the recommendation is across draws, the expected KPI of the plan and of today's plan, the uplift and the chance it is positive, the expected regret of committing to one plan, the shadow price of the next unit of money, the efficient frontier, and notes - for example when a recommendation goes beyond the spend the model has seen. The run also renders the mmm.budget_plans plot. Bounds the data cannot meet fail the run with OPTIMIZE_INFEASIBLE.

    End to end with the client¶

    From a fitted version to a plan you can defend, in Python (the CLI equivalent follows):

    1. Check the model first. A plan is only as good as the response curves under it. Run the governance policy for marketing-mix models - null tests against shuffled sales and fake noise channels, effect signs, holdout accuracy - and read why each check passed:

      validation = api.models.validate(model_id, version, note="Q4 refresh", wait=True)
print(validation.status.value)  # validated, awaiting_approval or checks_failed
for r in validation.results:
    print(r.check, r.status.value, r.message_plain, r.suggested_action or "")
if validation.status.value == "checks_failed":
    validation = api.validations.resubmit(validation.id, note="refitted on clean data")

    2. Look at the curves. The Model Explorer's response_curves section shows each channel's contribution as its spend is scaled, per posterior draw, with the current spend marked; decomposition shows what the model attributes to each channel over time:

      explored = api.models.explore(model_id, version, sections=["response_curves", "decomposition"])
curves = api.explorations.view(explored, "response_curves")

    3. Ask for the plan. api.models.optimize sends Prefer: wait and repeats the request with the same Idempotency-Key until the sandboxed run is done, so a slow plan never starts twice:

      plan = api.models.optimize(  # mmm-framework's planner on the posterior; waits for the plan
    model_id,
    version,
    budget_change_pct=0,  # reallocate today's total
    channels={"tv": {"min_multiplier": 0.5, "max_multiplier": 1.5}},
    objective="p10",  # plan for a cautious outcome, not the average one
    frontier={"n_points": 8},
)
assert plan.result is not None
for a in plan.result.allocation:
    print(f"{a.channel:10} {a.current_spend:>12,.0f} -> {a.optimal_spend:>12,.0f}")
uplift = plan.result.uplift
print("uplift", uplift.mean if uplift else None, "P(better)", plan.result.prob_positive_uplift)

    4. Test the biggest move. Score the plan's weekly spend as a forecast, then design a lift test for the channel the plan moves most; its result goes into the model's calibration block (see Lift tests) and the next plan knows it.

    The same from a terminal:

    mapi validate model model://growth/geo-mmm@v7
mapi explore model model://growth/geo-mmm@v7 -s response_curves -s decomposition --out views/
mapi optimize model://growth/geo-mmm@v7 --change-pct 0 \
  --channel 'tv={"min_multiplier": 0.5, "max_multiplier": 1.5}' \
  --objective p10 --frontier 8

    Read it the way the planning module intends. The plan protects the cautious outcome (the 10th percentile), so its expected revenue is a little below today's and it beats today's plan in only 48% of the draws: on this model, moving money from search to TV buys insurance, not growth, and the next euro returns about the same (2.9) in both channels - today's split is already close to efficient.     A workflow can do all of it on a schedule: the mmm_governed gallery template fits mmm.bayesian on a geo panel, runs the policy with submit: true and keeps the evidence pack (mapi workflows init mmm_governed).

    The frequentist model¶

    mmm.frequentist takes the same data and settings (no priors, likelihood or GP trend). mmm-framework searches adstock and saturation by an out-of-sample (rolling-origin) criterion, fixes them, solves the now-linear model by ridge regression with non-negative channel effects and repeats the solve on block-bootstrap resamples. The replicates are the "draws" of every output, so ranges are confidence intervals; there are no convergence diagnostics, business priors or lift tests. It is seed-deterministic and fits in seconds.

    model:
  use: mmm.frequentist@^1
  settings:
    hierarchy: {pool_over: [geo]}
    adstock: {kind: geometric, l_max: 6}
    saturation: {kind: logistic}
    bootstrap: 200
    search_budget: 200
  fit: {mode: evaluate_then_refit, seed: 7}

    estimator: constrained (a convex program) needs mmm-framework's optional cvxpy dependency, which the platform's images do not install; the fit then fails with CAPABILITY_UNSUPPORTED.

    Current limits¶

    • The multiplicative (log-log) specification, time-varying effects, price and promotion levers, events and cross-channel interactions of mmm-framework are not exposed: the platform's forward pass covers the additive model.
    • mmm-framework's mmm_extensions (nested/mediated and multivariate-outcome models) are not shipped yet: mmm.nested and mmm.multivariate have About cards but no plugin.
    • Fits run on CPU (JAX is used by the NumPyro backend only).

    Trust, but verify¶

    Both models pass the SDK contract kit (mapi_sdk.testing.check_model): bundle round trips, shuffled and subset markets, new levels, typed errors for gaps and periods before the data, the holdout scored through the same path as a forecast, additive contributions and the plots. The conformance corpus fits e33 (a geo panel with a GBP market, side tables and a weekly plan), guided_mmm (business priors and a lift test) and mmm_frequentist end to end, and a recovery test fits simulated data with known contributions and checks their split across channels.
